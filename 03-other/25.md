## 25 則陽 Zeyang

則陽游於楚
夷節言之於王
王未之見
夷節歸
When Zeyang was traveling in Chu,
Yi Jie spoke to the king of Chu about him
but gave up and went home
without having persuaded the king to grant Zeyang an interview.

彭陽見王果曰夫子何不譚我於王
Zeyang went to see Wang Guo and said, "Sir, I wonder if you would mention me to the king."[^25-1]

[^25-1]: Zeyang or Peng Yang (the name appears both ways in the passage)
is vaguely identified as a native of Lu. In hopes of official appointment,
he is obviously seeking an introduction to the king of Chu through various courtiers.

***

王果曰我不若公閱休
Wang Guo replied, "I would not be as good at that as Gong Yuexiu."

***

彭陽曰公閱休
Zeyang said, "Gong Yuexiu?

奚為者邪
What does he do?"

***

曰冬則擉鱉於江
"In winter he spears turtles by the river;
夏則休乎山樊
有過而問者曰此予宅也
in summer he loafs around the mountains,
and if anyone comes along and asks him about it, he says, 'This is my house!'

夫夷節已不能
而況我乎
Now since Yi Jie was unable to persuade the king,
what could I do?---
吾又不若夷節
I am not even a match for Yi Jie.

夫夷節之為人也
無德而有知
Yi Jie is the kind of man who has understanding,
though he lacks real virtue.

不自許
以之神其交
He is not permissive with himself
but puts his whole spirit into pleasing his friends.

固顛冥乎富貴之地
He has always been dazzled and misled by wealth and eminence---
非相助以德
相助消也
so he is not the kind to help others out with virtue
but instead will help them out with harm.

夫凍者假衣於春
A man who is chilled will think spring has come if he piles on enough clothes;
暍者反冬乎冷風
a man suffering from the heat will think winter has returned if he finds a cool breeze.[^25-2]

[^25-2]: I fail to see how this saying, if I understand it correctly, is meant to apply to the context.

夫楚王之為人也
形尊而嚴
其於罪也
無赦如虎
Now the king of Chu is the kind of man
who is majestic and stern in bearing,
and if offended,
he is as unforgiving as a tiger.

非夫佞人
正德
其孰能橈焉
No one but a gross flatterer
or a man of the most perfect virtue
can hope to talk him into anything.

***

故聖人
"The true sage, now---
其窮也使家人忘其貧
living in hardship, he can make his family forget their poverty;
其達也使王公忘其爵祿而化卑
living in affluence, he can make kings and dukes forget their titles and stipends and humble themselves before him.

其於物也與之為娛矣
His approach to things is to go along with them and be merry;
其於人也樂物之通而保己焉
his approach to men is to take pleasure in the progress of others and to hold on to what is his own.

故或不言而飲人以和
So there may be times when, without saying a word, he induces harmony in others;
與人並立而使人化
父子之宜
彼其乎歸居
just standing alongside others, he can cause them to change
until the proper relationship between father and son
has found its way into every home.[^25-3]

[^25-3]: The latter part of the sentence is unintelligible in the original,
and the translation is no more than a guess.

而一閒其所施
He does it all in a spirit of unity and effortlessness---
其於人心者若是其遠也
so far is he removed from the hearts of men.

故曰待公閱休
This is why I say you should wait for Gong Yuexiu."

---

聖人達綢繆
周盡一體矣
而不知其然
The sage penetrates bafflement and complication,
rounding all into a single body,
yet he does not know why---
性也
it is his inborn nature.

復命搖作而以天為師
人則從而命之也
He returns to fate and acts accordingly, using Heaven as his teacher,
and men follow after, pinning labels on him.

憂乎知而所行恆無幾時
其有止也若之何
But if he worried about how much he knew and his actions were never constant for so much as a year or a season,[^25-4]
then how could he ever find a stopping place?

[^25-4]: I follow Ma Xulun in the interpretation of *qi*;
the sentence is vague, and there are many other interpretations.

***

生而美者
人與之鑑
不告則不知其美於人也
When people are born with good looks,
you may hand them a mirror,
but if you don't tell them, they will never know that they are better looking than others.

若知之若不知之
若聞之若不聞之
其可喜也終無已
人之好之亦無已
Whether they know it or don't know it,
whether they are told of it or are not told of it,
however, their delightful good looks remain unchanged to the end,
and others can go on endlessly admiring them---
性也
it is a matter of inborn nature.

聖人之愛人也
人與之名
不告則不知其愛人也
The sage loves other men,
and men accordingly pin labels on him,
but if they do not tell him, then he will never know that he loves other men.

若知之若不知之
若聞之若不聞之
其愛人也終無已
人之安之亦無已
Whether he knows it or doesn't know it,
whether he is told of it or is not told of it,
however, his love for men remains unchanged to the end,
and others can find endless security in it---
性也
it is a matter of in-born nature.

---

舊國舊都
The old homeland, the old city---
望之暢然
just to gaze at it from afar is to feel a flush of joy.

雖使丘陵草木之緡
入之者十九
猶之暢然
Even when its hills and mounds are a tangle of weeds and brush,
and nine out of ten of the ones you knew have gone to lie under them,
still you feel joyful.

況見見聞聞者也
How much more so, then, when you see those you used to see, when you hear the voices you used to hear---
以十仞之臺縣眾閒者也
they stand out like eighty-foot towers among the crowd.[^25-5]

[^25-5]: Any number of different translations could be made of this haunting and troublesome paragraph,
all as tentative as the one I offer here. It has traditionally been interpreted to express
the joy a person experiences when he returns to his inborn nature.

---

冉相氏得其環中以隨成
Mr. Renxiang held on to the empty socket and followed along to completion.[^25-6]

[^25-6]: Compare sec. 2, p. 10: "When the hinge is fitted into the socket,
it can respond endlessly." Mr. Renxiang is vaguely identified as an ancient sage ruler.

與物無終無始
無幾無時日
Joining with things, he knew no end, no beginning,
no year, no season.[^25-7]

[^25-7]: I take *qi* as in the earlier passage; see n. 4.

與物化者
一不化者也
And because he changed day by day with things,
he was one with the man who never changes---
闔嘗舍之
so why should he ever try to stop doing this?

夫師天而不得師天
He who tries to make Heaven his teacher will never get Heaven to teach him---
與物皆殉
其以為事也若之何
he will end up following blindly along with all other things,
and then no matter how he goes about it, what can he do?

夫聖人未始有天
未始有人
未始有始
未始有物
The sage has never begun to think of Heaven,
has never begun to think of man,
has never begun to think of a beginning,
has never begun to think of things.

與世偕行而不替
He moves in company with the age, never halting;
所行之備而不洫
wherever he moves, he finds completion and no impediment.

其合之也若之何
Others try to keep up with him, but what can they do?

***

湯得其司御門尹登恆為之傅之
Tang got hold of the groom and guardsman Deng Heng and had him be his tutor.

從師而不囿
He followed him and treated him as a teacher but was not confined by him---
得其隨成
為之司其名之名
so he could follow along to completion,
becoming, as a result, a mere holder of titles.

嬴法得其兩見
This is called making yourself superfluous, a method by which two manifestations can be attained.[^25-8]

[^25-8]: Compare sec. 2, p. 11: "This is called walking two roads."
It would seem that Tang turned over the actual affairs of government to Deng Heng
and retained only the title of ruler for himself.
But this whole passage is barely intelligible, and there are many interpretations.

仲尼之盡慮
Confucius's injunction "Be done with schemes!"---
為之傅之
you could let that be your tutor as well.

容成氏曰除日無歲
Or Mr. Yongcheng's saying, "Be done with days and there will be no more years!

無內無外
No inside, no outside."

---

魏瑩與田侯牟約
田侯牟背之
King Ying of Wei made a treaty with Marquis Tian Mou of Qi,
but Marquis Tian Mou violated it.[^25-9]

[^25-9]: There is some doubt about the names and identity of these noblemen.

魏瑩怒
將使人刺之
King Ying, enraged,
was about to send a man to assassinate him.

犀首聞而恥之
Gong-sun Yan, the minister of war, heard of this and was filled with shame.

曰君為萬乘之君也
而以匹夫從讎
"You are the ruler of a state of ten thousand chariots," he said to the king,
"and yet you would send a commoner to carry out your revenge!

衍請受甲二十萬
為君攻之
虜其人民
係其牛馬
I beg to be given command of two hundred thousand armored troops
so that I may attack him for you,
make prisoners of his people,
and lead away his horses and cattle.

使其君內熱發於背
I will make him burn with anger so fierce that it will break out on his back.[^25-10]

[^25-10]: Men who develop ulcers on their back as a result of intense anger and frustration
are mentioned in other early Chinese texts.

然後拔其國
忌也出走
然後抶其背
折其脊
Then I will storm his capital,
and when Tian Ji[^25-11] tries to run away,
I will strike him in the back
and break his spine!"

[^25-11]: The commander of the Qi army.

***

季子聞而恥之曰築十仞之城
城者既十仞矣
則又壞之
此胥靡之所苦也
Jizi, hearing this, was filled with shame and said, "If one sets out to build an eighty-foot wall,
and then, when it is already seven-tenths finished,[^25-12]
deliberately pulls it down,
the convict laborers who built it will look upon it as a bitter waste.

[^25-12]: Following Yu Yue, I read *qi* in place of *shi*.

今兵不起七年矣
此王之基也
Now for seven years we have not had to call out the troops,
and this peace has been the foundation of your sovereignty.

衍亂人
Gongsun Yan is a troublemaker---
不可聽也
his advice must not be heeded!"

***

華子聞而醜之曰善言伐齊者亂人也
善言勿伐者亦亂人也
Huazi, hearing this, was filled with disgust and said, "He who is so quick to say 'Attack Qi!' is a troublemaker,
and he who is so quick to say 'Don't attack Qi!' is a troublemaker!

謂伐之與不伐亂人也者又亂人也
And he who says that both those who are for and against the attack are troublemakers is a troublemaker, too!"

王曰然則若何
"Then what should I do?" said the ruler.

***

曰君求其道而已矣
"Just try to find the Way, that's all."

***

惠子聞之而見戴晉人
Huizi, hearing this, introduced Dai Jinren to the ruler.

戴晉人曰有所謂蝸者
Dai Jinren said, "There is a creature called the snail---
君知之乎
does Your Majesty know it?"

曰然
"Yes."

***

有國於蝸之左角者曰觸氏
有國於蝸之右角者曰蠻氏
"On top of its left horn is a kingdom called Buffet,
and on top of its right horn is a kingdom called Maul.[^25-13]

[^25-13]: I borrow these translations of the names with gratitude from Waley (_Three Ways of Thought_, p. 64).

時相與爭地而戰
伏尸數萬
逐北旬有五日而後反
At times they quarrel over territory and go to war,
strewing the field with corpses by the ten thousands,
the victor pursuing the vanquished for half a month before returning home."

***

君曰噫
"Pooh!" said the ruler.

其虛言與
"What kind of empty talk is this?"

***

曰臣請為君實之
"But Your Majesty will perhaps allow me to show you the truth in it.

君以意在四方上下有窮乎
Do you believe that there is a limit to the four directions, to up and down?"

君曰無窮
"They have no limits," said the ruler.

***

曰知遊心於無窮
而反在通達之國
若存若亡乎
"And do you know that when the mind has wandered in these limitless reaches
and returns to the lands we know and travel,
they seem so small that it is not certain whether or not they even exist?"

君曰然
"Yes," said the ruler.

***

曰通達之中有魏
於魏中有梁
於梁中有王
"And among these lands we know and travel is the state of Wei,
and within the state of Wei is the city of Liang,
and within the city of Liang is Your Majesty.

王與蠻氏有辯乎
Is there any difference between you and the ruler of Maul?"

君曰無辯
"No difference," said the king.

***

客出而君惝然若有亡也
After the visitor left, the king sat stupefied, as though lost to the world.

客出
惠子見
The interview over,
Huizi appeared before him.

君曰客大人也
"That visitor of ours is a Great Man," said the king.

***

聖人不足以當之
"The sages themselves are unworthy of comparison with him!"

惠子曰夫吹筦也
猶有嗃也
Huizi said, "Blow on a flute,
and you get a nice shrill note;
吹劍首者
吷而已矣
but blow on the ring of your sword hilt,
and all you get is a feeble wheeze.

堯舜人之所譽也
道堯舜於戴晉人之前
譬猶一吷也
People are inclined to praise the sages Yao and Shun,
but if you started expounding on Yao and Shun in the presence of Dai Jinren,
it would sound like one little wheeze!"

---

孔子之楚
舍於蟻丘之漿
When Confucius was traveling to the capital of Chu,
he stopped for the night at a tavern at Ant Knoll.

其鄰有夫妻臣妾登極者
Next door a crowd of husbands and wives, menservants and maid-servants, had climbed up to the rooftop [to watch].[^25-14]

[^25-14]: The text says only that they had climbed to the roof (if that is, in fact,  the meaning of *dengji*).
Commentators disagree as to why they were there,
but it seems most natural to suppose that they had gathered to gawk at Confucius,
the pseudo sage, unaware that they were actually in the employ of a real sage.

子路曰是稯稯何為者邪
Zilu said, "Who are all those people milling around?"

***

仲尼曰是聖人僕也
"They are the servants of a sage," said Confucius.

是自埋於民
自藏於畔
"He has buried himself among the people,
hidden himself among the fields.

其聲銷
其志無窮
His reputation fades away,
but his determination knows no end.

其口雖言
其心未嘗言
Though his mouth speaks,
his mind has never spoken.

方且與世違而心不屑與之俱
Perhaps he finds himself at odds with the age and, in his heart, disdains to go along with it.

是陸沈者也
He is one who has 'drowned in the midst of dry land.'

是其市南宜僚邪
I would guess that it is Yiliao from south of the Market."[^25-15]

[^25-15]: See sec. 20, p. 157, and sec. 24, p. 208.

子路請往召之
"May I go next door and call him over?" asked Zilu.

***

孔子曰已矣
"Let it be!" said Confucius.

彼知丘之著於己也
知丘之適楚也
"He knows that I am out to make a name for myself,
and he knows I am on my way to the capital of Chu.

以丘為必使楚王之召己也
彼且以丘為佞人也
He is sure to assume that I am trying to get the king of Chu to give me a position
and will accordingly take me for a sycophant.

夫若然者
其於佞人也羞聞其言
而況親見其身乎
A man like that
is ashamed even to hear the words of a sycophant,
much less appear in person before him!

而何以為存
What makes you think he is still at home, anyway?"

子路往視之
其室虛矣
Zilu went next door to have a look
and found the house deserted.

---

長梧封人問子牢曰君為政焉勿鹵莽
治民焉勿滅裂
The border guard of Zhangwu said to Zilao,[^25-16] "In running the government, you mustn't be slipshod;
in ordering the people, you mustn't be slapdash!

[^25-16]: A disciple of Confucius.

昔予為禾
In the past, I used to grow grain.

耕而鹵莽之
則其實亦鹵莽而報予
I plowed in a slipshod way
and got a slipshod crop in return.

芸而滅裂之
其實亦滅裂而報予
I weeded in a slapdash way
and got a slap-dash crop in return.

予來年變齊
深其耕而熟耰之
The following year, I changed my methods,
plowing deeper than before and raking with great care---
其禾蘩以滋
予終年厭飧
the grain grew thick and luxuriant,
and I had all I wanted to eat for the whole year!"

***

莊子聞之曰今人之治其形
理其心
多有似封人之所謂
Zhuangzi, hearing of this, said, "People of today, when they come to ordering their bodies
and regulating their minds, too,
often do it in a manner like that which the border guard described.

遁其天
離其性
滅其情
亡其神
以眾為
They turn their backs on the Heavenly part,
deviate from the inborn nature,
destroy the true form,
and annihilate the spirit,
just to be doing what the crowd is doing.

故鹵莽其性者
欲惡之孽
為性萑葦蒹葭
So he who is slipshod with his inborn nature
will find the evils of desire and hate
affecting his inborn nature like weeds and rushes.

始萌以扶吾形
尋擢吾性
When they first sprout up, he thinks they will be a comfort to the body,
but in time they end by stifling the inborn nature.

並潰漏發
不擇所出
Side by side, they begin to break out and ooze forth,
not on just one part of the body,
but all over.

漂疽疥癕
內熱溲膏
Festering ulcers and boils,
internal fevers and pus-filled urine---
是也
these are the results!"

---

柏矩學於老聃曰請之天下遊
Bo Ju, having studied under Lao Dan, said, "I would like permission to go wandering about the world."

***

老聃曰已矣
"Let it be!" said Lao Dan.

天下猶是也
"The world is right here."

***

又請之
老聃曰汝將何始
When Bo Ju repeated his request,
Lao Dan said, "Where will you go first?"

***

曰始於齊
"I will begin with Qi."

至齊
見辜人焉
When he arrived in Qi,
he saw the body of a criminal who had been executed.[^25-17]

[^25-17]: Bodies of executed criminals were exposed in the marketplace.

推而強之
解朝服而幕之
號天而哭之曰子乎子乎
Pushing and dragging until he had it laid out in proper position,
he took off his formal robes and covered it with them,
wailing to Heaven and crying out, "Alas, alas!

天下有大菑
子獨先離之
The world is in dire misfortune,
and you have been quicker than the rest of us to encounter it.

曰莫為盜
'Thou shalt not steal;
莫為殺人
thou shalt not murder!' they say.

榮辱立
然後睹所病
But when glory and disgrace have once been defined,
you will see suffering;
貨財聚
然後睹所爭
when goods and wealth have once been gathered together,
you will see wrangling.

今立人之所病
聚人之所爭
窮困人之身
使無休時
欲無至此
To define something that brings suffering to men,
to gather together what sets them to wrangling,
inflicting misery and weariness on them,
never granting them a time of rest,
and yet to hope somehow that they will not end up like this---
得乎
how could it be possible?

***

古之君人者以得為在民
以失為在己
以正為在民
以枉為在己
"The gentlemen of old attributed what success they had to the people
and what failure they had to themselves,
attributed what was upright to the people
and what was askew to themselves.

故一形有失其形者
退而自責
Therefore, if there was something wrong with the body of even a single being,
they would retire and take the blame themselves.

今則不然
But that is not the way it is done today.

匿為物而愚不識
They make things obscure and then blame people for not understanding;[^25-18]
大為難而罪不敢
they enlarge the difficulties and then punish people for not being able to cope with them;
重為任而罰不勝
they pile on responsibilities and then penalize people for not being able to fulfill them;
遠其塗而誅不至
they make the journey longer and then chastise people for not reaching the end of it.

[^25-18]: Following Yu Yue, I read *guo* in place of *yu*,
but perhaps the phrase should be further emended.

民知力竭
則以偽繼之
When the knowledge and strength of the people are exhausted,
they will begin to piece them out with artifice;
日出多偽
士民安得不偽
and when day by day the amount of artifice in the world increases,
how can men keep from resorting to artifice?

夫力不足則偽
A lack of strength invites artifice;
知不足則欺
a lack of knowledge invites deceit;
財不足則盜
a lack of goods invites theft.

盜竊之行
於誰責而可乎
But these thefts and robberies---
who in fact deserves the blame for them?"

---

蘧伯玉行年六十而六十化
Qu Boyu has been going along for sixty years and has changed sixty times.

未嘗不始於是之
而卒詘之以非也
There was not a single instance in which what he called right in the beginning
he did not, in the end, reject and call wrong.

未知今之所謂是之非五十九年非也
So now there's no telling whether what he calls right at the moment is not,
in fact, what he called wrong during the past fifty-nine years.

萬物有乎生而莫見其根
The ten thousand things have their life, yet no one sees its roots;
有乎出而莫見其門
they have their comings forth, yet no one sees the gate.

人皆尊其知之所知
而莫知恃其知之所不知而後知
Men all pay homage to what understanding understands,
but no one understands enough to rely on what understanding does not understand and thereby come to understand.

可不謂大疑乎
Can we call this anything but great perplexity?

已乎已乎
Let it be, let it be!

且無所逃
There is no place you can escape it.

此所謂然與然乎
This is what is called saying both "that is so" and "is that so?"[^25-19]

[^25-19]: Compare sec. 2, p. 17, "If so were really so," etc.

---

仲尼問於大史大弢伯常騫狶韋曰夫衛靈公飲酒湛樂
不聽國家之政
Confucius said to the Grand Historiographers Da Tao, Bo Changqian, and Xi Wei, "Duke Ling of Wei drank wine and wallowed in pleasure,
paying no heed to the government of the state;
田獵畢弋
不應諸侯之際
he went hunting and gaming with nets and stringed arrows,
ignoring his obligations to the other feudal lords.

其所以為靈公者何邪
How then does he come to be called Duke Ling?"[^25-20]

[^25-20]: Ling was the posthumous title bestowed on him by the court historiographers,
whose duty it was to choose a title that was appropriate to the life and moral qualities of the deceased ruler.
Ling may have either good or bad connotations, depending on how one interprets it.
In what follows, it is apparent that Confucius is taking it in a good sense,
Da Tao in a bad one, and Bo Changqian in both senses.

***

大弢曰是因是也
Da Tao said, "It fitted the facts."

***

伯常騫曰夫靈公有妻三人
同濫而浴
Bo Changqian said, "Duke Ling had three wives
with whom he would bathe in the same tub.

史鰌奉御而進所
搏幣而扶翼
But when Shi Qiu appeared in his presence to offer a gift of cloth,
the duke would accept it in person and respectfully attend Shi Qiu.[^25-21]

[^25-21]: This is Fukunaga's guess as to what this impenetrable sentence means; he emends *suo* to *er*.

其慢若彼之甚也
見賢人若此其肅也
He was so depraved as to bathe with his wives
and yet so correct in his behavior before a worthy man---
是其所以為靈公也
this is why he was titled Duke Ling."

***

狶韋曰夫靈公也死
卜葬於故墓不吉
卜葬於沙丘而吉
Xi Wei said, "When Duke Ling died,
we divined to see if he should be buried in the family graveyard,
but the omens were unfavorable.

掘之數仞
Then we divined to see if he should be buried at Sand Hill,
and the omens were favorable.

得石槨焉
洗而視之
有銘焉曰不馮其子
Digging down several fathoms, we found a stone coffin,
and when we had washed and examined it,
we discovered an inscription that said: 'You cannot depend on your heirs---
靈公奪而里之
Duke Ling will seize this plot for his own burial.'[^25-22]

[^25-22]: Following texts that read *mai* in place of *li*.

夫靈公之為靈也久矣
So it appears that Duke Ling had already been titled Ling for a long long time.

之二人何足以識之
How could these two here know enough to understand this!"

---

少知問於大公調曰何謂丘里之言
Little Understanding said to Great Impartial Accord, "What is meant by the term 'community words'?"

***

大公調曰丘里者
合十姓百名而以為風俗也
Great Impartial Accord said, "'Community words'
refers to the combining of ten surnames and a hundred given names into a single social unit.[^25-23]

[^25-23]: That is, "community words" are general terms or concepts
that subsume a number of differing particulars.
This section in parts resembles the discussion of semantics in sec. 17, pp. 129-131,
and in _Xunzi_, sec. 22.

合異以為同
Differences are combined into a sameness;
散同以為異
samenesses are broken up into differences.

今指馬之百體而不得馬
Now we may point to each of the hundred parts of a horse's body and never come up with a 'horse'---
而馬係於前者
yet here is the horse, tethered right before our eyes.

立其百體而謂之馬也
So we take the hundred parts and set up the term 'horse.'

是故丘山積卑而為高
Thus it is that hills and mountains pile up one little layer on another to reach loftiness;
江河合水而為大
the Yangtze and the Yellow River combine stream after stream to achieve magnitude;
大人合并而為公
and the Great Man combines and brings together things to attain generality.[^25-24]

[^25-24]: *Gong*, "common," "public," "generally accepted";
translated earlier as "impartial" in order to bring out the contract with "partiality."

是以自外入者
有主而不執
Therefore, when things enter his mind from the outside,
there is a host to receive them but not to cling to them;
由中出者
有正而不距
and when things come forth from his mind,
there is a mark to guide them but not to constrain them.[^25-25]

[^25-25]: Compare the similar passage in sec. 14, p. 114.

四時殊氣
天不賜
故歲成
The four seasons each differ in breath,
but Heaven shows no partiality[^25-26] among them,
and therefore the year comes to completion.

[^25-26]: Following Ma Xulun's emendation.
The word "breath" refers to the prevailing wind, temperature,
and other weather phenomena associated with each season.

五官殊職
君不私
故國治
The five government bureaus differ in function,
but the ruler shows no partiality among them,
and therefore the state is well ordered.

文武
大人不賜
故德備
In both civil and military affairs,
the Great Man shows no partiality,
and therefore his virtue is complete.[^25-27]

[^25-27]: This sentence does not fit into the parallelism and is probably defective.

萬物殊理
道不私
故無名
The ten thousand things differ in principle,
but the Way shows no partiality among them,
and therefore they may achieve namelessness.[^25-28]

[^25-28]: That is, can become one with the nameless Way.

無名故無為
Being nameless, they are without action;
無為而無不為
without action, yet there is nothing they do not do.

***

時有終始
世有變化
"The seasons have their end and beginning,
the ages their changes and transformations.

禍福淳淳
至有所拂者
而有所宜
Bad fortune and good, tripping and tumbling,
come now with what repels you,
now with what you welcome.

自殉殊面
有所正者
有所差
Set in your own opinion, at odds with others,
now you judge things to be upright,
now you judge them to be warped.

比於大澤
百材皆度
觀於大山
木石同壇
But if you could only be like the great swamp,
which finds accommodation for a hundred different timbers,
or take your model from the great mountain,
whose trees and rocks share a common groundwork!

此之謂丘里之言
This is what is meant by the term 'community words.'"

***

少知曰然則
謂之道
足乎
Little Understanding said, "Well, then,
if we call these [general concepts] the Way,
will that be sufficient?"

***

大公調曰不然
"Oh, no," said Great Impartial Accord.

今計物之數
不止於萬
"If we calculate the number of things that exist,
the count certainly does not stop at ten thousand.

而期曰萬物者
Yet we set a limit and speak of the 'ten thousand things'---
以數之多者號而讀之也
because we select a number that is large and agree to apply it to them.

是故天地者形之大者也
陰陽者氣之大者也
道者為之公
In the same way, heaven and earth are forms that are large,
the yin and yang are breaths that are large,
and the Way is the generality that embraces them.

因其大而號以讀之
則可也
If from the point of view of largeness we agree to apply [the name 'Way'] to it,
then there will be no objection.

已有之矣
乃將得比哉
則若以斯辯
譬猶狗馬
But if, having established this name,
we go on and try to compare it to the reality,
then it will be like trying
to compare a dog to a horse---
其不及遠矣
the distance between them is impossibly far."[^25-29]

[^25-29]: That is, whatever name we agree to use in designating the Way,
we must not suppose that it can in any sense adequately describe or convey an idea of the Way itself.

***

少知曰四方之內
六合之裏
萬物之所生惡起
Little Understanding said, "Here within the four directions
and the six realms,
where do the ten thousand things spring from when they come into being?"

***

太公調曰陰陽相照
相蓋相治
四時相代
相生相殺
Great Impartial Accord said, "The yin and yang shine on each other,
maim each other, heal each other;
the four seasons succeed each other,
give birth to each other, slaughter each other.

欲惡去就於是橋起
Desire and hatred, rejection and acceptance, thereupon rise up in succession;[^25-30]
雌雄片合於是庸有
the pairing of halves between male and female thereupon becomes a regular occurrence.

[^25-30]: Following Wang Yun's interpretation.

安危相易
Security and danger trade places with each other;
禍福相生
bad and good fortune give birth to each other;
緩急相摩
tense times and relaxed ones buffet each other;
聚散以成
gathering together and scattering bring it all to completion.

此名實之可紀
These names and realities can be recorded;
精微之可志也
their details and minute parts can be noted.

隨序之相理
橋運之相使
窮則反
終則始
The principle of following one another in orderly succession,
the property of moving in alternation,
turning back when they have reached the limit,
beginning again when they have ended---
此物之所有
these are inherent in things.

言之所盡
知之所至
極物而已
But that which words can adequately describe,
that which understanding can reach to,
extends only as far as the level of 'things,' no further.

覩道之人
不隨其所廢
不原其所起
The man who looks to the Way
does not try to track down what has disappeared,
does not try to trace the source of what springs up.

此議之所止
This is the point at which debate comes to a stop."

***

少知曰季真之莫為
接子之或使
Little Understanding said, "Ji Zhen's contention that 'nothing does it'
and Jiezi's contention that 'something makes it like this'---
二家之議
孰正於其情
孰偏於其理
of the views of these two schools,
which correctly describes the truth of the matter,
and which is one sided in its understanding of principles?"[^25-31]

[^25-31]: Ji Zhen and Jiezi are philosophers of whom little is known.
As we see here, the latter taught the existence of some prime mover or governor of the universe,
while the former denied it.

***

太公調曰雞鳴狗吠
Great Impartial Accord said, "Chickens squawk, dogs bark---
是人之所知
this is something men understand.

雖有大知
不能以言讀其所自化
又不能以意其所將為
But no matter how great their understanding,
they cannot explain in words how the chicken and the dog have come to be what they are,
nor can they imagine in their minds what they will become in the future.

斯而析之
精至於無倫
大至於不可圍
You may pick apart and analyze
till you have reached what is so minute that it is without form,
what is so large that it cannot be encompassed.

或之使
莫之為
未免於物而終以為過
But whether you say that 'nothing does it'
or that 'something makes it like this,'
you have not yet escaped from the realm of 'things,' and so in the end you fall into error.

或使則實
If 'something makes it like this,' then it is real;
莫為則虛
if 'nothing does it,' then it is unreal.

有名有實
是物之居
When there are names and realities,
you are in the presence of things.

無名無實
在物之虛
When there are no names and realities,
you exist in the absence of things.[^25-32]

[^25-32]: Are these two sentences meant to express a contrast between the relativistic and the absolute viewpoints,
or to be two statements of the relativistic viewpoint? I am unable to decide.

可言可意
言而愈疏
You can talk about it, you can think about it;
but the more you talk about it, the further away you get from it.

***

未生不可忌
已死不可阻
"Before they are born, things cannot decline to be born;
already dead, they cannot refuse to go.

死生非遠也
理不可睹
Death and life are not far apart,
though the principle that underlies them cannot be seen.

或之使
莫之為
'Nothing does it,'
'something makes it like this'---
疑之所假
these are speculations born out of doubt.

吾觀之本
其往無窮
I look for the roots of the past,
but they extend back and back without end.

吾求之末
其來無止
I search for the termination of the future,
but it never stops coming at me.

無窮無止
言之無也
與物同理
Without end, without stop,
it is the absence of words,
which shares the same principle with things themselves.

或使
莫為
But 'nothing does it,'
'something makes it like this'---
言之本也
與物終始
these are the commencement of words,
and they begin and end along with things.

***

道不可有
有不可無
"The Way cannot be thought of as being,
nor can it be thought of as nonbeing.

道之為名
所假而行
In calling it the Way,
we are only adopting a temporary expedient.

或使
莫為
'Nothing does it,'
'something makes it like this'---
在物一曲
these occupy a mere corner of the realm of things.

夫胡為於大方
What connection could they have with the Great Method?

言而足
則終日言而盡道
If you talk in a worthy manner,
you can talk all day long, and all of it will pertain to the Way.

言而不足
則終日言而盡物
But if you talk in an unworthy manner,
you can talk all day long, and all of it will pertain to mere things.

道物之極
言默不足以載
The perfection of the Way and things---
neither words nor silence is worthy of expressing it.

非言非默
Not to talk, not to be silent---
議其有極
this is the highest form of debate."
