## 27 寓言 Imputed Words

寓言十九
Imputed words make up nine-tenths of it;
重言十七
repeated words make up seven-tenths of it;
卮言日出
和以天倪
goblet words come forth day after day,
harmonizing things in the Heavenly Equality.[^27-1]

[^27-1]: See p. 17. The passage that follows describes three literary devises
used in the _Zhuangzi_ as a whole: (1) *yuyan*, "imputed words," words
put into the mouth of historical or fictional persons to make them more compelling;
(2) *chongyan*, "repeated words" (another interpretation would make it *zhongyan* or "weighty words"),
words of the wise old men of the past that are "repeated" or quoted to give authority to the argument;
and (3) *zhiyan*, "goblet words," words that are like a goblet that tips when full and rights itself when empty,
that is, that adapt to and follow along with the fluctuating nature of the world
and thus achieve a state of harmony.

***

寓言十九
藉外論之
These imputed words that make up nine-tenths of it
are like persons brought in from outside for the purpose of exposition.

親父不為其子媒
親父譽之
不若非其父者也
A father does not act as go-between for his own son
because the praises of the father
would not be as effective as the praises of an outsider.

非吾罪也人之罪也
與己同則應不與己同則反
同於己為是之異於己為非之
It is the fault of other men, not mine,
[that I must resort to such a device, for if I were to speak in my own words],
then men would respond only to what agrees with their own views and reject what does not,
would pronounce "right" what agrees with their own views and "wrong" what does not.

***

重言十七
所以已言也
These repeated words that make up seven-tenths of it
are intended to put an end to argument.

是為耆艾
They can do this because they are the words of the elders.

年先矣而無經緯本末
以期年耆者
是非先也
If, however, one is ahead of others in age but does not have a grasp of the warp and woof, the root and branch of things,
that is commensurate with his years,
then he is not really ahead of others.

人而無以先人無人道也
人而無人道是之謂陳人
An old man who is not in some way ahead of others has not grasped the Way of man,
and if he has not grasped the Way of man, he deserves to be looked on as a mere stale remnant of the past.

***

卮言日出
和以天倪
因以曼衍
所以窮年
With these goblet words that come forth day after day,
I harmonize all things in the Heavenly Equality,
leave them to their endless changes,
and so live out my years.

不言則齊
As long as I do not say anything about them, they are a unity.

齊與言不齊
But the unity and what I say about it have ceased to be a unity;
言與齊不齊也
what I say and the unity have ceased to be a unity.[^27-2]

[^27-2]: Compare sec. 2, p. 12,
but it seems odd that the two clauses should repeat the same idea.

故曰無言
Therefore I say, we must have no-words!

言無言
終身言
未嘗言
With words that are no-words,
you may speak all your life long,
and you will never have said anything.

終身不言
未嘗不言
Or you may go through your whole life *without* speaking them,
in which case you will never have stopped speaking.

***

有自也而可
There is that which makes things acceptable;
有自也而不可
there is that which makes things unacceptable;
有自也而然
there is that which makes things so;
有自也而不然
there is that which makes things not so.

惡乎然
What makes them so?

然於然
Making them so makes them so.

惡乎不然
What makes them not so?

不然於不然
Making them not so makes them not so.

惡乎可
What makes them acceptable?

可於可
Making them acceptable makes them acceptable.

惡乎不可
What makes them not acceptable?

不可於不可
Making them not acceptable makes them not acceptable.

物固有所然
Things all must have that which is so;
物固有所可
things all must have that which is acceptable.

無物不然
無物不可
There is nothing that is not so,
nothing that is not acceptable.[^27-3]

[^27-3]: Compare sec. 2, p. 10.

非卮言日出
和以天倪
孰得其久
If there were no goblet words coming forth day after day
to harmonize all by the Heavenly Equality,
then how could I survive for long?

***

萬物皆種也
以不同形相禪
The ten thousand things all come from the same seed,
and with their different forms they give place to one another.

始卒若環
莫得其倫
Beginning and end are part of a single ring,
and no one can comprehend its principle.

是謂天均
天均者天倪也
This is called Heaven the Equalizer,
which is the same as the Heavenly Equality.

---

莊子謂惠子曰孔子行年六十
而六十化
Zhuangzi said to Huizi, "Confucius has been going along for sixty years,
and he has changed sixty times.

始時所是
卒而非之
What at the beginning he used to call right
he has ended up calling wrong.

未知今之所謂是之非
五十九年非也
So now there's no telling whether what he calls right at the moment is not,
in fact, what he called wrong during the past fifty-nine years."[^27-4]

[^27-4]: The same remark was made on p. 222 in reference to Qu Boyu.

***

惠子曰孔子勤志服知也
Huizi said, "Confucius keeps working away at it, trying to make knowledge serve him."

***

莊子曰孔子謝之矣
"Oh, no---Confucius has given all that up," said Zhuangzi.

而其未之嘗言
"It's just that he never talks about it.

孔子云夫受才乎大本
復靈以生
Confucius said, 'We receive our talents from the Great Source,
and with the spirit hidden within us,[^27-5] we live.'

[^27-5]: Following Zhang Binglin's interpretation.

鳴而當律
言而當法
利義陳乎前
而好惡是非
直服人之口而已矣
[As for you, you] sing on key,
you talk by the rules,
you line up 'profit' and 'righteousness' before us,
but your 'likes' and 'dislikes,' your 'rights' and 'wrongs,'
are merely something that command lip service from others, that's all.

使人乃以心服而不敢蘁立
If you could make men pay service with their minds and never dare stand up in defiance---
定天下之定
this would settle things for the world so they would stay settled.

已乎已乎
But let it be, let it be!

吾且不得及彼乎
As for me, what hope have I of ever catching up with Confucius?"

---

曾子再仕而心再化
Zengzi twice held office, each time with a change of heart.[^27-6]

[^27-6]: Zeng Shen, the paragon of filial piety, appeared earlier; see esp. sec. 26, p. 227.

曰吾及親仕
三釜而心樂
"The first time, when I was taking care of my parents,
I received a salary of only three *fu* of grain, and yet my heart was happy," he said.

後仕三千鍾
而不洎吾心悲
"The second time I received a salary of three thousand *zhong*,
but I no longer had them to take care of, and my heart was sad."

***

弟子問於仲尼曰若參者
可謂無所縣其罪乎
One of the disciples asked Confucius, "May we say that someone like Zeng Shen
has escaped the crime of entanglement?"

***

曰既已縣矣
"But he was already entangled!

夫無所縣者
可以有哀乎
If he hadn't been entangled,
how could he have had any cause for sorrow?

彼視三釜
三千鍾
如觀雀蚊虻相過乎前也
He would have regarded three *fu*
or three thousand *zhong*
as so many sparrows or mosquitoes passing in front of him!"

---

顏成子游謂東郭子綦曰自吾聞子之言
一年而野
Yan Cheng Ziyou said to Ziqi of East Wall, "When I began listening to your words,
the first year I was a bumpkin;
二年而從
the second I followed along;
三年而通
the third I worked into it;
四年而物
the fourth I was just another thing;
五年而來
the fifth it began to come;
六年而鬼入
the sixth the spirits descended to me;
七年而天成
the seventh the Heavenly part was complete;
八年而不知死不知生
the eighth I didn't understand death and didn't understand life;
九年而大妙
and with the ninth I reached the Great Mystery.

***

生有為死也
"When the living start doing things, they are dead.

勸公以其死也
有自也
When they strive for public causes because private ones mean death,
they are following a path.

而生陽也
無自也
But what lives in the light
is following no path at all.[^27-7]

[^27-7]: Literally, "what lives in the yang"; compare sec. 2, p. 8.
"And when their minds draw near to death, nothing can restore them to the light."

而果然乎
What is the result then?

惡乎其所適
How can there be any place that is fitting?

惡乎其所不適
How can there be any place that isn't fitting?

天有曆數
地有人據
吾惡乎求之
Heaven has its cycles and numbers,
earth its flats and slopes[^27-8]---
yet why should I seek to comprehend them?

[^27-8]: Following Zhang Binglin's interpretation.

莫知其所終
No one knows when they will end---
若之何其無命也
how then can we say that they are fated to die?

莫知其所始
No one knows when they began---
若之何其有命也
how then can we say that they are not fated to die?

有以相應也
There seems to be something that responds---
若之何其無鬼邪
how then can we say there are no spirits?

無以相應也
There seems to be something that does not respond---
若之何其有鬼邪
how then can we say that spirits do exist?"

---

眾罔兩問於景曰若向也俯而今也仰
Penumbra said to Shadow, "A little while ago you were looking down, and now you're looking up;
向也括而今被髮
a little while ago your hair was bound up, and now it's hanging loose;
向也坐而今也起
a little while ago you were sitting, and now you're standing up;
向也行而今也止
a little while ago you were walking, and now you're still---
何也
why is this?"

***

景曰搜搜也
Shadow said, "Quibble, quibble!

奚稍問也
Why bother asking about such things?

予有而不知其所以
I do them, but I don't know why.

予蜩甲也
蛇蛻也
I'm the shell of the cicada,
the skin of the snake---
似之而非也
something that seems to be but isn't.

火與日吾屯也
In firelight or sunlight, I draw together;
陰與夜吾代也
in darkness or night, I disappear.

彼吾所以有待邪
But do you suppose I have to wait around for those things?

而況乎以有待者乎
(And how much less so in the case of that which waits for nothing!)

彼來則我與之來
If those things come, then I come with them;
彼往則我與之往
if they go, then I go with them;
彼強陽則我與之強陽
if they come with the Powerful Yang, then I come with the Powerful Yang.

強陽者
But this Powerful Yang---
又何以有問乎
why ask questions about it?"[^27-9]

[^27-9]: The term "Powerful Yang" appeared in sec. 22, p. 180;
the yang is the element of fire and hence is the essence of the firelight and sunlight mentioned earlier.
This whole section is a reworking of the passage in sec. 2, p. 18.

---

陽子居南之沛
老聃西遊於秦
邀於郊
至於梁而遇老子
Yang Ziju went south to Pei,
and when he got to Liang,
he went out to the edge of the city to greet Lao Dan,
who had been traveling west to Qin,
and escort him in.

老子中道
仰天而歎曰始以汝為可教
今不可也
Laozi stood in the middle of the road,
looked up to heaven, and sighed, saying, "At first I thought that you could be taught,
but now I see it's hopeless!"

***

陽子居不答
至舍
進盥漱巾櫛
脫屨戶外
膝行而前曰向者弟子欲請夫子
夫子行不閒是以不敢
Yang Ziju made no reply,
but when they reached the inn,
he fetched a basin of water, a towel, and a comb and,
taking off his shoes outside the door of the room,
came crawling forward on his knees and said, "Earlier I had hoped to ask you, sir, what you meant by your remark,
but I saw that you were occupied and didn't dare.

今閒矣
請問其過
Now that you have a free moment,
may I ask where my fault lies?"

***

老子曰而睢睢盱盱
Laozi said, "High and mighty, proud and haughty---
而誰與居
who could stand to live with you![^27-10]

[^27-10]: According to another interpretation,
these four adjectives are descriptions of good qualities, that is,
of what Laozi wants Yang to become.
Fukunaga takes them as synonymous with those in sec. 7, p. 55,
translated as "peaceful and easy, wide-eyed, and blank."

大白若辱
The greatest purity looks like shame;
盛德若不足
abundant virtue seems to be insufficient."[^27-11]

[^27-11]: Almost identical with a passage in _Daodejing_ XLI.

***

陽子居蹴然變容曰敬聞命矣其往也
舍者迎將其家
When Yang Ziju first arrived at the inn,
the people in the inn came out to greet him.

公執席
妻執巾櫛
舍者避席
煬者避灶
The innkeeper stood ready with a mat,
his wife with towel and comb,
while the other guests moved politely off their mats,
and those who had been warming themselves at the stove stepped aside.

其反也
舍者與之爭席矣
But when Yang returned from his interview with Laozi,
the people at the inn tried to push him right off his own mat.[^27-12]

[^27-12]: Because he has ceased to look and act like a man of any importance,
that is, had become a true follower of the Way.
