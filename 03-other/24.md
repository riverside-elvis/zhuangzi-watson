## 24 徐無鬼 Xu Wugui

徐無鬼因女商見魏武侯
Through Nü Shang, the recluse Xu Wugui obtained an interview with Marquis Wu of Wei.

武侯勞之曰先生病矣
Marquis Wu greeted him with words of comfort, saying, "Sir, you are not well.

苦於山林之勞
故乃肯見於寡人
I suppose that the hardships of life in the mountain forests have become too much for you,
and so at last you have consented to come and visit me."

***

徐無鬼曰我則勞於君
"I am the one who should be comforting you!" said Xu Wugui.

君有何勞於我
"What reason have you to comfort me?

君將盈耆欲
長好惡
則性命之情病矣
If you try to fulfill all your appetites and desires
and indulge your likes and dislikes,
then you will bring affliction to the true form of your inborn nature and fate.

君將黜耆欲
掔好惡
則耳目病矣
And if you try to deny your appetites and desires
and forcibly change your likes and dislikes,
then you will bring affliction to your ears and eyes.

我將勞君
It is my place to comfort you---
君有何勞於我
what reason have you to comfort me!"

***

武侯超然不對
Marquis Wu, looking very put out, made no reply.

少焉徐無鬼曰嘗語君吾相狗也
After a little while, Xu Wugui said, "Let me try telling you about the way I judge dogs.

下之質執飽而止
A dog of the lowest quality thinks only of catching its fill of prey---
是狸德也
that is, it has the nature of a wildcat.

中之質若視日
One of middling quality always seems to be looking up at the sun.[^24-1]

[^24-1]: It is proud and self-confident.

上之質若亡其一
But one of the highest quality acts as though it had lost its own identity.

吾相狗又不若吾相馬也
And I'm even better at judging horses than I am at judging dogs.

吾相馬
直者中繩
曲者中鉤
方者中矩
圓者中規
是國馬也
When I judge a horse,
if he can gallop as straight as a plumb line,
arc as neat as a curve,
turn as square as a T square,
and round as true as a compass,
then I'd say he was a horse for the kingdom to boast of.

而未若天下馬也
But not a horse for the whole world to boast of.

天下馬
A horse the whole world can boast of---
有成材
his talents are already complete.

若卹若失
若喪其一若是者
超軼絕塵
He seems dazed, he seems lost,
he seems to have become unaware of his own identity,
and in this way he overtakes, passes, and leaves the others behind in the dust.

不知其所
You can't tell where he's gone to!"

***

武侯大悅而笑
Marquis Wu, greatly pleased, burst out laughing.

***

徐無鬼出女商曰先生獨何以說吾君乎
When Xu Wugui emerged from the interview, Nü Shang said, "Sir, may I ask what you were talking to our ruler about?

吾所以說
吾君者橫說之則以詩書禮樂
When I talk to him,
I talk to him back and forth about the *Odes* and *Documents*, about ritual and music;
從說之則以金板六弢
and then I talk to him up and down about the *Golden Tablets* and the *Six Bow-Cases*.[^24-2]

[^24-2]: Probably works on military affairs, though their identity is uncertain.

奉事而大有功者不可為數
而吾君未嘗啟齒
I have made proposals that led to outstanding success in more cases than can be counted,
and yet he never so much as bared his teeth in a smile.

今先生何以說吾君
使吾君說若此乎
Now what were you talking to him about
that you managed to delight him in this fashion?"

***

徐無鬼曰吾直告之吾相狗馬耳
Xu Wugui said, "I was merely explaining to him how I judge dogs and horses, that was all."

女商曰若是乎
"Was that all?" said Nü Shang.

***

曰子不聞夫越之流人乎
"Haven't you ever heard about the men who are exiled to Yue?" said Xu Wugui.

去國數日
見其所知而喜
"A few days after they have left their homelands,
they are delighted if they come across an old acquaintance.

去國旬月
見其所嘗見於國中者喜
When a few weeks or a month has passed,
they are delighted if they come across someone they had known by sight when they were at home.

及期年也
見似人者而喜矣
And by the time a year has passed,
they are delighted if they come across someone who even looks as though he might be a countryman.

不亦去人滋久
思人滋深乎
The longer they are away from their countrymen,
the more deeply they long for them---
isn't that it?

夫逃虛空者
藜藋柱乎鼪鼬之逕
踉位其空
聞人足音跫然而喜矣
A man who has fled into the wilderness,
where goosefoot and woodbine tangle the little trails of the polecat and the weasel,
and has lived there in emptiness and isolation for a long time,
will be delighted if he hears so much as the rustle of a human footfall.

而況乎兄弟親戚之謦欬其側者乎
And how much more so if he hears his own brothers and kin chattering and laughing at his side!

久矣夫
莫以真人之言謦欬吾君之側乎
It has been a long time, I think,
since one who speaks like a True Man has sat chattering and laughing at our ruler's side."

---

徐無鬼見武侯
Xu Wugui was received in audience by Marquis Wu.

武侯曰先生居山林
食芧栗
厭蔥韭
以賓寡人
"Sir," said Marquis Wu, "for a long time now, you have lived in your mountain forest,
eating acorns and chestnuts,
getting along on wild leeks and scallions,
and scorning me completely.

久矣夫今老邪
其欲干酒肉之味邪
Now is it old age,
or perhaps a longing for the taste of meat and wine, that has brought you here?

其寡人亦有社稷之福邪
Or perhaps you have come to bring a blessing to my altars of the soil and grain."

***

徐無鬼曰
無鬼生於貧賤
未嘗敢飲食君之酒肉
Xu Wugui said, "I was born to poverty and lowliness
and have never ventured to eat or drink any of your wine or meat, my lord.

將來勞君也
I have come in order to comfort you."

***

君曰何哉
"What?" said the ruler.

奚勞寡人
"Why should you comfort me?"

曰勞君之神與形
"I want to bring comfort to your spirit and body."

武侯曰何謂邪
"What do you mean by that?" asked Marquis Wu.

***

徐無鬼曰天地之養也一
Xu Wugui said, "Heaven and earth provide nourishment for all things alike.

登高不可以為長
To have ascended to a high position cannot be considered an advantage;
居下不可以為短
to live in lowliness cannot be considered a handicap.

君獨為萬乘之主
以苦一國之民
以養耳目鼻口
Now you, as the sole ruler of this land of ten thousand chariots,
may tax the resources of the entire populace of your realm
in nourishing the appetites of your ears and eyes, your nose and mouth.

夫神者不自許也
But the spirit will not permit such a way of life.

夫神者好和而惡姦
The spirit loves harmony and hates licentiousness.

夫姦病也故勞之
Licentiousness is a kind of sickness, and that is why I have come to offer my comfort.

唯君所病之何也
I just wonder, my lord, how aware you are of your own sickness."[^24-3]

[^24-3]: The meaning is doubtful. As Fukunaga points out, the sentence seems to be related to _Daodejing_ LXXI.

***

武侯曰欲見先生久矣
Marquis Wu said, "I have, in fact, been hoping to see you for a long time, sir.

吾欲愛民而為義偃兵
I would like to cherish my people, practice righteousness, and lay down the weapons of war---
可乎
how would that do?"

***

徐無鬼曰不可
"It won't!" said Xu Wugui.

愛民害民之始也
"To cherish the people is to open the way to harming them!

為義偃兵造兵之本也
To practice righteousness and lay down your weapons is to sow the seeds for more weapon wielding!

君自此為之則殆不成
If you go at it this way, I'm afraid you will never succeed.

凡成美惡器也
All attempts to create something admirable are the weapons of evil.

君雖為仁義
幾且偽哉
You may think you are practicing benevolence and righteousness,
but in effect you will be creating a kind of artificiality.

形固造形
When a model exists, copies will be made of it;
成固有伐
when success has been gained, boasting follows;
變固外戰
when debate[^24-4] exists, there will be outbreaks of hostility.

[^24-4]: I follow Fukunaga in taking *bian* as a loan for the *bian* that means argument or debate.

君亦必
無盛鶴列於麗譙之間
無徒驥於錙壇之宮
On the other hand,
it will not do, my lord, to have files of marching soldiers filling the whole area in your fortress towers,
or ranks of cavalry drawn up before the Palace of the Black Altar.

無藏逆於得
Do not store in your heart what is contrary to your interests.

無以巧勝人
Do not try to outdo others in skill.

無以謀勝人
Do not try to overcome others by stratagems.

無以戰勝人
Do not try to conquer others in battle.

夫殺人之士民兼人之土地
以養吾私與吾神者
其戰不知孰善勝之惡乎在
If you kill the officials and people of another ruler and annex his lands,
using them to nourish your personal desires and your spirit,
then I cannot say which contender is the better fighter and to which the real victory belongs!

君若勿已矣
修胸中之誠
以應天地之情而勿攖
If you must do something,
cultivate the sincerity that is in your breast
and use it to respond without opposition to the true form of Heaven and earth.

夫民死已脫矣
Then the people will have won their reprieve from death.

君將惡乎用夫偃兵哉
What need will there be for you to resort to this 'laying down of weapons'?"

---

黃帝將見大隗乎具茨之山
The Yellow Emperor set out to visit Great Clod at Juci Mountain.[^24-5]

[^24-5]: Great Clod (I take *wei* as standing for *kuai*) here represents the way.
The names of the Yellow Emperor's attendants probably have some allegorical significance as well,
but their exact meaning is uncertain, and it seems best not to attempt to translate them.

方明為御
昌宇驂乘
Fang Ming was his carriage driver,
while Chang Yu rode at his right side;
張若謵朋前馬
昆閽滑稽後車
Zhang Ruo and Xi Peng led the horses,
and Kun Hun and Gu Ji followed behind the carriage.

至於襄城之野
七聖皆迷無所問塗
By the time they reached the wilds of Xiangcheng,
all seven sages had lost their way and could find no one to ask for directions.

適遇牧馬童子問塗焉
Just then they happened on a young boy herding horses and asked him for directions.

曰若知具茨之山乎
"Do you know the way to Juci Mountain?" they inquired.

曰然
"Yes."

若知大隗之所存乎
"And do you know where Great Clod is to be found?"

曰然
"Yes."

***

黃帝曰異哉小童
"What an astonishing young man!" said the Yellow Emperor.

非徒知具茨之山
又知大隗之所存
"You not only know the way to Juci Mountain,
but you even know where Great Clod is to be found!

請問為天下
Do you mind if I ask you about how to govern the empire?"

***

小童曰夫為天下者亦若此而已矣
"Governing the empire just means doing what I'm doing here, doesn't it?" said the young boy.

又奚事焉
"What about it is special?

予少而自遊於六合之內
予適有瞀病
When I was little, I used to go wandering within the Six Realms,
but in time I contracted a disease that blurred my eyesight.

有長者教予曰若乘日之車
而遊於襄城之野
今予病少痊
An elderly gentleman advised me to mount the chariot of the sun
and go wandering in the wilds of Xiangcheng,
and now my illness is getting a little better.

予又且復遊於六合之外
Soon I can go wandering once more, this time beyond the Six Realms.

夫為天下亦若此而已
Governing the empire just means doing what I'm doing---
予又奚事焉
I don't see why it has to be anything special."

***

黃帝曰夫為天下者則誠非吾子之事
"It's true that governing the empire is not something that need concern you, sir," said the Yellow Emperor.

雖然請問為天下
"Nevertheless, I would like to ask you how it should be done."

***

小童辭
黃帝又問
小童曰夫為天下者亦奚以異乎牧馬者哉
The young boy made excuses,
but when the Yellow Emperor repeated his request,
the boy said, "Governing the empire, I suppose, is not much different from herding horses.

亦去其害馬者
Get rid of whatever is harmful to the horses---
而已矣
that's all."

***

黃帝再拜稽首
稱天師而退
The Yellow Emperor, addressing the boy as "Heavenly Master,"
bowed twice, touching his head to the ground, and retired.

---

知士無思慮之變則不樂
The wise man is not happy without the modulations of idea and thought;
辯士無談說之序則不樂
the rhetorician is not happy without the progression of argument and rebuttal;
察士無淩誶之事則不樂
the examiner is not happy without the tasks of interrogation and intimidation.

皆囿於物者也
All are penned in by these things.

招世之士興朝
Men who attract the attention of the age win glory at court;
中民之士榮官
men who hit it off well with the people shine in public office;
筋力之士矜難
men of strength and sinew welcome hardship;
勇敢之士奮患
men of bravery and daring are spurred on by peril;
兵革之士樂戰
men of arms and armor delight in combat;
枯槁之士宿名
men of haggard-hermit looks reach out for fame;
法律之士廣治
men of laws and regulations long for broader legislation;
禮教之士敬容
men of ritual and instruction revere appearances;
仁義之士貴際
men of benevolence and righteousness value human relationships.

農夫無草萊之事則不比
The farmer is not content if he does not have his work in the fields and weed patches;
商賈無市井之事則不比
the merchant is not content if he does not have his affairs at the marketplace and well side.

庶人有旦暮之業則勸
The common people work hardest when they have their sunup-to-sundown occupations;
百工有器械之巧則壯
the hundred artisans are most vigorous when they are exercising their skills with tools and machines.

錢財不積則貪者憂
If his goods and coins do not pile up, the greedy man frets;
權勢不尤則夸者悲
if his might and authority do not increase, the ambitious man grieves.

勢物之徒樂變
遭時有所用
不能無為也
Servants to circumstance and things, they delight in change,
and if the moment comes when they can put their talents to use,
then they cannot keep from acting.

此皆順比於歲
不物於易者也
In this way, they all follow along with the turning years,
letting themselves be changed by things.[^24-6]

[^24-6]: I follow Ma Xulun in reading *er* in place of *bu*.

馳其形性
潛之萬物
終身不反
Driving their bodies and natures on and on,
they drown in the ten thousand things and,
to the end of their days never turn back.

悲夫
Pitiful, are they not?

---

莊子曰射者非前期而中
謂之善射
天下皆羿也
Zhuangzi said, "If an archer, without taking aim at the mark, just happens to hit it,
and we dub him a skilled archer,
then everyone in the world can be an Archer Yi---
可乎
all right?"

惠子曰可
"All right," said Huizi.

***

莊子曰天下非有公是也
而各是其所是
天下皆堯也
Zhuangzi said, "If there is no publicly accepted 'right' in the world,
but each person takes right to be what he himself thinks is right,
then everyone in the world can be a Yao---
可乎
all right?"

惠子曰可
"All right," said Huizi.

***

莊子曰然則儒墨楊秉四
與夫子為五
Zhuangzi said, "Well then, here are the four schools of the Confucians, Mo, Yang, and Bing[^24-7]
and, with your own, that makes five.

[^24-7]: The philosophers Mo Di and Yang Zhu appeared on p. 61.
Bing is the polite name of the logician Gongsun Long (see p. 135);
some scholars take the fourth philosopher to be Song Keng (see p. 291).

果孰是邪
Now which of you is, in fact, right?

或者若魯遽者邪
Or is it perhaps like the case of Lu Ju?

其弟子曰我得夫子之道矣
His disciple said to him, 'Master, I have grasped your Way.

吾能冬爨鼎而夏造冰矣
I can build a fire under the cauldron in winter and make ice in summer.'

魯遽曰是直以陽召陽
以陰召陰
'But that is simply using the yang to attract the yang,
and the yin to attract the yin,' said Lu Ju.[^24-8]

[^24-8]: Winter is dominated by yin, the element of cold and water;
summer by yang, the element of heat and fire.
But to produce fire, the disciple must have utilized some source of heat,
and to produce ice, some source of cold;
hence he was merely "using the yang to attract the yang," etc.

非吾所謂道也
'That is not what I call the Way!

吾示子乎吾道
I will show you my Way!'

於是為之調瑟
廢一於堂
廢一於室
Thereupon he tuned two lutes,
placed one in the hall,
and the other in an inner room.

鼓宮宮動
When he struck the *gong* note on one lute, the *gong* on the other lute sounded;
鼓角角動
when he struck the *jue* note, the other *jue* sounded---
音律同矣
the pitch of the two instruments was in perfect accord.

夫或改調一弦
於五音無當也
Then he changed the tuning of one string
so that it no longer corresponded to any of the five notes.

鼓之二十五弦皆動
When he plucked this string, it set all the twenty-five strings of the other instrument to jangling.

未始異於聲
But he was still using sounds to produce his effect;
而音之君已
in this case it just happened to be the note that governs the other notes.

且若是者邪
Now is this the way it is in your case?"[^24-9]

[^24-9]: The point of the story seems to be that although Lu Ju made fun of his disciple for
"simply using the yang to attract the yang," his own stunts were confined to the same level;
that is, he used sounds to produce sounds.
In the same way, the various philosophers debate back and forth,
but none ever succeeds in going beyond the level of the relative.

***

惠子曰今夫儒墨楊秉
且方與我以辯
相拂以辭
相鎮以聲
Huizi said, "The followers of Confucius, Mo, Yang, and Bing
often engage with me in debate,
each of us trying to overwhelm the others with phrases
and to silence them with shouts---
而未始吾非也
but so far they have never proved me wrong.

則奚若矣
So what do you make of that?"

***

莊子曰齊人蹢子於宋者
其命閽也不以完
Zhuangzi said, "A man of Qi sold his own son into service in Song,
having dubbed him Gatekeeper and maimed him;[^24-10]
其求鈃鍾也以束縛
but when he acquired any bells or chimes, he wrapped them up carefully to prevent breakage.

[^24-10]: It was the custom to employ condemned criminals
who had had their feet cut off or maimed as gatekeepers,
though not, as in this case, deliberately to maim men for that purpose.

其求唐子也
而未始出域
Another man went looking for a lost son
but was unwilling to go any farther than the border in his search---
有遺類矣夫
there are men as mixed up as this, you know.

楚人寄而蹢閽者
夜半於無人之時
而與舟人鬥
Or like the man of Chu who had been maimed and sold into service as a gatekeeper
and who, in the middle of the night when no one else was around,
picked a fight with the boatman.

未始離於岑
而足以造於怨也
Though he didn't actually arouse any criticism,
what he did was enough to create the grounds for a nasty grudge."[^24-11]

[^24-11]: This last paragraph is all but unintelligible as it stands.
For the most part, I follow Fukunaga's emendations and interpretations.
Zhuangzi is warning Huizi that his debates with the other philosophers may actually put him in peril.

---

莊子送葬
過惠子之墓
Zhuangzi was accompanying a funeral
when he passed by Huizi's grave.

顧謂從者曰郢人堊慢其鼻端若蠅翼
使匠石斲之
Turning to his attendants, he said, "There was once a plasterer who, if he got a speck of mud on the tip of his nose no thicker than a fly's wing,
would get his friend Carpenter Shi to slice it off for him.

匠石運斤成風
聽而斲之
盡堊而鼻不傷
郢人立不失容
Carpenter Shi, whirling his hatchet with a noise like the wind,
would accept the assignment and proceed to slice,
removing every bit of mud without injury to the nose,
while the plasterer just stood there completely unperturbed.

宋元君聞之
召匠石曰嘗試為寡人為之
Lord Yuan of Song, hearing of this feat,
summoned Carpenter Shi and said, 'Could you try performing it for me?'

匠石曰臣則嘗能斲之
But Carpenter Shi replied, 'It's true that I was once able to slice like that---
雖然臣之質死久矣
but the material I worked on has been dead these many years.'

自夫子之死也
吾無以為質矣
Since you died, Master Hui,
I have had no material to work on.

吾無與言之矣
There's no one I can talk to any more."

---

管仲有病
桓公問之
When Guan Zhong fell ill,
Duke Huan went to inquire how he was.[^24-12]

[^24-12]: On Guan Zhong and Duke Huan, see p. 150.

曰仲父之病病矣
"Father Zhong," he said, "you are very ill.

可不謂云
If---can I help but say it?---
至於大病
則寡人惡乎屬國而可
if your illness should become critical,
then to whom could I entrust the affairs of the state?"

***

管仲曰公誰欲與
Guan Zhong said, "To whom would Your Grace like to entrust them?"

公曰鮑叔牙
"Bao Shuya," said the duke.

***

曰不可
"That will never do!

其為人
絜廉善士也
He is a fine man,
a man of honesty and integrity.

其於不己若者不比之
But he will have nothing to do with those who are not like himself.

又一聞人之過
終身不忘
And if he once hears of some-one's error,
he won't forget it to the end of his days.

使之治國
上且鉤乎君
下且逆乎民
If he were given charge of the state,
he would be sure to tangle with you on the higher level
and rile the people below him.

其得罪於君也
將弗久矣
It would be no time at all
before he did something you considered unpardonable."

***

公曰然則孰可
"Well then, who will do?" asked the duke.

***

對曰勿已則隰朋可
"If I must give an answer, then I would say that Xi Peng will do.

其為人也上忘而下畔
He forgets those in high places and does not abandon those in low ones.[^24-13]

[^24-13]: Following the version in *Liezi*, sec. 6,
I supply a *bu* before the second verb.
There are many versions of this anecdote found in early philosophical texts,
and this sentence appears in different form in each.

愧不若黃帝
而哀不己若者
He is ashamed that he himself is not like the Yellow Emperor,
and pities those who are not like himself.

以德分人謂之聖
He who shares his virtue with others is called a sage;
以財分人謂之賢
he who shares his talents with others is called a worthy man.

以賢臨人未有得人者也
If he uses his worth in an attempt to oversee others, then he will never win their support;
以賢下人未有不得人者也
but if he uses it to humble himself before others, then he will never fail to win their support.

其於國有不聞也
With such a man, there are things within the state that he doesn't bother to hear about,
其於家有不見也
things within the family that he doesn't bother to look after.

勿已則隰朋可
If I must give an answer, I would say that Xi Peng will do."

吳王浮於江
登乎狙之山
The king of Wu, boating on the Yangtze,
stopped to climb a mountain noted for its monkeys.

眾狙見之
恂然棄而走
逃於深蓁
When the pack of monkeys saw him,
they dropped what they were doing in terror
and scampered off to hide in the deep brush.

有一狙焉
委蛇攫搔
But there was one monkey who,
lounging about nonchalantly, picking at things, scratching,
見巧乎王

王射之
敏給搏捷矢
When the king shot at him,
he snatched hold of the flying arrows with the greatest nimbleness and speed.

王命相者趨射
狙執死
The king thereupon ordered his attendants to hurry forward and join in the shooting,

王顧謂其友顏不疑曰之狙也
伐其巧
恃其便
以敖予
The king turned to his friend Yan Buyi and said, "This monkey,
flouting its skill,
trusting to its tricks,
deliberately displayed its contempt for me---
以至此殛也

戒之哉
Take warning from it!

嗟乎
Ah---
無以汝色驕人哉

以助其色
去樂辭顯
When Yan Buyi returned, he put himself under the instruction of Dong Wu,
learning to wipe the expression from his face,
to discard delight, to excuse himself from renown---
三年而國人稱之
and at the end of three years, everyone in the state was praising him.

南伯子綦隱几而坐
仰天而噓
Ziai of Nanpo[^24-14] sat leaning on his armrest,
staring up at the sky and breathing.

[^24-14]: Presumably the same as Ziqi of South Wall; see p. 7.

顏成子入見曰夫子物之尤也
Yan Chengzi entered and said, "Master, you surpass all other things!

形固可使若槁骸
心固可使若死灰乎
Can you really make the body like a withered tree
and the mind like dead ashes?"

***

曰吾嘗居山穴之中矣
"Once I lived in a mountain cave.

當是時也田禾一覩我
而齊國之眾三賀之
At that time, Tian He came to pay me one visit,
and the people of the state of Qi congratulated him three times.[^24-15]

[^24-15]: Tian He was a high minister of Qi who became its virtual ruler; see p. 69.
The people congratulated him because he recognized and paid honor to the sage recluse Ziqi.

我必先之彼故知之
I must have had hold of[^24-16] something in order for him to find out who I was;
我必賣之彼故鬻之
I must have been peddling something in order for him to come and buy.

[^24-16]: I follow Xi Tong in taking *xian* as an error for *you*.
Ziqi means that by becoming a recluse,
he was deliberately courting notoriety and hence was no better than any other seeker of fame.

若我而不有之
彼惡得而知之
If I had not had hold of something,
then how would he have been able to find out who I was?

若我而不賣之
彼惡得而鬻之
If I had not been peddling something,
then how would he have been able to buy?

嗟乎我悲人之自喪者
Ah, how I pitied those men who destroy themselves!

吾又悲夫悲人者
吾又悲夫悲人之悲者
Then again, I pitied those who pity others,
and again, I pitied those who pity those who pity others.

其後而日遠矣
But all that was long ago."

仲尼之楚
楚王觴之
When Confucius visited Chu,
the king of Chu ordered a toast.

孫叔敖執爵而立
市南宜僚受酒而祭曰古之人乎
Sunshu Ao came forward and stood with the wine goblet,
while Yiliao from south of the Market took some of the wine and poured a libation, saying, "[You have the wisdom of] the men of old, have you not?

於此言已
On this occasion, perhaps you would speak to us about it."

***

曰丘也聞不言之言矣
未之嘗言
Confucius said, "I have heard of the speech that is not spoken,
though I have never tried to speak about it.

於此乎言之
Shall I take this occasion to speak about it now?

市南宜僚弄丸
而兩家之難解
Yiliao from south of the Market juggled a set of balls,
and the trouble between the two houses was resolved.

孫叔敖甘寢秉羽
而郢人投兵
Sunshu Ao rested comfortably, waving his feather fan,
and the men of Ying put away their arms.

丘願有喙三尺
I wish I had a beak three feet long!"[^24-17]

[^24-17]: In 479 BCE, the year of Confucius's death,
a nobleman of one branch of the royal family of Chu led an uprising.
He tried to enlist the support of Xiong Yiliao from south of the Market (see sec. 20, p. 157),
first attempting to persuade him, then threatening him at the point of a sword,
but Yiliao steadfastly refused.
Partly as a result, the revolt quickly failed,
and peace was restored among the various branches of the royal family.
The juggling of the balls presumably symbolized unconcern in the face of danger.
Sunshu Ao, a high minister of Chu who lived a generation before Confucius,
governed so effectively that he was able to rest in ease,
and the people of the Chu capital, Ying, with no fear of foreign invasion,
could lay away their arms; see p. 174.
Both men appear here as examples of the superiority of silence over talk.
The "beak three feet long" apparently represents the "speech that is not spoken," that is,
the state of enlightenment; compare sec. 12, p. 89:
"You may join in the cheeping and chirping,
and when you have joined in the cheeping and chirping,
you may join with Heaven and earth."

***

彼之謂不道之道
此之謂不言之辯
These were men who followed what is called the Way that is not a way,
and this exchange of theirs is what is called the debate that is not spoken.

故德總乎道之所一
而言休乎知之所不知
至矣
Therefore, when virtue is resolved in the unity of the Way
and words come to rest at the place where understanding no longer understands,
we have perfection.

道之所一者
德不能同也
The unity of the Way
is something that virtue can never master;[^24-18]
知之所不能知者
辯不能舉也
what understanding does not understand
is something that debate can never encompass.

[^24-18]: Following texts that read *zhou* instead of *tong*.

名若儒墨而凶矣
To apply names in the manner of the Confucians and Mohists is to invite evil.

故海不辭東流
The sea does not refuse the rivers that come flowing eastward into it---
大之至也
it is the perfection of greatness.

聖人并包天地
澤及天下
而不知其誰氏
The sage embraces all heaven and earth,
and his bounty extends to the whole world,
yet no one knows who he is or what family he belongs to.

是故生無爵
死無諡
For this reason, in life he holds no titles,
in death he receives no posthumous names.

實不聚
名不立
Realities do not gather about him,
names do not stick to him---
此之謂大人
this is what is called the Great Man.

---

狗不以善吠為良
A dog is not considered superior merely because it is good at barking;
人不以善言為賢
a man is not considered worthy merely because he is good at speaking.

而況為大乎
Much less, then, is he to be considered great.

夫為大不足以為大
而況為德乎
That which has become great does not think it worth trying to become great,
much less to become virtuous.

夫大備矣莫若天地
然奚求焉而大備矣
Nothing possesses a larger measure of greatness than Heaven and earth,
yet when have they ever gone in search of greatness?

知大備者無求
無失
無棄
不以物易己也
He who understands what it means to possess greatness does not seek,
does not lose,
does not reject,
and does not change himself for the sake of things.

反己而不窮
He returns to himself and finds the inexhaustible;
循古而不摩
he follows antiquity and discovers the imperishable---
大人之誠
this is the sincerity of the Great Man.

子綦有八子
陳諸前
召九方歅曰為我相吾子
孰為祥
Ziqi had eight sons,
and lining them up in front of him,
he summoned Jiufang Yin and said, "Please physiognomize my sons for me
and tell me which one is destined for good fortune."

***

九方歅曰梱也
Jiufang Yin replied, "Kun---
為祥
he is the one who will be fortunate."

***

子綦瞿然喜曰奚若
Ziqi, both astonished and pleased, said, "How so?"

曰梱也將與國君同食以終其身
"Kun will eat the same food as the lord of a kingdom and will continue to do so to the end of his days."

***

子綦索然出涕曰吾子何為以至於是極也
Tears sprang from Ziqi's eyes, and in great dejection he said, "Why should my boy be brought to this extreme?"

***

九方歅曰夫與國君同食
澤及三族
而況父母乎
"He who eats the same food as the ruler of a kingdom
will bring bounty to all his three sets of relatives,
not to mention his own father and mother," said Jiufang Yin.

今夫子聞之而泣
"Yet now when you hear of this, sir, you burst out crying---
是禦福也
this will only drive the blessing away!

子則祥矣
父則不祥
The son is auspicious enough,
but the father is decidedly inauspicious!"

***

子綦曰歅
汝何足以識之
Ziqi said, "Yin,
what would you know about this sort of thing!

而梱祥邪
You say Kun will be fortunate---
盡於酒肉
入於鼻口矣
but you are speaking solely of the meat and wine
that are to affect his nose and mouth.

而何足以知其所自來
How could you understand where such things come from!

吾未嘗為牧而牂生於奧
未嘗好田而鶉生於宎
Suppose, although I have never been a shepherd, a flock of ewes were suddenly to appear in the southwest corner of my grounds or that,
although I have no taste for hunting, a covey of quail should suddenly appear in the southeast corner---
若勿怪何邪
if this were not to be considered peculiar, then what would be?

吾所與吾子遊者
遊於天地
When my son and I go wandering,
we wander through Heaven and earth.

吾與之邀樂於天
吾與之邀食於地
He and I seek our delight in Heaven
and our food from the earth.

吾不與之為事
不與之為謀
不與之為怪
He and I do not engage in any undertakings,
do not engage in any plots,
do not engage in any peculiarities.

吾與之乘天地之誠
而不以物與之相攖
He and I ride on the sincerity of Heaven and earth
and do not allow things to set us at odds with it.

吾與之一委蛇
而不與之為事所宜
He and I stroll and saunter in unity,
but never do we try to do what is appropriate to the occasion.

今也然有世俗之償焉
Now you tell me of this vulgar and worldly 'reward' that is to come to him.

凡有怪徵者
必有怪行
As a rule, where there is some peculiar manifestation,
there must invariably have been some peculiar deed to call it forth.

殆乎非我與吾子之罪
But surely this cannot be due to any fault of my son and me---
幾天與之也
it must be inflicted by Heaven.

吾是以泣也
It is for this reason that I weep!"

***

無幾何而使梱之於燕
盜得之於道
Not long afterward, Ziqi sent his son Kun on an errand to the state of Yan,
and along the way he was seized by bandits.

全而鬻之則難
不若刖之則易
They considered that he would be difficult to sell as a slave in his present state
but that if they cut off his feet, they could dispose of him easily.[^24-19]

[^24-19]: As we have seen earlier,
men whose feet were maimed were employed as gatekeepers because they couldn't run away.

於是乎刖而鬻之於齊
Accordingly, they cut off his feet and sold him in the state of Qi.

適當渠公之街
然身食肉而終
As it happened, he was made gatekeeper of the inner chamber in the palace of Duke Kang[^24-20]
and so was able to eat meat until the end of his days.

[^24-20]: Following Sun Yirang's emendations.

齧缺遇許由
Nie Que happened to meet Xu You.

曰子將奚之
"Where are you going?" he asked.

曰將逃堯
"I'm running away from Yao."

曰奚謂邪
"Why is that?"

***

曰夫堯畜畜然仁
"Because Yao is so earnestly and everlastingly benevolent!

吾恐其為天下笑
I'm afraid he'll make himself the laughingstock of the world.

後世其人與人相食與
In later ages, men may even end up eating one another because of him![^24-21]

[^24-21]: Compare sec. 23, p. 190.

夫民不難聚也
There is nothing difficult about attracting the people.

愛之則親
Love them and they will feel affection for you;
利之則至
benefit them and they will flock to you;
譽之則勸
praise them and they will do their best;
致其所惡則散
do something they dislike and they will scatter.

愛利出乎仁義
Love and benefit are the products of benevolence and righteousness.

捐仁義者寡
利仁義者眾
There are few men who will renounce benevolence and righteousness,
but many who will seek to benefit by them.

夫仁義之行
唯且無誠
且假乎禽貪者器
To practice benevolence and righteousness in such a fashion
is at best a form of insincerity,
at worst a deliberate lending of weapons to the evil[^24-22] and rapacious.

[^24-22]: Reading *xiong* in place of *qin*.

是以一人之斷制利天下
譬之猶一覕也
Moreover, to have one man laying down decisions and regulations for the 'benefit' of the world
is like trying to take in everything at a single glance.

夫堯知賢人之利天下也
而不知其賊天下也
Yao understands that the worthy man can benefit the world,
but he does not understand that he can also ruin the world.

夫唯外乎賢者知之矣
Only a man who has gotten outside the realm of 'worthiness' can understand that!"

---

有暖姝者
有濡需者
有卷婁者
There are the smug-and-satisfied,
there are the precariously perched,
and there are the bent-with-burdens.

所謂暖姝者
學一先生之言
則暖暖姝姝而私自說也
自以為足矣
而未知未始有物也
What I call the smug-and-satisfied are those who,
having learned the words of one master,
put on a smug and satisfied look, privately much pleased with themselves,
considering that what they've gotten is quite sufficient,
and not even realizing that they haven't begun to get anything at all.

是以謂暖姝者也
These are what I call the smug-and-satisfied.

***

濡需者豕蝨是也
What I call the precariously perched are like the lice on a pig.

擇疏鬣
自以為廣宮大囿
They pick out a place where the bristles are long and sparse
and call it their spacious mansion, their ample park;
奎蹄曲隈
乳閒股腳
自以為安室利處
or a place in some corner of the hams or hoofs,
between the nipples, or down around the haunches,
and call it their house of repose, their place of profit.

不知屠者之一旦鼓臂
布草
操煙火
而己與豕俱焦也
They do not know that one morning the butcher will give a swipe of his arm,
spread out the grass,
light up the fire,
and that they will be roasted to a crisp along with the pig.

此以域進
此以域退
Their advancement in the world is subject to such limitations as this,
and their retirement from it is subject to similar limitations.

此其所謂濡需者也
This is what I call the precariously perched.

***

卷婁者舜也
What I call the bent-with-burdens are those like Shun.

羊肉不慕蟻
The mutton doesn't long for the ants;
蟻慕羊肉
it is the ants that long for the mutton.

羊肉羶也
舜有羶行
百姓悅之
Mutton has a rank odor,
and Shun must have done rank deeds
for the hundred clans to have delighted in him so.

故三徙成都
至鄧之虛
而十有萬家
Therefore, though he changed his residence three times, each place he lived in turned into a city,
and by the time he reached the wilderness of Deng,
he had a hundred thousand households with him.

堯聞舜之賢
舉之童土之地
曰冀得其來之澤
Yao heard of the worthiness of Shun
and raised him up from the barren plains,
saying, "May I hope that you will come and bestow your bounty on us?"

舜舉乎童土之地
年齒長矣
聰明衰矣
而不得休歸
When Shun was raised up from the barren plains,
he was already well along in years,
and his hearing and eyesight were failing,
and yet he was not able to go home and rest.

所謂卷婁者也
This is what I call the bent-with-burdens.

***

是以神人惡眾至
眾至則不比
Therefore the Holy Man hates to see the crowd arriving,
and if it does arrive, he does not try to be friendly with it;
不比則不利也
not being friendly with it, he naturally does nothing to benefit it.

故無所甚親
無所甚疏
So he makes sure that there is nothing he is very close to
and nothing he is very distant from.

抱德煬和
以順天下
Embracing virtue, infused with harmony,
he follows along with the world---
此謂真人
this is what is called the True Man.

於蟻棄知
於魚得計
於羊棄意
He leaves wisdom to the ants,
takes his cue from the fishes,
leaves willfulness to the mutton.[^24-23]

[^24-23]: The ants and mutton (the text says "sheep," but presumably the word "meat" has dropped out)
appeared earlier; on the fishes who "forget one another in the rivers and lakes," see sec. 6, p. 50.

***

以目視目
以耳聽耳
以心復心
Use the eye to look at the eye,
the ear to listen to the ear,
and the mind to restore the mind.

若然者其平也繩
Do this, and your levelness will be as though measured with the line;
其變也循
your transformations will be a form of compliance.

古之真人以天待之
The True Man of ancient times used Heaven to deal with man;
不以人入天
he did not use man to work his way into Heaven.

古之真人得之也生
失之也死
得之也死
失之也生
The True Man of ancient times got it and lived,
lost it and died,
got it and died,
lost it and lived.

藥也其實堇也
Medicines serve as an example.[^24-24]

[^24-24]: As there are times when now one medicine, now another, will be appropriate,
so there are times when life is appropriate, times when death is.
The remainder of the chapter is rather disconnected in thought,
and it is often difficult to make out the author's intent.

桔梗也
雞壅也
豕零也
There are monkshood,
balloonflower,
cockscomb,
and chinaroot;
是時為帝者也
何可勝言
each has a time when it is the sovereign remedy,
though the individual cases are too numerous to describe.

***

句踐也以甲楯三千
棲於會稽
Goujian, with his three thousand men in armor and shield,
took up his position at Kuaiji;
唯種也能知亡之所以存
唯種也不知身之所以愁
at that time, Zhong alone was able to understand how a perishing state can be saved,
but he alone did not understand how the body may be brought to grief.[^24-25]

[^24-25]: Goujian, king of Yue, was defeated by the troops of Wu (see p. 5)
and forced to flee with a band of followers to the top of Mount Kuaiji.
There he plotted revenge with Zhong and another trusted minister.
But later, when he had successfully turned the tables and defeated Wu,
he grew suspicious of Zhong and forced him to commit suicide.

故曰鴟目有所適
鶴脛有所節
Therefore it is said, The owl's eyes have their special aptness,
the stork's legs have their proper proportions;
解之也悲
to try to cut away anything would make the creatures sad.

***

故曰風之過河也有損焉
It is said, When the wind passes over it, the river loses something;
日之過河也有損焉
when the sun passes over it, it loses something.

請只風與日相與守河
而河以為未始其攖也
But even if we asked the wind and sun to remain constantly over the river,
the river would not regard this as the beginning of any real trouble for itself---
恃源而往者也
it relies on the springs that feed it and goes on its way.

故水之守土也審
The water sticks close to the land;
影之守人也審
the shadow sticks close to the form;
物之守物也審
things stick close to things.

故目之於明也殆
Therefore keen sight may be a danger to the eye;
耳之於聰也殆
sharp hearing may be a danger to the ear;
心之於殉也殆
and the pursuit of thought may be a danger to the mind.

凡能其於府也殆
殆之成也不給改
禍之長也茲萃
All the faculties that are stored up in man are a potential source of danger,
and if this danger becomes real and is not averted,
misfortunes will go on piling up in increasing number.

其反也緣功
A return to the original condition takes effort;
其果也待久
its accomplishment takes time.

而人以為己寶
And yet men look on these faculties as their treasures---
不亦悲乎
is it not sad?

故有亡國戮民無已
不知問是也
Therefore we have this endless destruction of states and slaughter of the people---
because no one knows enough to ask about This![^24-26]

[^24-26]: The Way.

***

故足之於地也踐
雖踐
恃其所不蹍而後善博也
The foot treads a very small area of the ground,
but although the area is small,
the foot must rely on the support of the untrod ground all around before it can go forward in confidence.

人之於知也少
雖少
恃其所不知而後知天之所謂也
The understanding of man is paltry,
but although it is paltry,
it must rely on all those things that it does not understand before it can understand what is meant by Heaven.

知大一
知大陰
知大目
知大均
知大方
知大信
知大定
至矣
To understand the Great Unity,
to understand the Great Yin,
to understand the Great Eye,
to understand the Great Equality,
to understand the Great Method,
to understand the Great Trust,
to understand the Great Serenity---
this is perfection.

大一通之
大陰解之
大目視之
大均緣之
大方體之
大信稽之
大定持之
With the Great Unity you may penetrate it;[^24-27]
with the Great Yin, unknot it;
with the Great Eye, see it;
with the Great Equality, follow it;
with the Great Method, embody it;
with the Great Trust, reach it;
with the Great Serenity, hold it fast.

[^24-27]: The Way.

***

盡有天
循有照
冥有樞
始有彼
End with what is Heavenly,
follow what is bright,
hide in what is pivotal,
begin in what is objective---
則其解之也似不解之者
then your comprehension will seem like noncomprehension;
其知之也似不知之也
your understanding will seem like no understanding;
不知而後知之
not understanding it, you will later understand it.

其問之也不可以有崖
而不可以無崖
Your questions about it cannot have a limit,
and yet they cannot not have a limit.

頡滑有實
Vague and slippery, there is yet some reality there.

古今不代
Past and present, it does not alter---
而不可以虧
nothing can do it injury.

則可不謂有大揚搉乎
We may say that there is one great goal, may we not?

闔不亦問是已

Why act in such perplexity?

以不惑解惑復於不惑
是尚大不惑
If we use the unperplexed to dispel perplexity and return to unperplexity,
this will be the greatest unperplexity.
