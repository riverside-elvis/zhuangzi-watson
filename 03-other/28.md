## 28 讓王 Giving Away a Throne

堯以天下讓許由
許由不受
Yao wanted to cede the empire to Xu You,
but Xu You refused to accept it.[^28-1]

[^28-1]: On Yao, Xu You, and the ceding of the throne, see p. 3.
In this chapter, the writer illustrates the theme with tales of various historical or legendary figures.

又讓於子州支父
Then he tried to give it to Zichou Zhifu.

子州支父曰以為我天子
Zichou Zhifu said, "Make me the Son of Heaven?---
猶之可也
that would be all right, I suppose.

雖然我適有幽憂之病
方且治之
But I happen to have a deep-seated and worrisome illness
that I am just now trying to put in order.

未暇治天下也
So I have no time to put the empire in order."

夫天下至重也
而不以害其生
The empire is a thing of supreme importance,
yet he would not allow it to harm his life.

又況他物乎
How much less, then, any other thing!

唯無以天下為者
可以託天下也
Only he who has no use for the empire
is fit to be entrusted with it.

---

舜讓天下於子州支伯
子州支伯曰予適有幽憂之病
方且治之
Shun wanted to cede the empire to Zizhou Zhibo,
but Zizhou Zhibo said, "I happen to have a deep-seated and worrisome illness
that I am just now trying to put in order.

未暇治天下也
So I have no time to put the empire in order."

故天下大器也
而不以易生
The empire is a great vessel,
yet he would not exchange his life for it.

此有道者之所以異乎俗者也
This is how the possessor of the Way differs from the vulgar man.

---

舜以天下讓善卷
善卷曰余立於宇宙之中
Shun tried to cede the empire to Shan Quan,
but Shan Quan said, "I stand in the midst of space and time.

冬日衣皮毛
Winter days, I dress in skins and furs;
夏日衣葛絺
summer days, in vine cloth and hemp.

春耕種
In spring, I plow and plant---
形足以勞動
this gives my body the labor and exercise it needs;
秋收斂
in fall, I harvest and store away---
身足以休息
this gives my form the leisure and sustenance it needs.

日出而作
When the sun comes up, I work;
日入而息
when the sun goes down, I rest.

逍遙於天地之間
而心意自得
I wander free and easy between heaven and earth,
and my mind has found all that it could wish for.

吾何以天下為哉
What use would I have for the empire?

悲夫子之不知余也
What a pity that you don't understand me!"

遂不受
於是去而入深山
莫知其處
In the end,
he would not accept but went away, entering deep into the mountains,
and no one ever knew where he had gone.

---

舜以天下讓其友
石戶之農
Shun wanted to cede the empire to his friend,
the farmer of Stone Door.

石戶之農曰捲捲乎后之為人
The farmer of Stone Door said, "Such vigor and vitality you have, my lord!

葆力之士也
You are a gentleman of perseverance and strength!"

以舜之德為未至也
於是夫負妻戴
攜子以入於海
終身不反也
Then, surmising that Shun's virtue would hardly amount to very much,
he lifted his wife on his back,
took his son by the hand, and disappeared among the islands of the sea,
never to return to the end of his days.

---

大王亶父居邠
狄人攻之
When the Great King Danfu was living in Bin,
the Di tribes attacked his territory.[^28-2]

[^28-2]: Danfu, ancestor of the royal house of Zhou,
was the grandfather of King Wen, the founder of the Zhou dynasty.

事之以皮帛而不受
He offered them skins and silks, but they refused them;
事之以犬馬而不受
he offered them dogs and horses, but they refused them;
事之以珠玉而不受
he offered them pearls and jades, but they refused them.

狄人之所求者土地也
What the men of the Di tribes were after was his land.

大王亶父曰與人之兄居而殺其弟
The Great King Danfu said, "To live among the older brothers and send the younger brothers to their death;
與人之父居而殺其子
to live among the fathers and send the sons to their death---
吾不忍也
this I cannot bear!

子皆勉居矣
My people, be diligent and remain where you are.

為吾臣與為狄人臣
奚以異
What difference does it make
whether you are subjects of mine or of the men of Di?

且吾聞之
不以所用養害所養
I have heard it said,
one must not injure that which he is nourishing for the sake of that by which he nourishes it."[^28-3]

[^28-3]: That is, the lives of his people are far more precious to the ruler
than the possession of his territory.
This moral and the story of Danfu that illustrates it
are famous in early Chinese literature and are found in numerous texts of the period.

因杖筴而去之
民相連而從之
遂成國於岐山之下
Then, using his riding whip as a cane, he departed,
but his people, leading one another, followed him and,
in time, founded a new state at the foot of Mount Qi.

***

夫大王亶父可謂能尊生矣
The Great King Danfu may be said to have known how to respect life.

能尊生者
雖貴富
不以養傷身
He who knows how to respect life,
though he may be rich and honored,
will not allow the means of nourishing life to injure his person.

雖貧賤
不以利累形
Though he may be poor and humble,
he will not allow concerns of profit to entangle his body.

今世之人
居高官尊爵者
皆重失之
The men of the present age,
if they occupy high office and are honored with titles,
all think only of how serious a matter it would be to lose them.

見利輕亡其身
Eyes fixed on profit, they make light of the risk to their lives.

豈不惑哉
Are they not deluded indeed?

---

越人三世弒其君
The men of Yue three times in succession assassinated their ruler.

王子搜患之
逃乎丹穴
而越國無君
Prince Sou, fearful for his life,
fled to the Cinnabar Cave,
and the state of Yue was left without a ruler.

求王子搜不得
從之丹穴
王子搜不肯出
The men of Yue, searching for Prince Sou and failing to find him,
trailed him to the Cinnabar Cave,
but he refused to come forth.

越人薰之以艾
乘以王輿
They smoked him out with mugwort
and placed him in the royal carriage.

王子搜援綏登車
仰天而呼曰君乎
As Prince Sou took hold of the strap and pulled himself up into the carriage,
he turned his face to heaven and cried, "To be a ruler!

君乎
A ruler!

獨不可以舍我乎
Could I alone not have been spared this?"

王子搜非惡為君也
It was not that he hated to become their ruler;
惡為君之患也
he hated the perils that go with being a ruler.

若王子搜者
可謂不以國傷生矣
Prince Sou, we may say, was the kind
who would not allow the state to bring injury to his life.

此固越人之所欲得為君也
This, in fact, was precisely why the people of Yue wanted to obtain him for their ruler.

---

韓魏相與爭侵地
The states of Han and Wei were fighting over a piece of territory.

子華子見昭僖侯
Master Huazi went to see Marquis Zhaoxi, the ruler of Han.

昭僖侯有憂色
Marquis Zhaoxi had a worried look on his face.

子華子曰今使天下書銘於君之前
書之言曰左手攫之則右手廢
Master Huazi said, "Suppose the men of the empire were to draw up a written agreement and place it before you,
and the inscription read: 'Seize this with your left hand and you will lose your right hand;
右手攫之則左手廢
seize it with your right hand and you will lose your left;
然而攫之者必有天下
yet he who seizes this will invariably gain possession of the empire.'

君能攫之乎
Would you be willing to seize it?"

昭僖侯曰寡人不攫也
"I would not!" said Marquis Zhaoxi.

***

子華子曰甚善
"Very good!" exclaimed Master Huazi.

自是觀之
兩臂重於天下也
"From this I can see
that your two hands are more important to you than the empire.

身亦重於兩臂
韓之輕於天下亦遠矣
And of course, your body as a whole is a great deal *more* important than your two hands,
while the state of Han is a great deal *less* important than the empire as a whole.

今之所爭者
其輕於韓又遠
Moreover, this piece of territory that you are fighting over
is a great deal less important than the state of Han as a whole.

君固愁身傷生
以憂戚不得也
And yet you make yourself miserable and endanger your life,
worrying and fretting because you can't get possession of it!"

***

僖侯曰善哉
"Excellent!" said Marquis Zhaoxi.

教寡人者眾矣
未嘗得聞此言也
"Many men have given me advice,
but I have never been privileged to hear words such as these!"

子華子可謂知輕重矣
Master Huazi, we may say, understood the difference between important and unimportant things.

---

魯君聞顏闔得道之人也
使人以幣先焉
The ruler of Lu, having heard that Yan He was a man who had attained the Way,
sent a messenger with gifts to open relations with him.

顏闔守陋閭
苴布之衣而自飯牛
魯君之使者至
顏闔自對之
Yan He was in his humble, back-alley home,
wearing a robe of coarse hemp and feeding a cow,
when the messenger from the ruler of Lu arrived,
and he came to the door in person.

使者曰此顏闔之家與
"Is this the home of Yan He?" asked the messenger.

顏闔對曰此闔之家也
"Yes, this is He's house," said Yan He.

使者致幣
顏闔曰恐聽者謬
The messenger then presented his gifts,
but Yan He said, "I'm afraid you must have gotten your instructions mixed up.

而遺使者罪
不若審之
You'll surely be blamed if you give these to the wrong person,
so you'd better check once more."

使者還
反審之
復來求之
則不得已
The messenger returned,
checked his instructions,
and then went looking for Yan He a second time,
but he was never able to find him.

故若顏闔者真惡富貴也
Men like Yan He truly despise wealth and honor.

***

故曰道之真以治身
Hence it is said, The Truth of the Way lies in looking out for oneself;
其緒餘以為國家
its fringes and leftovers consist in managing the state and its great families;
其土苴以治天下
its offal and weeds consist in governing the empire.

由此觀之
帝王之功
聖人之餘事也
非所以完身養生也
The accomplishments of emperors and kings
are superfluous affairs
as far as the sage is concerned,
not the means by which to keep the body whole and to care for life.

今世俗之君子
多為身棄生以殉物
Yet how many gentlemen of the vulgar world today
endanger themselves and throw away their lives in the pursuit of mere things!

豈不悲哉
How can you help pitying them?

凡聖人之動作也
必察其所以之
與其所以為
Whenever the sage makes a move,
you may be certain that he has looked carefully
to see where he is going and what he is about.

今且有人於此
以隨侯之珠彈千仞之雀
Now suppose there was a man here who took the priceless pearl of the marquis of Sui
and used it as a pellet to shoot at a sparrow a thousand yards up in the air---
世必笑之
the world would certainly laugh at him.

是何也
Why?

則其所用者重
而所要者輕也
Because that which he is using is of such great value,
and that which he is trying to acquire is so trifling.

夫生者
And life---
豈特隨侯之重哉
surely it is of greater value than the pearl of the marquis of Sui!

---

子列子窮
容貌有飢色
Master Liezi was living in poverty,
and his face had a hungry look.

客有言之於鄭子陽者曰列御寇
蓋有道之士也
A visitor mentioned this to Ziyang, the prime minister of Zheng, saying, "Lie Yukou
appears to be a gentleman who has attained the Way.

居君之國而窮
Here he is living in Your Excellency's state, and in utter poverty!

君無乃為不好士乎
It would almost seem that Your Excellency has no fondness for such gentlemen, does it not?"

***

鄭子陽即令官遺之粟
Ziyang immediately ordered his officials to dispatch a gift of grain.

子列子見使者
再拜而辭
Master Liezi received the messenger,
bowed twice, and refused the gift.

使者去
子列子入
其妻望之而拊心曰妾聞為有道者之妻子
皆得佚樂
When the messenger had left
and Master Liezi had gone back into his house,
his wife, filled with bitterness, beat her breast and said, "I have heard that the wives and children of men who have attained the Way
all live in ease and happiness---
今有飢色
but here *we* are with our hungry looks!

君過而遺先生食
先生不受
His Excellency, realizing his error, has sent the Master something to eat,
but the Master refuses to accept it---
豈不命邪
I suppose this is what they call Fate!"

***

子列子笑謂之曰君非自知我也
Master Liezi laughed and said, "His Excellency does not know me personally---
以人之言而遺我粟
he sent me the grain simply because of what someone had told him.

至其罪我也
又且以人之言
And someday he could just as well condemn me to punishment,
again simply because of what someone told him.

此吾所以不受也
That's why I refused to accept."

***

其卒
民果作難
而殺子陽
In the end, as it happened,
rebellion broke out among the people of Zheng,
and Ziyang was murdered.

---

楚昭王失國
屠羊說走而從於昭王
When King Zhao of Chu was driven from his state,
the sheep butcher Yue fled at the same time and followed King Zhao into exile.[^28-4]

[^28-4]: King Zhao was forced by the invading armies of Wu to flee his state in 506 BCE;
he returned the following year.

昭王反國
將賞從者
及屠羊說
屠羊說曰大王失國
說失屠羊
When King Zhao regained control of the state,
he set about rewarding his followers,
but when it came the turn of the sheep butcher Yue,
Yue said, "His Majesty lost control of the state,
and I lost my job as sheep butcher.

大王反國
說亦反屠羊
Now His Majesty has regained the state,
and I have also gotten back my sheep-butchering job.

臣之爵祿已復矣
So my 'title and stipend' have already been restored to me.

又何賞之言
Why should there be any talk of a reward?"

王曰強之
"Force him to take it!" ordered the king.

***

屠羊說曰大王失國
非臣之罪
But the sheep butcher Yue said, "The fact that His Majesty lost the kingdom
was no fault of mine---
故不敢伏其誅
therefore I would not venture to accept any punishment for it.

大王反國
非臣之功
And the fact that His Majesty has regained the kingdom
is no accomplishment of mine---
故不敢當其賞
therefore I would not venture to accept any reward for it."

王曰見之
"Bring him into my presence!" ordered the king.

***

屠羊說曰楚國之法
必有重賞大功
而後得見
But the sheep butcher Yue said, "According to the laws of the state of Chu,
a man must have received weighty awards and accomplished great deeds
before he may be granted an audience with the ruler.

今臣之知不足以存國
而勇不足以死寇
Now I was not wise enough to save the state
or brave enough to die in combat with the invaders.

吳軍入郢
說畏難而避寇
When the armies of Wu entered the city of Ying,
I was afraid of the dangers ahead, so I ran away from the invaders.

非故隨大王也
I did not purposely follow after His Majesty.

今大王欲廢法毀約
Now His Majesty wishes to disregard the laws and break the precedents by granting me an audience.

而見說
此非臣之所以聞於天下也
But in view of the facts,
that would not win me any kind of reputation in the world!"

***

王謂司馬子綦曰屠羊說居處卑賤
而陳義甚高
The king said to Ziqi, his minister of war, "The sheep butcher Yue is a man of mean and humble position,
and yet his pronouncements on righteousness are lofty indeed!

子綦為我延之以三旌之位
I want you to promote him to one of the 'three banner' offices."[^28-5]

[^28-5]: Some versions of the text call them the "three scepter" offices;
they are defined by commentators as the three highest ministerial posts in the state of Chu.

***

屠羊說曰夫三旌之位
吾知其貴於屠羊之肆也
萬鍾之祿
吾知其富於屠羊之利也
When told of this, the sheep butcher Yue said, "I am fully aware that the 'three banner' rank
is a far more exalted place than a sheep butcher's stall
and that a stipend of ten thousand *zhong*
is more wealth than I will ever acquire slaughtering sheep.

然豈可以食爵祿
而使吾君有妄施之名乎
But how could I, merely out of greed for title and stipend,
allow my ruler to gain a reputation for irresponsibly handing out such favors?

說不敢當
I dare not accept.

願復反吾屠羊之肆
Please let me go back to my sheep butcher's stall."

遂不受也
And in the end, he refused to accept the position.

---

原憲居魯
環堵之室
Yuan Xian lived in the state of Lu,
in a tiny house that was hardly more than four walls.

茨以生草
蓬戶不完
桑以為樞
It was thatched with growing weeds,
had a broken door
made of woven brambles and branches of mulberry for the doorposts;
而甕
牖二室
褐以為塞
jars with the bottoms out,
hung with pieces of coarse cloth for protection from the weather,
served as windows for its two rooms.[^28-6]

[^28-6]: Yuan Xian, a disciple of Confucius, was famous for his indifference to poverty.
Zigong, who figures in this anecdote, was the most affluent of Confucius's disciples.

上漏下溼
匡坐而弦
The roof leaked, and the floor was damp,
but Yuan Xian sat up in dignified manner, played his lute, and sang.

子貢乘大馬
中紺而表素
軒車不容巷
往見原憲
Zigong, wearing an inner robe of royal blue and an outer one of white,
and riding in a grand carriage
whose top was too tall to get through the entrance to the lane,
came to call on Yuan Xian.

原憲華冠縰履
杖藜而應門
Yuan Xian, wearing a bark cap and slippers with no heels,
and carrying a goosefoot staff, came to the gate to greet him.

***

子貢曰嘻
"Goodness!" exclaimed Zigong.

先生何病
"What distress you are in, sir!"

***

原憲應之曰憲聞之無財謂之貧
Yuan Xian replied, "I have heard that if one lacks wealth, that is called poverty;
學而不能行謂之病
and if one studies but cannot put into practice what he has learned, that is called distress.

今憲貧也非病也
I am poor, but I am not in distress!"

***

子貢逡巡而有愧色
Zigong backed off a few paces with a look of embarrassment.

原憲笑曰夫希世而行
比周而友
學以為人
教以為己
仁義之慝
輿馬之飾
Yuan Xian laughed and said, "To act out of worldly ambition,
to band with others in cliquish friendships,
to study in order to show off to others,
to teach in order to please one's own pride,
to mask one's evil deeds behind benevolence and righteousness,
to deck oneself out with carriages and horses---
憲不忍為也
I could never bear to do such things!"

---

曾子居衛
縕袍無表
顏色腫噲
手足胼胝
Zengzi[^28-7] lived in the state of Wei,
wearing a robe of quilted hemp with the outside worn through,
his face blotchy and swollen,
his hands and feet hard and callused.

[^28-7]: Zeng Shen; see p. 227.

三日不舉火
十年不製衣
He would go three days without lighting a fire,
ten years without making himself a new suit of clothes.

正冠而纓絕
If he tried to straighten his cap, the chin strap would break;
捉衿而肘見
if he pulled together his lapels, his elbows poked through the sleeves;
納履而踵決
if he stepped into his shoes, his heels broke out at the back.

曳縰而歌商頌
聲滿天地
若出金石
Yet, shuffling along, he would sing the sacrificial hymns of Shang
in a voice that filled heaven and earth,
as though it issued from a bell or a chiming stone.

天子不得臣
The Son of Heaven could not get him for his minister;
諸侯不得友
the feudal lords could not get him for their friend.

故養志者忘形
Hence he who nourishes his will forgets about his bodily form;
養形者忘利
he who nourishes his bodily form forgets about questions of gain;
致道者忘心矣
and he who arrives at the Way forgets about his mind.

孔子謂顏回曰回來
Confucius said to Yan Hui, "Come here, Hui.

家貧居卑
Your family is poor and your position very lowly.

胡不仕乎
Why don't you become an official?"

***

顏回對曰不願仕
Yan Hui replied, "I have no desire to become an official.

回有郭外之田五十畝
足以給饘粥
郭內之田十畝
足以為絲麻
I have fifty *mou* of farmland outside the outer wall,[^28-8]
which is enough to provide me with porridge and gruel,
and I have ten *mou* of farmland inside the outer wall,
which is enough to keep me in silk and hemp.

[^28-8]: About enough land to feed four people; cf. _Mencius_ IA, 24.

鼓琴足以自娛
Playing my lute gives me enjoyment enough;
所學夫子之道者足以自樂也
studying the Way of the Master gives me happiness enough.

回不願仕
I have no desire to become an official."

***

孔子愀然變容曰善哉回
Confucius's face took on a sheepish expression, and he said, "Excellent, Hui---
之意
this determination of yours!

丘聞之知足者不以利自累也
I have heard that he who knows what is enough will not let himself be entangled by thoughts of gain;
審自得者失之而不懼
that he who really understands how to find satisfaction will not be afraid of other kinds of loss;
行修於內者無位而不怍
and that he who practices the cultivation of what is within him will not be ashamed because he holds no position in society.

丘誦之久矣
今於回而後見之
I have been preaching these ideas for a long time,
but now for the first time I see them realized in you, Hui.

是丘之得也
This is what *I* have gained."

---

中山公子牟謂瞻子曰身在江海之上
心居乎魏闕之下
Prince Mou of Wei, who was living in Zhongshan, said to Zhanzi, "My body is here beside these rivers and seas,
but my mind is still back there beside the palace towers of Wei.

奈何
What should I do about it?"[^28-9]

[^28-9]: Prince Mou of Wei appeared on p. 135.
Apparently he was trying, without much success, to live the life of a hermit.
Zhanzi, or Zhan He, is mentioned in early texts as a Daoist adept.

***

瞻子曰重生
"Attach more importance to life!" said Zhanzi.

重生則利輕
"He who regards life as important will think lightly of material gain."

***

中山公子牟曰雖知之
"I know that's what I should do," said Prince Mou.

未能自勝也
"But I can't overcome my inclinations."

***

瞻子曰不能自勝則從
"If you can't overcome your inclinations, then follow them!" said Zhanzi.

神無惡乎
"But won't that do harm to the spirit?"

***

不能自勝而強不從者
此之謂重傷
"If you can't overcome your inclinations and yet you try to force yourself not to follow them,
this is to do a double injury to yourself.

重傷之人
無壽類矣
Men who do such double injury to themselves
are never found in the ranks of the long-lived!"

***

魏牟萬乘之公子也
其隱巖穴也
難為於布衣之士
Wei Mou was a prince of a state of ten thousand chariots,
and it was more difficult for him to retire and live among the cliffs and caves
than for an ordinary person.

雖未至乎道
可謂有其意矣
Although he did not attain the Way,
we may say that he had the will to do so.

---

孔子窮於陳蔡之間
Confucius was in distress between Chen and Cai.

七日不火食
藜羹不糝
For seven days, he ate no properly cooked food
but only a soup of greens without any grain in it.

顏色甚憊
而弦歌於室
His face became drawn with fatigue,
but he sat in his room playing the lute and singing.

顏回擇菜
子路子貢相與言
Yan Hui was outside picking vegetables,
and Zilu and Zigong were talking with him.

曰夫子再逐於魯
"Our Master was twice driven out of Lu," they said.

削迹於衛
伐樹於宋
窮於商周
圍於陳蔡
"They wiped out his footprints in Wei,
chopped down a tree on him in Song,
made trouble for him in Shang and Zhou,
and are now besieging him here at Chen and Cai.

殺夫子者無罪
藉夫子者無禁
Anyone who kills him will be pardoned of all guilt,
and anyone who wishes to abuse him is free to do so.

弦歌鼓琴
未嘗絕音
Yet he keeps playing and singing,
strumming the lute without ever letting the sound die away.

君子之無恥也若此乎
Can a gentleman really be as shameless as all this?"

***

顏回無以應
入告孔子
Yan Hui, having no answer,
went in and reported what they had said to Confucius.

孔子推琴喟然而歎曰由與賜細人也
Confucius pushed aside his lute,
heaved a great sigh, and said, "Those two are picayune men!

召而來
Call them in here---
吾語之
I'll talk to them."

***

子路子貢入
子路曰如此者可謂窮矣
When Zilu and Zigong had entered the room,
Zilu said, "I guess you could say that all of us are really blocked in this time."[^28-10]

[^28-10]: The passage that follows involves a great deal of wordplay
on the various meanings of *qiong* (to be blocked, hence, to be in trouble, in distress, etc.)
and *da* (to get through, hence to master, to succeed).

***

孔子曰是何言也
Confucius said, "What kind of talk is that!

君子通於道之謂通
When the gentleman gets through to the Way, this is called 'getting through.'

窮於道之謂窮
When he is blocked off from the Way, this is called 'being blocked.'

今丘抱仁義之道
以遭亂世之患
Now I embrace the way of benevolence and righteousness and,
with it, encounter the perils of an age of disorder.

其何窮之為
Where is there any 'being blocked' about this?

故內省而不窮於道
So I examine what is within me and am never blocked off from the Way.

臨難而不失其德
I face the difficulties ahead and do not lose its Virtue.

天寒既至
霜露既降
吾是以知松柏之茂也
When the cold days come
and the frost and snow have fallen,
then I understand how the pine and the cypress flourish.[^28-11]

[^28-11]: A paraphrase of Confucius's remarks in _Analects_ IX, 27:
"Only when the year grows cold do we see that the pine and cypress are the last to fade."

陳蔡之隘於丘其幸乎
These perils here in Chen and Cai are a blessing to me!"

孔子削然反琴而弦歌
Confucius then turned complacently back to his lute and began to play and sing again.

子路扢然執干而舞
子貢曰吾不知天之高也
地之下也
Zilu excitedly snatched up a shield and began to dance,
while Zigong said, "I did not realize that Heaven is so far above,
earth so far below!"

***

古之得道者
窮亦樂
通亦樂
The men of ancient times who had attained the Way
were happy if they were blocked in,
and happy if they could get through.

所樂非窮通也
It was not the fact that they were blocked or not that made them happy.

道德於此
則窮通為寒暑風雨之序矣
As long as you have really gotten hold of the Way,[^28-12]
then being blocked or getting through are no more than the orderly alternation of cold and heat, of wind and rain.

[^28-12]: Reading *de* (get) in place of *de* (virtue);
compare the parallel text in _Lüshi chunchiu_, ch. 14, sec. 6.

故許由娛於潁陽
而共伯得乎共首
Therefore Xu You enjoyed himself on the sunny side of the Ying River,
and Gong Bo found what he wanted on top of a hill.[^28-13]

[^28-13]: Gong Bo was said to have occupied the throne for fourteen years (842-828 BCE)
but abdicated and retired to a place called Mount Gong.

---

舜以天下讓其友
北人無擇
Shun wanted to cede the empire to his friend,
a man from the north named Wuze.

北人無擇曰異哉
后之為人也
Wuze said, "What a peculiar man
this ruler of ours is!

居於甽畝之中
而遊堯之門
First he lived among the fields and ditches,
then he went wandering about the gate of Yao.

不若是而已
又欲以其辱行漫我
Not content to let it rest at that,
he now wants to take his disgraceful doings and dump them all over me.

吾羞見之
I would be ashamed even to see him!"

因自投清泠之淵
Thereupon he threw himself into the deeps at Chingling.

---

湯將伐桀
因卞隨而謀
When Tang was about to attack Jie,
he went to Bian Sui for help in plotting the strategy.[^28-14]

[^28-14]: Tang attacked and overthrew his sovereign, Jie,
the last ruler of the Xia dynasty, and founded the Shang or Yin dynasty.

卞隨曰非吾事也
"It's nothing I'd know anything about!" said Bian Sui.

湯曰孰可
"Who would be good?" asked Tang.

曰吾不知也
"I don't know."

***

湯又因瞀光而謀
Tang then went to Wu Guang and asked for help.

瞀光曰非吾事也
"It's nothing I'd know anything about!" said Wu Guang.

湯曰孰可
"Who would be good?" asked Tang.

曰吾不知也
"I don't know."

湯曰伊尹何如
"How about Yi Yin?" asked Tang.

***

曰強力忍垢
"A man of violence and force, willing to put up with disgrace---
吾不知其他也
I know nothing else about him."

***

湯遂與伊尹
謀伐桀
In the end Tang went to Yi Yin,
and together they plotted the attack.

剋之
以讓卞隨
卞隨辭曰后之伐桀也
謀乎我
Having overthrown Jie,
Tang then offered to cede the throne to Bian Sui,
Bian Sui refused, saying, "When you were plotting to attack Jie,
you came to me for advice---
必以我為賊也
so you must have thought I was capable of treason.

勝桀而讓我
Now you have defeated Jie and want to cede the throne to me---
必以我為貪也
so you must think I am avaricious.

吾生乎亂世
而無道之人
再來漫我以其辱行
I was born into this world of disorder,
and now a man with no understanding of the Way
twice comes and tries to slop his disgraceful doings all over me!

吾不忍數聞也
I can't bear to go on listening to such proposals again and again!"

乃自投稠水而死
Thereupon he threw himself into the Chou River and drowned.

***

湯又讓瞀光曰知者謀之
武者遂之
仁者居之
Tang tried to cede the throne to Wu Guang, saying, "The wise man does the plotting,
the military man the seizing,
and the benevolent man the occupying---
古之道也
such was the way of antiquity.

吾子胡不立乎
Now why will you not accept the position?"

***

瞀光辭曰廢上非義也
But Wu Guang refused, saying, "To depose your sovereign is no act of righteousness;
殺民非仁也
to slaughter the people is no act of benevolence;
人犯其難
我享其利非廉也
to inflict trouble on other men
and enjoy the benefits yourself is no act of integrity.

吾聞之曰非其義者不受其祿
I have heard it said, If the man is without righteousness, do not take his money;
無道之世不踐其土
if the world is without the Way, do not tread on its soil.

況尊我乎
And you expect me to accept such a position of honor?

吾不忍久見也
I can't bear the sight of you any longer!"

乃負石而自沈於廬水
Thereupon he loaded a stone onto his back and drowned himself in the Lu River.

---

昔周之興
有士二人處於孤竹
曰伯夷叔齊
Long ago, when the Zhou dynasty first came to power,
there were two gentlemen who lived in Guzhu
named Bo Yi and Shu Qi.

二人相謂曰吾聞西方有人
似有道者
They said to each other, "We hear that in the western region
there is a man who seems to possess the Way.

試往觀焉
Let us try going to look for him."

至於岐陽
武王聞之
使叔旦往見之
When they reached the sunny side of Mount Qi,
King Wu, hearing of them,
sent his younger brother Dan to meet them.[^28-15]

[^28-15]: Dan is better known by his title, the Duke of Zhou.
Other versions of the story make it clear that the "man who seems to possess the Way,"
whose reputation had attracted the brothers, was King Wu's father, King Wen,
who was already dead by this time.

與盟曰加富二等
就官一列
血牲而埋之
He offered to draw up a pact with them, saying, "You will be granted wealth of the second order
and offices of the first rank,
the pact to be sealed in blood and buried."[^28-16]

[^28-16]: According to ancient custom, an animal was sacrificed,
and the parties to the pact smeared the corners of their mouths with its blood;
then the text of the agreement was also smeared with blood and was buried beneath the sacrificial altar.

***

二人相視而笑曰嘻
The two men looked at each other and laughed, saying, "Hah---
異哉
how peculiar!

此非吾所謂道也
This is certainly not what *we* would call the Way!

昔者神農之有天下也
時祀盡敬
而不祈喜
In ancient times, when Shennong held possession of the empire,
he performed the seasonal sacrifices with the utmost reverence,
but he did not pray for blessings.

其於人也
忠信盡治
而無求焉
In his dealings with men,
he was loyal and trustworthy and observed perfect order,
but he did not seek anything from them.

樂與政為政
He delighted in ruling for the sake of ruling;
樂與治為治
he delighted in bringing order for the sake of order.

不以人之壞自成也
He did not use other men's failures to bring about his own success;
不以人之卑自高也
he did not use other men's degradation to lift himself up.

不以遭時自利也
Just because he happened along at a lucky time, he did not try to turn it to his own profit.

今周見殷之亂而遽為政
上謀而下行貨
阻兵而保威
割牲而盟以為信
揚行以說眾
殺伐以要利
Now the Zhou, observing that the Yin have fallen into disorder, suddenly makes a show of its rule,
honoring those who know how to scheme, handing out bribes,[^28-17]
relying on weapons to maintain its might,
offering sacrifices and drawing up pacts to impress men with its good faith,
lauding its achievements
in order to seize gain---
是推亂以易暴也
this is simply to push aside disorder and replace it with violence!

[^28-17]: Following Wang Niansun's suggestions, I omit the word *xia*.

***

吾聞古之士
遭治世
不避其任
"We have heard that the gentlemen of old,
if they happened upon a well-ordered age,
did not run away from public office;
遇亂世
不為苟存
but if they encountered an age of disorder,
they did not try to hold on to office at any cost.

今天下闇
周德衰
Now the world is in darkness,
and the virtue of the Zhou in decline.[^28-18]

[^28-18]: That is, King Wu, by resorting to arms and overthrowing the Yin dynasty,
has shown himself far inferior to his father, King Wen,
or his great grandfather, the Great King Danfu of the anecdote on p. 240.
But some commentators would emend this to read "the virtue of the Yin."

其並乎周以塗吾身也
不如避之以絜吾行
Rather than remain side by side with the Zhou and defile our bodies,
it would be better to run away and thus protect the purity of our conduct!"

二子北至於首陽之山
遂餓而死焉
The two gentlemen thereupon went north as far as Mount Shouyang,
where they eventually died of starvation.

***

若伯夷叔齊者
其於富貴也苟可得已
則必不賴
Men such as Bo Yi and Shu Qi
will have nothing to do with wealth and eminence
if they can possibly avoid it.

高節戾行
獨樂其志
不事於世
To be lofty in principle and meticulous in conduct,
delighting in one's will alone
without stooping to serve the world---
此二士之節也
such was the ideal of these two gentlemen.
