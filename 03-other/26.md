## 26 外物 External Things

外物不可必
External things cannot be counted on.

故龍逢誅
比干戮
箕子狂
惡來死
桀紂亡
Hence Longfeng was executed,
Bi Gan was sentenced to death,
Prince Ji feigned madness,
E Lai was killed,
and Jie and Zhou were overthrown.[^26-1]

[^26-1]: Guan Longfeng, minister to the tyrant Jie,
and Prince Bi Gan, minister to the tyrant Zhou, appeared on p. 23.
Prince Ji was a relative of Zhou who had to feign madness in order to escape execution.
E Lai assisted Zhou and was put to death when Zhou was overthrown.

人主莫不欲其臣之忠
There is no ruler who does not want his ministers to be loyal.

而忠未必信
But loyal ministers are not always trusted.

故伍員流於江
萇弘死於蜀
藏其血三年
化而為碧
Hence Wu Yun was thrown into the Yangzi,
and Chang Hong died in Shu,
where the people stored away his blood,
and after three years it was transformed into green jade.[^26-2]

[^26-2]: Wu Yun, or Wu Zixu, the loyal minister of Wu, appeared on p. 140.
He was forced by the king to commit suicide,
and his body was thrown into the Yangzi.
Chang Hong is mentioned in the _Zuozhuan_ as a minister of the Zhou court who was killed in 492 BCE.
But if this is the same man, the story of his exile and suicide in Shu
and the miraculous transformation of his blood must come from later legend.

人親莫不欲其子之孝
There is no parent who does not want his son to be filial.

而孝未必愛
But filial sons are not always loved.

故孝己憂而曾參悲
Hence Xiaoji grieved, and Zeng Shen sorrowed.[^26-3]

[^26-3]: Xiaoji was the eminently filial son of King Wuding of the Shang;
he was said to have been persecuted by an evil stepmother.
Zeng Shen, a disciple of Confucius and likewise a paragon of filial piety, was despised by his father.

***

木與木相摩則然
When wood rubs against wood, flames spring up.

金與火相守則流
When metal remains by the side of fire, it melts and flows away.

陰陽錯行
則天地大絯
When the yin and yang go awry,
then heaven and earth see astounding sights.

於是乎有雷有霆
水中有火
乃焚大槐
Then we hear the crash and roll of thunder,
and fire comes in the midst of rain
and burns up the great pagoda tree.

有甚憂兩陷而無所逃
Delight and sorrow are there to trap man on either side so that he has no escape.

螴蜳不得成
Fearful and trembling, he can reach no completion.

心若縣於天地之間
慰睯沈屯
His mind is as though trussed and suspended between heaven and earth,
bewildered and lost in delusion.

利害相摩
生火甚多
眾人焚和
Profit and loss rub against each other
and light the countless fires
that burn up the inner harmony of the mass of men.

月固不勝火
於是乎有僓然而道盡
The moon cannot put out the fire,
so that in time, all is consumed, and the Way comes to an end.[^26-4]

[^26-4]: This paragraph presents numerous difficulties of interpretation,
and the translation is tentative at many points.
In places the language appears to be that of ancient Chinese medicine,
with its theories of the influences of the yin and yang acting within the body.
Thus the moon may represent the watery force of the yin,
or perhaps the cold light of the mind.

---

莊周家貧
故往貸粟於監河侯
Zhuang Zhou's family was very poor,
and so he went to borrow some grain from the marquis of Jianhe.

監河侯曰諾
The marquis said, "Why, of course.

我將得邑金
將貸子三百金
I'll soon be getting the tribute money from my fief,
and when I do, I'll be glad to lend you three hundred pieces of gold.

可乎
Will that be all right?"

***

莊周忿然作色曰周昨來
有中道而呼者
Zhuang Zhou flushed with anger and said, "As I was coming here yesterday,
I heard someone calling me on the road.

周顧視車轍中
有鮒魚焉
I turned around and saw that there was a perch in the carriage rut.

周問之曰鮒魚來
I said to him, 'Come, perch---
子何為者邪
what are you doing here?'

對曰我東海之波臣也
He replied, 'I am a Wave Official of the Eastern Sea.

君豈有斗升之水而活我哉
Couldn't you give me a dipperful of water so I can stay alive?'

周曰諾
I said to him, 'Why, of course.

我且南遊吳越之王
I'm just about to start south to visit the kings of Wu and Yue.

激西江之水而迎子
I'll change the course of the West River and send it in your direction.

可乎
Will that be all right?'

鮒魚忿然作色曰吾失我常與
The perch flushed with anger and said, 'I've lost my element!

我無所處
I have nowhere to go!

吾得斗升之水然活耳
If you can get me a dipper of water, I'll be able to stay alive.

君乃言此
曾不如早索我於枯魚之肆
But if you give me an answer like that,
then you'd best look for me in the dried fish store!'"

---

任公子為大鉤巨緇
五十犗以為餌
蹲乎會稽
投竿東海
Prince Ren made an enormous fishhook with a huge line,
baited it with fifty bullocks,
settled himself on top of Mount Kuaiji,
and cast with his pole into the eastern sea.

旦旦而釣
期年不得魚
Morning after morning, he dropped the hook,
but for a whole year he got nothing.

已而大魚食之
牽巨鉤錎沒而下
At last a huge fish swallowed the bait
and dived down, dragging the enormous hook.

騖揚而奮鬐
白波若山
海水震蕩
It plunged to the bottom in a fierce charge, rose up and shook its dorsal fins
until the white waves were like mountains
and the sea waters lashed and churned.

聲侔鬼神
憚赫千里
The noise was like that of gods and demons,
and it spread terror for a thousand *li*.

任公子得若魚
離而腊之
自制河以東
蒼梧以北
莫不厭若魚者
When Prince Ren had landed his fish,
he cut it up and dried it,
and from Zhihe east,
from Cangwu north,
there was no one who did not get his fill.

已而後世輇才諷說之徒
皆驚而相告也
Since then, the men of later generations who have piddling talents and a penchant for odd stories
all astound one another by repeating the tale.

***

夫揭竿累
趣灌瀆
守鯢鮒
其於得大魚難矣
Now if you shoulder your pole and line,
march to the ditches and gullies,
and watch for minnows and perch,
then you'll have a hard time ever landing a big fish.

飾小說以干縣令
其於大達亦遠矣
If you parade your little theories and fish for the post of district magistrate,
you will be far from the Great Understanding.

是以未嘗聞任氏之風俗
其不可與經於世亦遠矣
So if a man has never heard of the style of Prince Ren,
he's a long way from being able to join with the men who run the world.

---

儒以詩禮發冢
The Confucians rob graves in accordance with the *Odes* and ritual.

大儒臚傳曰東方作矣
The big Confucian announces to his underlings: "The east grows light!

事之何若
How is the matter proceeding?"

***

小儒曰未解裙襦
口中有珠
The little Confucians say: "We haven't got the grave clothes off him yet,
but there's a pearl in his mouth![^26-5]

[^26-5]: The pearl or other precious stone customarily placed in the mouth of the corpse at burial.

詩固有之曰青青之麥
生於陵陂
Just as the *Ode* says: Green, green the grain
Growing on grave mound slopes;
生不布施
死何含珠為
If in life you gave no alms
In death how do you deserve a pearl?"

接其鬢
壓其顪
儒以金椎控其頤
徐別其頰
無傷口中珠
They push back his sidelocks,
press down his beard,
and then one of them pries into his chin with a little metal gimlet
and gently pulls apart the jaws
so as not to injure the pearl in his mouth.

---

老萊子之弟子出薪
遇仲尼
A disciple of Lao Laizi[^26-6] was out gathering firewood
when he happened to meet Confucius.

[^26-6]: A Daoist sage and reputed author of a work in sixteen sections that is no longer extant.
He is sometimes identified with Laozi.

反以告曰有人於彼
修上而趨下
末僂而後耳
視若營四海
He returned and reported, "There's a man over there
with a long body and short legs,
his back a little humped and his ears set way back,
who looks as though he were trying to attend to everything within the four seas.

不知其誰氏之子
I don't know who it can be."

***

老萊子曰是丘也
Lao Laizi said, "That's Kong Qiu.

召而來
Tell him to come over here!"

***

仲尼至曰丘
去汝躬矜與汝容知
斯為君子矣
When Confucius arrived, Lao Laizi said, "Qiu,
get rid of your proud bearing and that knowing look on your face,
and you can become a gentleman!"

***

仲尼揖而退
蹙然改容而問曰業可得進乎
Confucius bowed and stepped back a little,
a startled and changed expression on his face, and then asked, "Do you think I can make any progress in my labors?"

***

老萊子曰夫不忍一世之傷
而驁萬世之患
Lao Laizi said, "You can't bear to watch the sufferings of one age,
and so you go and make trouble for ten thousand ages to come![^26-7]

[^26-7]: Following texts that read *wu* in place of *ao*.

抑固窶邪
Are you just naturally a boor?

亡其略弗及邪
Or don't you have the sense to understand the situation?

惠以歡為驁
You take pride in practicing charity and making people happy[^26-8]---
終身之醜
the shame of it will follow you all your days!

[^26-8]: The meaning is very doubtful.

中民之行進焉耳
These are the actions, the 'progress' of mediocre men---
相引以名
相結以隱
與其譽堯而非桀
不如兩忘而閉其所譽
men who pull one another around with fame,
drag one another into secret schemes,
join together to praise Yao and condemn Jie,
when the best thing would be to forget them both and put a stop to praise!

反無非傷也
What is contrary cannot fail to be injured;
動無非邪也
what moves [when it shouldn't] cannot fail to be wrong.

聖人躊躇以興事
以每成功
The sage is hesitant and reluctant to begin an affair,
and so he always ends in success.

奈何哉
But what good are these actions of yours?

其載焉終矜爾
They end in nothing but a boast!"[^26-9]

[^26-9]: This last speech of Lao Laizi presents numerous difficulties,
and the translation is tentative.

---

宋元君夜半而夢人被髮
闚阿門曰予自宰路之淵
Lord Yuan of Song one night dreamed he saw a man with disheveled hair
who peered in at the side door of his chamber and said, "I come from the Zailu Deeps.

予為清江使河伯之所
漁者余且得予
I was on my way as envoy from the Clear Yangzi to the court of the Lord of the Yellow River
when a fisherman named Yu Ju caught me!"

***

元君覺
使人占之曰此神龜也
When Lord Yuan woke up,
he ordered his men to divine the meaning, and they replied, "This is a sacred turtle."

君曰漁者有余且乎
左右曰有
"Is there a fisherman named Yu Ju?" he asked,
and his attendants replied, "There is."

君曰令余且會朝
"Order Yu Ju to come to court!" he said.

***

明日余且朝
君曰漁何得
The next day Yu Ju appeared at court,
and the ruler said, "What kind of fish have you caught recently?"

***

對曰且之網得白龜焉
Yu Ju replied, "I caught a white turtle in my net.

其圓五尺
It's five feet around."

***

君曰獻若之龜
"Present your turtle!" ordered the ruler.

龜至
君再欲殺之
再欲活之
心疑
卜之曰殺龜以卜
When the turtle was brought,
the ruler could not decide whether to kill it
or let it live,
and being in doubt,
he consulted his diviners, who replied, "Kill the turtle and divine with it---
吉
it will bring good luck."

乃刳龜
七十二鑽
而無遺筴
Accordingly the turtle was stripped of its shell,
and of seventy-two holes drilled in it for prognostication,
not one failed to yield a true answer.[^26-10]

[^26-10]: Small indentations were drilled in the carapace,
and heat was applied: divination was based on the shape of the cracks that resulted.

***

仲尼曰神龜能見夢於元君
而不能避余且之網
Confucius said, "The sacred turtle could appear to Lord Yuan in a dream,
but it couldn't escape from Yu Ju's net.

知能七十二鑽而無遺筴
不能避刳腸之患
It knew enough to give correct answers to seventy-two queries,
but it couldn't escape the disaster of having its belly ripped open.

如是則知有所困
神有所不及也
So it is that knowledge has its limitations,
and the sacred has that which it can do nothing about.

雖有至知
萬人謀之
Even the most perfect wisdom
can be outwitted by ten thousand schemers.

魚不畏網而畏鵜鶘
Fish do not [know enough to] fear a net but only to fear pelicans.

去小知而大知明
Discard little wisdom, and great wisdom will become clear.

去善而自善矣
Discard goodness, and goodness will come of itself.

嬰兒生無石師而能言
The little child learns to speak, though it has no learned teachers---
與能言者處也
because it lives with those who know how to speak."

---

惠子謂莊子曰子言無用
Huizi said to Zhuangzi, "Your words are useless!"

***

莊子曰知無用而始可與言用矣
Zhuangzi said, "A man has to understand the useless before you can talk to him about the useful.

夫地非不廣且大也
人之所用容足耳
The earth is certainly vast and broad,
though a man uses no more of it than the area he puts his feet on.

然則廁足而墊之
致黃泉
人尚有用乎
If, however, you were to dig away all the earth from around his feet
until you reached the Yellow Springs,[^26-11]
then would the man still be able to make use of it?"

[^26-11]: See p. 136, n. 17.

***

惠子曰無用
"No, it would be useless," said Huizi.

***

莊子曰然則無用之為用也亦明矣
"It is obvious, then," said Zhuangzi, "that the useless has its use."

---

莊子曰人有能遊
且得不遊乎
Zhuangzi said, "If you have the capacity to wander,
how can you keep from wandering?

人而不能遊
且得遊乎
But if you do not have the capacity to wander,
how can you wander?

夫流遁之志
決絕之行
A will that takes refuge in conformity,
behavior that is aloof and eccentric---
噫其非至知厚德之任與
neither of these, alas, is compatible with perfect wisdom and solid virtue.

覆墜而不反
You stumble and fall but fail to turn back;
火馳而不顧
you race on like fire and do not look behind you.

雖相與為君臣
時也
But though you may be one time a ruler another time a subject,
this is merely a matter of the times.

易世而無以相賤
Such distinctions change with the age, and you cannot call either one or the other lowly.

故曰至人不留行焉
Therefore I say, the Perfect Man is never a stickler in his actions.

***

夫尊古而卑今
"To admire antiquity and despise the present---
學者之流也
this is the fashion of scholars.

且以豨韋氏之流觀今之世
夫孰能不波
And if one is to look at the present age after the fashion of Xiwei,
then who can be without prejudice?[^26-12]

[^26-12]: Xiwei, identified as a mythical ruler of high antiquity, appeared on p. 45,
as the sage who "held up heaven and earth."
The Confucians and Mohists are the most notorious extollers of antiquity,
but the same tendency is discernible at times in the Daoist school, for example,
in Laozi's description of the ideal simplicity and primitiveness of the society of very ancient times.
I suspect that "the fashion of Xiwei" is a reference to these advocates of ancient simplicity
in the Daoist school, though our understanding of the passage is greatly hampered
by the fact that we know almost nothing about the Xiwei legend.
As this passage makes clear, Zhuangzi's ideal "wandering"---that is,
living in accordance with the Way---does not permit either a forced conformity
with the world or a forced withdrawal from, and denial of, the world.

唯至人乃能遊於世而不僻
順人而不失己
Only the Perfect Man can wander in the world without taking sides,
can follow along with men without losing himself.

彼教不學
承意不彼
His teachings are not to be learned,
and one who understands his meaning has no need for him.[^26-13]

[^26-13]: The second part of the sentence is obscure in the original.

***

目徹為明
耳徹為聰
鼻徹為顫
口徹為甘
心徹為知
知徹為德
"The eye that is penetrating sees clearly,
the ear that is penetrating hears clearly,
the nose that is penetrating distinguishes odors,
the mouth that is penetrating distinguishes flavors,
the mind that is penetrating has understanding,
and the understanding that is penetrating has virtue.

凡道不欲壅
壅則哽
In all things, the Way does not want to be obstructed,
for if there is obstruction, there is choking;
哽而不止則跈
if the choking does not cease, there is disorder;
跈則眾害生
and disorder harms the life of all creatures.

***

物之有知者恃息
"All things that have consciousness depend on breath.

其不殷
非天之罪
But if they do not get their fill of breath,
it is not the fault of Heaven.

天之穿之
日夜無降
Heaven opens up the passages
and supplies them day and night without stop.

人則顧塞其竇
But man, on the contrary, blocks up the holes.

胞有重閬
The cavity of the body is a many-storied vault;
心有天遊
the mind has its Heavenly wanderings.

室無空虛
則婦姑勃谿
But if the chambers are not large and roomy,
then the wife and mother-in-law will fall to quarreling.

心無天遊
則六鑿相攘
If the mind does not have its Heavenly wanderings,
then the six apertures of sensation will defeat one another.

***

大林丘山之善於人也
亦神者不勝
"The great forests, the hills and mountains, excel man
in the fact that their growth is irrepressible.

德溢乎名
名溢乎暴
[In man,] virtue spills over into a concern for fame,
and a concern for fame spills over into a love of show.

謀稽乎誸
Schemes are laid in time of crisis;
知出乎爭
wisdom is born from contention;
柴生乎守
obstinacy comes from sticking to a position;
官事果乎眾宜
government affairs are arranged for the convenience of the mob.[^26-14]

[^26-14]: I take fame, show, schemes, wisdom,
and the arranging of government affairs for the convenience of the mob to be "unnatural"
and undesirable aims and activities that interfere with man's growth.

春雨日時
草木怒生
銚鎒於是乎始修
In spring, when the seasonable rains and sunshine come,
the grass and trees spring to life,
and the sickles and hoes are,
for the first time, prepared for use.

草木之到植者過半
而不知其然
At that time, more than half the grass and trees that had been pushed over begin to grow again,
though no one knows why.[^26-15]

[^26-15]: This whole paragraph, and especially the last sentence,
is very difficult to interpret, and there is no agreement among commentators as to the exact meaning.

***

靜然可以補病
眥搣可以休老
寧可以止遽
"Stillness and silence can benefit the ailing,
massage can give relief to the aged,
and rest and quiet can put a stop to agitation.

雖然若是
勞者之務也
But these are remedies that the troubled and weary man has recourse to.

非佚者之所未嘗過而問焉
The man who is at ease does not need them and has never bothered to ask about them.

聖人之所以駴天下
神人未嘗過而問焉
The Holy Man does not bother to ask
what methods the sage uses to reform the world.

賢人所以駴世
聖人未嘗過而問焉
The sage does not bother to ask
what methods the worthy man uses to reform the age.

君子所以駴國
賢人未嘗過而問焉
The worthy man does not bother to ask
what methods the gentleman uses to reform the state.

小人所以合時
君子未嘗過而問焉
The gentleman does not bother to ask
what methods the petty man uses to get along with the times.

***

演門有親死者
以善毀
爵為官師
"There was a man of Yan Gate who, on the death of his parents,
won praise by starving and disfiguring himself
and was rewarded with the post of Official Teacher.

其黨人毀
而死者半
The other people of the village likewise starved and disfigured themselves,
and more than half of them died.

堯與許由天下
許由逃之
Yao offered the empire to Xu You,
and Xu You fled from him.

湯與務光天下
務光怒之
Tang offered it to Wu Guang,
and Wu Guang railed at him.

紀他聞之
帥弟子而踆於窾水
諸侯弔之三年
When Ji Tuo heard of this,
he took his disciples and went off to sit by the Kuan River,
where the feudal lords went to console him for three years.

申徒狄因以踣河
Shentu Di, for the same reason, jumped into the Yellow River.[^26-16]

[^26-16]: Xu You, the recluse who refused Yao's throne, appeared on p. 3.
A similar story is told about King Tang and the recluse Wu Guang.
Ji Tuo and Shentu Di, along with Wu Guang, were mentioned on p. 43,
but we know nothing of their stories.
Apparently they withdrew or committed suicide out of sympathy for the insult
that had been done to Wu Guang in offering him a throne.

***

荃者所以在魚
"The fish trap exists because of the fish;
得魚而忘荃
once you've gotten the fish, you can forget the trap.

蹄者所以在兔
The rabbit snare exists because of the rabbit;
得兔而忘蹄
once you've gotten the rabbit, you can forget the snare.

言者所以在意
Words exist because of meaning;
得意而忘言
once you've gotten the meaning, you can forget the words.

吾安得忘言之人而與之言哉
Where can I find a man who has forgotten words so I can have a word with him?"
