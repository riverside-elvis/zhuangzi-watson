## 32 列御寇 Lie Yukou

列御寇之齊
中道而反
Lie Yukou was going to Qi,
but halfway there he turned around and came home.

遇伯昏瞀人
By chance he met Bohun Wuren.

伯昏瞀人曰奚方而反
"What made you turn around and come back?" asked Bohun Wuren.

曰吾驚焉
"I was scared."

曰惡乎驚
"Why were you scared?"

***

曰吾嘗食於十漿
而五漿先饋
"I stopped to eat at ten soup stalls along the way,
and at five of them they served me soup ahead of everybody else!"

伯昏瞀人曰若是則汝何為驚已
"What was so scary about that?" said Bohun Wuren.

***

曰夫內誠不解
形諜成光
以外鎮人心
使人輕乎貴老
"If you can't dispel the sincerity inside you,
it oozes[^32-1] out of the body and forms a radiance that,
once outside, overpowers men's minds
and makes them careless of how they treat their own superiors and old people.

[^32-1]: Following Sun Yirang's emendation.

而齏其所患
And it's from this kind of confusion that trouble comes.

夫漿特為食羹之貨
多餘之贏
The soup sellers have nothing but their broths to peddle,
and their margin of gain can't be very large.[^32-2]

[^32-2]: Supplying a negative from the parallel passage in _Liezi_, sec. 2.

其為利也薄
其為權也輕
而猶若是
而況於萬乘之主乎
If people with such skimpy profits
and so little power still
treat me like this,
then what would it be like with the ruler of Qi, the lord of a state of ten thousand chariots?

身勞於國而知盡於事
彼將任我以事而效我以功
Body wearied by the burden of such a state, wisdom exhausted in its administration,
he would want to shift his affairs onto me and make me work out some solution---
吾是以驚
that was what scared me!"

***

伯昏瞀人曰善哉觀乎
"You sized it up very well," said Bohun Wuren.

汝處已人將保汝矣
"But even if you stay at home, people are going to flock around you."

***

無幾何而往則戶外之屨滿矣
Not long afterward, Bohun Wuren went to Liezi's house
and found the area outside his door littered with shoes.[^32-3]

[^32-3]: Chinese at this time sat on mats on the floor;
consequently they removed their shoes before stepping up into a house.

伯昏瞀人北面而立
敦杖蹙之乎頤
He stood gazing north, staff held straight up,
chin wrinkled where it rested on it.

立有間不言而出
After standing there a while, he went away without a word.

賓者以告列子
The servant in charge of receiving guests went in and reported this to Liezi.

列子提屨
跣而走
暨乎門
Liezi snatched up his shoes
and ran barefoot after him,
overtaking him at the gate.

曰先生既來
曾不發藥乎
"Now that you've come all this way,
don't you have any 'medicine' to give me?"[^32-4]

[^32-4]: That is, good advice.

***

曰已矣
"It's no use.

吾固告汝曰人將保汝
果保汝矣
I told you from the beginning that people would come flocking around you,
and here they are flocking around you.

非汝能使人保汝
It's not that you're able to make them come to you---
而汝不能使人無保汝也
it's that you're unable to keep them from coming.

而焉用之
But what good is it to you?

感豫出異也
If you move other people and make them happy, you must be showing them something unusual in yourself.

必且有感搖而本才
又無謂也
And if you move others, you invariably upset your own basic nature,
in which case there's nothing more to be said.

與汝遊者
These men you wander around with---
又莫汝告也
none will give you any good advice.

彼所小言盡人毒也
All they have are petty words, the kind that poison a man.

莫覺莫悟
No one understands, no one comprehends---
何相孰也
so who can give any help to anyone else?

巧者勞而知者憂
The clever man wears himself out, the wise man worries.

無能者無所求
But the man of no ability has nothing he seeks.

飽食而敖遊
He eats his fill and wanders idly about.

汎若不繫之舟
虛而敖遊者也
Drifting like an unmoored boat,
emptily and idly he wanders along."

---

鄭人緩也
呻吟裘氏之地祗三年
而緩為儒
There was a man from Zheng named Huan who,
after three years of reciting and memorizing texts at a place called Qiushi,
finally became a Confucian scholar.

潤河九里
澤及三族
As the Yellow River spreads its moisture for nine *li* along its banks,
so Huan's affluence spread to his three sets of relatives.

使其弟墨
儒墨相與辯
其父助翟
He saw to it that his younger brother Di became a Mohist,
and the Confucian and the Mohist debated with each other,
but their father always took sides with the younger brother.

十年而緩自殺
Ten years of this, and Huan committed suicide.

其父夢之曰使而子為墨者予也
Appearing to his father in a dream, he said, "It was *I* who made it possible for your son to become a Mohist.

闔胡嘗視其良
Why don't you try taking a look at my grave---
既為秋柏之實矣
I have become the berries on the catalpa and the cypress there!"[^32-5]

[^32-5]: Is the fact that he has changed into berries
an indication of unappeased anger that will not let him rest in his grave,
or has it some other significance? I do not know.

***

夫造物者之報人也
不報其人而報其人之天
When the Creator rewards a man,
he does not reward what is man-made in the man but what is Heaven-made.

彼故使彼
It was what was in the younger brother that made him a Mohist.

夫人以己為有以異於人
以賤其親
Yet there are those like Huan who think they are different from others
and even despise their own kin.

齊人之井
飲者相捽也
Like men from Qi drinking at a well,
they try to elbow one another away.[^32-6]

[^32-6]: The story to which this refers is unknown.

故曰今之世皆緩也
So it is said, In the world today, we have nothing but Huans---
自是
they all think that they alone are right.

有德者以不知也
而況有道者乎
But the man who truly possesses Virtue is not even aware of it,
much less the man who possesses the Way.

古者謂之遁天之刑
In ancient times it was said of men like Huan that they had committed the crime of hiding from Heaven.

***

聖人安其所安
不安其所不安
The sage rests where there is rest
and does not try to rest where there is no rest.

眾人安其所不安
不安其所安
The common run of men try to rest where there is no rest
and do not rest where rest is to be found.

***

莊子曰知道易
Zhuangzi said, To know the Way is easy;
勿言難
to keep from speaking about it is hard.

知而不言
To know and not to speak---
所以之天也
this gets you to the Heavenly part.

知而言之
To know and to speak---
所以之人也
this gets you to the human part.

古之人天而不人
Men in the old days looked out for the Heavenly, not the human.

***

朱泙漫學屠龍於支離益
Zhuping Man studied the art of butchering dragons under Crippled Yi.

單千金之家
三年技成
而無所用其巧
It cost him all the thousand pieces of gold he had in his house,
and after three years he'd mastered the art,
but there was no one who could use his services.

***

聖人以必不必
The sage looks at the inevitable and decides that it is not inevitable---
故無兵
therefore he has no recourse to arms.

眾人以不必必之
The common man looks at what is not inevitable and decides that it is inevitable---
故多兵
therefore he has frequent recourse to arms.

順於兵故行有求
He who turns to arms is always seeking something.

兵恃之則亡
He who trusts to arms is lost.

***

小夫之知不離苞苴竿牘
The understanding of the little man never gets beyond gifts and wrappings, letters and calling cards.

敝精神乎蹇淺
而欲兼濟道物
太一形虛
He wastes his spirit on the shallow and trivial
and yet wants to be the savior of both the world and the Way,
to blend both form and emptiness in the Great Unity.

若是者迷惑於宇宙
Such a man will blunder and go astray in time and space;
形累不知太初
his body entangled, he will never come to know the Great Beginning.

彼至人者歸精神乎無始
而甘冥乎無何有之鄉
But he who is a Perfect Man lets his spirit return to the Beginningless,
to lie down in pleasant slumber in the Village of Not-Anything-at-All;
水流乎無形
發泄乎太清
like water he flows through the Formless
or trickles forth from the Great Purity.

悲哉乎
How pitiful---
汝為知在毫毛
而不知大寧
you whose understanding can be encompassed in a hair tip,
who know nothing of the Great Tranquillity!

---

宋人有曹商者
為宋王使秦
A man of Song, one Cao Shang,
was sent by the king of Song as envoy to the state of Qin.

其往也得車數乘
王說之益車百乘
On his departure, he was assigned no more than four or five carriages,
but the king of Qin, greatly taken with him, bestowed on him an additional hundred carriages.

反於宋見莊子曰夫處窮閭阨巷
困窘織屨
槁項黃馘者
When he returned to Song, he went to see Zhuangzi and said, "Living in poor alleyways and cramped lanes,
skimping, starving, weaving one's own sandals,
with withered neck and sallow face---
商之所短也
that sort of thing I'm no good at.

一悟萬乘之主
而從車百乘者
But winning instant recognition from the ruler of a state of ten thousand chariots
and returning with a hundred of them in one's retinue---
商之所長也
that's where I excel!"

***

莊子曰秦王有病召醫
Zhuangzi said, "When the king of Qin falls ill, he calls for his doctors.

破癰潰痤者得車一乘
舐痔者得車五乘
The doctor who lances a boil or drains an abscess receives one carriage in payment,
but the one who licks his piles for him gets five carriages.

所治愈下
得車愈多
The lower down the area to be treated,
the larger the number of carriages.

子豈治其痔邪
何得車之多也
From the large number of carriages you've got,
I take it you must have been treating his piles.

子行矣
Get out!"

---

魯哀公問於顏闔曰吾以仲尼為貞幹
國其有瘳乎
Duke Ai of Lu said to Yan He, "If I were to make Confucius my pillar and stanchion,
do you think it would improve the health of the state?"

***

曰殆哉
"Beware---
圾乎
that way lies danger!

仲尼方且飾羽而畫
從事華辭
以支為旨
Confucius will deck things out in feathers and paint
and conduct his affairs with flowery phrases,
mistaking side issues for the crux.

忍性以視民
而不知不信
He is willing to distort his inborn nature in order to make himself a model for the people,
not even realizing that he is acting in bad faith.

受乎心
宰乎神
He takes everything to heart,
submits all to the judgment of the spirit---
夫何足以上民
how could such a man be worth putting in charge of the people?

彼宜女與
Does he meet with your approval?

予頤與
Would you like to provide for his support?

誤而可矣
It would be a mistake, but you may do it if you like.

今使民離實學偽
非所以視民也
Yet one who would induce the people to turn their backs on reality and study hypocrisy
is hardly fit to be made a model for the people.

為後世慮
不若休之
If we are to take thought for later ages,
it would be best to drop the scheme.

***

難治也
"Governing is a difficult thing.

施於人而不忘
To dispense favors to men without ever forgetting that you are doing so---
非天布也
this is not Heaven's way of giving.

商賈不齒
Even merchants and peddlers are unwilling to be ranked with such a person;
雖以事齒之
神者勿齒
and although their occupations may seem to rank them with him,
in their hearts they will never acquiesce to such a ranking.[^32-7]

[^32-7]: Meaning very doubtful.

為外刑者金與木也
External punishments are administered by implements of metal and wood;
為內刑者動與過也
internal punishments are inflicted by frenzy and excess.

宵人之離外刑者金木訊之
When the petty man meets with external punishments, the implements of metal and wood bear down on him;
離內刑者陰陽食之
when he incurs internal punishment, the yin and yang eat him up.[^32-8]

[^32-8]: An upset in the balance of the yin and yang within the body
will bring on a consuming sickness; see p. 26, n. 9.

夫免乎外內之刑者
To escape both external and internal punishment---
唯真人能之
only the True Man is capable of this."

---

孔子曰凡人心險於山川
難於知天
Confucius said, "The mind of man is more perilous than mountains or rivers,
harder to understand than Heaven.

天猶有春秋冬夏旦暮之期
Heaven at least has its fixed times of spring and fall, winter and summer, daybreak and dusk.

人者厚貌深情
But man is thick-skinned and hides his true form deep within.

故有貌愿而益
Thus he may have an earnest face and yet be supercilious;
有長若不肖
he may seem to have superior qualities and yet be worthless.

有順懁而達
He may appear to be going about things in a scatterbrained way and yet know exactly what he is doing.

有堅而縵
Seeming to be firm, he may in fact be lax;
有緩而釬
seeming to be mild, he may in fact be ruthless.

故其就義若渴者
其去義若熱
Therefore those who flock to righteousness like thirsty men to water
may later flee from it as though from fire.

***

故君子遠使之而觀其忠
近使之而觀其敬
"For this reason the gentleman will employ a man on a distant mission and observe his degree of loyalty,
will employ him close at hand and observe his degree of respect.

煩使之而觀其能
卒然問焉而觀其知
He will hand him troublesome affairs and observe how well he manages them,
will suddenly ask his advice and observe how wisely he answers.

急與之期而觀其信
委之以財而觀其仁
告之以危而觀其節
He will exact some difficult promise from him and see how well he keeps it,
turn over funds to him and see with what benevolence he dispenses them,
inform him of the danger he is in and note how faithful he is to his duties.

醉之以酒而觀其側
雜之以處而觀其色
He will get him drunk with wine and observe how well he handles himself,
place him in mixed company and see what effect beauty has on him.

九徵至不肖人得矣
By applying these nine tests, you may determine who is the unworthy man."

---

正考父
Zheng Kaofu---
一命而傴
when he received his first appointment to office, he bowed his head;
再命而僂
when he received his second appointment, he bent his back;
三命而俯
when he received his third appointment, he hunched far over;
循牆而走
hugging the wall, he scurried along.[^32-9]

[^32-9]: Zheng Kaofu, "The Upright Ancestor,"
was a forebear of Confucius who served at the court of Song in the eighth century BCE.
The three appointments represent three advancements in court rank.
According to _Zhuozhuan_, _Zhao_ seventh year, the passage describing him here
was part of the inscription on a bronze vessel used in his mortuary temple.

孰敢不軌
Who would dare to ignore his example?

如而夫者
But the ordinary man---
一命而呂鉅
on receiving his first appointment, he begins to strut;
再命而於車上舞
on receiving his second appointment, he does a dance in his carriage;
三命而名諸父
on receiving his third appointment, he addresses his father's brothers by their personal names.

孰協唐許
What a difference from the ways of Yao and Xu You!

***

賊莫大乎德有心而心有眼
There is no greater evil than for the mind to be aware of virtue and to act as though it were a pair of eyes.

及其有眼也而內視
內視而敗矣
For when it starts acting like a pair of eyes, it will peer out from within,
and when it peers out from within, it is ruined.

凶德有五
中德為首
There are five types of dangerous virtue,
of which inner virtue is the worst.[^32-10]

[^32-10]: The writer nowhere states what the other four types are.

何謂中德
What do I mean by inner virtue?

中德也者有以自好也
而吡其所不為者也
He who possesses inner virtue thinks himself always in the right
and denigrates those who do not do as he does.

窮有八極
達有三必
形有六府
There are eight extremes that bring a man trouble,
three conditions necessary for advancement,
and six respositories of punishment.[^32-11]

[^32-11]: Following Xi Tong, I read *xing* to mean "punishment."

美髯長大壯麗勇敢
Beauty, a fine beard, a tall stature, brawn, strength, style, bravery, decisiveness---
八者俱過人也
因以是窮
when a man has all these to a degree that surpasses others,
they bring him trouble.

緣循
偃佒
困畏
Tagging along with things,
bobbing and weaving,
cringing and fawning---
不若人三者俱通達
if a man can do all three of these in a way that others do not, then he will succeed in advancing.

知慧外通
Wisdom and knowledge, and the outward recognition they involve;
勇動多怨
bravery and decisiveness, and the numerous resentments they arouse;
仁義多責
benevolence and righteousness, and all the responsibilities they involve---
these six are what bring you punishment.[^32-12]

[^32-12]: The end of this sentence has dropped out of most texts.

達生之情者傀
He who has mastered the true form of life is a giant;
達於知者肖
he who has mastered understanding is petty.

達大命者隨
He who has mastered the Great Fate follows along;
達小命者遭
he who has mastered the little fates must take what happens to come his way.[^32-13]

[^32-13]: There would seem to be a play on the various meanings of *ming*---
"appointment," "fate," "command," etc.; see p. 28, n. 12.
Some commentators take it to mean "life span."

---

人有見宋王者
錫車十乘
There was a man who had an audience with the king of Song
and received from him a gift of ten carriages.

以其十乘驕稚莊子
With his ten carriages, he went bragging and strutting to Zhuangzi.

莊子曰河上有家貧恃緯蕭而食者
Zhuangzi said, "There's a poor family down by the river who make their living by weaving articles out of mugwort.

其子沒於淵
得千金之珠
The son was diving in the deepest part of the river
and came upon a pearl worth a thousand pieces of gold.

其父謂其子曰取石來鍛之
His father said to him, 'Bring a rock and smash it to bits!

夫千金之珠
必在九重之淵而驪龍頷下
A pearl worth a thousand in gold
could only have come from under the chin of the Black Dragon who lives at the bottom of the ninefold deeps.

子能得珠者
必遭其睡也
To be able to get the pearl,
you must have happened along when he was asleep.

使驪龍而寤
子尚奚微之有哉
If the Black Dragon had been awake,
do you think there'd have been so much as a shred of you left?'

今宋國之深非直九重之淵也
宋王之猛非直驪龍也
Now the state of Song is deeper than the ninefold deeps,
and the king of Song more truculent than the Black Dragon.

子能得車者
必遭其睡也
In order to get these carriages,
you must have happened along when he was asleep.

使宋王而寤
子為齏粉夫
If the king of Song had been awake,
you'd have ended up in little pieces!"

---

或聘於莊子
Someone sent gifts to Zhuangzi with an invitation to office.

莊子應其使曰
Zhuangzi replied to the messenger in these words:

子見夫犧牛乎
"Have you ever seen a sacrificial ox?

衣以文繡
食以芻叔
They deck him out in embroidery and trimmings,
gorge him on grass and beanstalks.

及其牽而入於太廟
雖欲為孤犢
其可得乎
But when at last they lead him off into the great ancestral temple,
then, although he might wish he could become a lonely calf once more,
is it possible?"

---

莊子將死
弟子欲厚葬之
When Zhuangzi was about to die,
his disciples expressed a desire to give him a sumptuous burial.

莊子曰吾以天地為棺槨
以日月為連璧
星辰為珠璣
萬物為齎送
Zhuangzi said, "I will have heaven and earth for my coffin and coffin shell,
the sun and moon for my pair of jade disks,
the stars and constellations for my pearls and beads,
and the ten thousand things for my parting gifts.

吾葬具豈不備邪
The furnishings for my funeral are already prepared---
何以加此
what is there to add?"

***

弟子曰吾恐烏鳶之食夫子也
"But we're afraid the crows and kites will eat you, Master!" said his disciples.

***

莊子曰在上為烏鳶食
在下為螻蟻食
Zhuangzi said, "Above ground, I'll be eaten by crows and kites;
below ground, I'll be eaten by mole crickets and ants.

奪彼與此何其偏也
Wouldn't it be rather bigoted to deprive one group in order to supply the other?

***

以不平平其平也不平
"If you use unfairness to achieve fairness, your fairness will be unfair.

以不徵徵其徵也不徵
If you use a lack of proof to establish proofs, your proofs will be proofless.

明者唯為之使
神者徵之
The bright-eyed man is no more than the servant of things,
but the man of spirit knows how to find real proofs.

夫明之不勝神也
The bright-eyed is no match for the man of spirit---
久矣
from long ago this has been the case.

而愚者恃其所見入於人
Yet the fool trusts to what he can see and immerses himself in the human.

其功外也
All his accomplishments are beside the point---
不亦悲乎
pitiful, isn't it!"
