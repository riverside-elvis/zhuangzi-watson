## 13 天道 The Way of Heaven

天道運而無所積
It is the Way of heaven to keep moving and to allow no piling up---
故萬物成
hence the ten thousand things come to completion.

帝道運而無所積
It is the Way of the emperor to keep moving and to allow no piling up---
故天下歸
hence the whole world repairs to his court.

聖道運而無所積
It is the Way of the sage to keep moving and to allow no piling up---
故海內服
hence all within the seas bow to him.

明於天
通於聖
六通四辟於帝王之德者
Comprehending Heaven,
conversant with the sage,
walker in the six avenues and four frontiers of the Virtue of emperors and kings---
其自為也
the actions of such a man come naturally;
昧然無不靜者矣
dreamily, he never lacks stillness.

***

聖人之靜也非曰靜也善故靜也
The sage is still not because he takes stillness to be good and therefore is still.

萬物無足以鐃心者
The ten thousand things are insufficient to distract his mind---
故靜也
that is the reason he is still.

水靜則明燭鬚眉
Water that is still gives back a clear image of beard and eyebrows;
平中準
大匠取法焉
reposing in the water level,
it offers a measure to the great carpenter.

水靜猶明
而況精神
And if water in stillness possesses such clarity,
how much more must pure spirit.

聖人之心靜乎天地之鑑也
萬物之鏡也
The sage's mind in stillness is the mirror of Heaven and earth,
the glass of the ten thousand things.

***

夫虛靜
恬淡
寂漠
無為者
Emptiness, stillness,
limpidity,
silence,
inaction---
天地之平
而道德之至
these are the level of Heaven and earth,
the substance of the Way and its Virtue.

故帝王聖人休焉
Therefore the emperor, the king, the sage rest in them.

休則虛
Resting, they may be empty;
虛則實
empty, they may be full;
實者倫矣
and fullness is completion.[^13-1]

[^13-1]: Following texts that read *bei* in place of *lun*.

虛則靜
Empty, they may be still;
靜則動
still, they may move;
動則得矣
moving, they may acquire.

靜則無為
Still, they may rest in inaction;
無為也
則任事者責矣
resting in inaction,
they may demand success from those who are charged with activities.

無為則俞俞
Resting in inaction, they may be merry;
俞俞者憂患不能處
年壽長矣
being merry, they may shun the place of care and anxiety,
and the years of their life will be long.

***

夫虛靜
恬淡
寂寞
無為者萬物之本也
Emptiness,
stillness,
limpidity,
silence,
inaction are the root of the ten thousand things.

明此以南鄉堯之為君也
To understand them and face south is to become a ruler such as Yao was;
明此以北面舜之為臣也
to understand them and face north is to become a minister such as Shun was.[^13-2]

[^13-2]: Shun served as a minister under Yao before Yao ceded the throne to him;
hence here he represents the ideal minister.

以此處上帝王天子之德也
To hold them in high station is the Virtue of emperors and kings, of the Son of Heaven;
以此處下玄聖素王之道也
to hold them in lowly station is the way of the dark sage, the uncrowned king.

以此退居而閒游
江海山林之士服
Retire with them to a life of idle wandering,
and you will command first place among the recluses of the rivers and seas, the hills and forests.

以此進為而撫世
則功大名顯而天下一也
Come forward with them to succor the age,
and your success will be great, your name renowned, and the world will be united.

靜而聖
動而王
In stillness you will be a sage,
in action a king.

無為也而尊
Resting in inaction, you will be honored;
樸素而天下莫能與之爭美
of unwrought simplicity, your beauty will be such that no one in the world may vie with you.

***

夫明白於天地之德者
此之謂大本大宗
He who has a clear understanding of the Virtue of Heaven and earth
may be called the Great Source, the Great Ancestor.

與天和者也
He harmonizes with Heaven;
所以均調天下與人和者也
and by doing so he brings equitable accord to the world and harmonizes with men as well.

與人和者謂之人樂
To harmonize with men is called human joy;
與天和者謂之天樂
to harmonize with Heaven is called Heavenly joy.

莊子曰吾師乎
吾師乎
Zhuangzi has said, "This Teacher of mine,
this Teacher of mine---
虀萬物
而不為戾
he passes judgment on the ten thousand things,
but he doesn't think himself severe;
澤及萬世
而不為仁
his bounty extends to ten thousand generations,
but he doesn't think himself benevolent.

長於上古
而不為壽
He is older than the highest antiquity,
but he doesn't think himself long-lived;
覆載天地
刻雕眾形
而不為朽
he covers heaven, bears up the earth,
carves and fashions countless forms,
but he doesn't think himself skilled."[^13-3]

[^13-3]: See p. 52, where these words are attributed to Xu You.

此之謂天樂
This is what is called Heavenly joy.

***

故曰知天樂者
其生也天行
So it is said, for him who understands Heavenly joy,
life is the working of Heaven;
其死也物化
death is the transformation of things.

靜而與陰同德
In stillness, he and the yin share a single Virtue;
動而與陽同波
in motion, he and the yang share a single flow.

故知天樂者無天怨
無人非
無物累
無鬼責
Thus he who understands Heavenly joy incurs no wrath from Heaven,
no opposition from man,
no entanglement from things,
no blame from the spirits.

故曰其動也天
其靜也地
So it is said, his movement is of Heaven,
his stillness of earth.

一心定而王天下
With his single mind in repose, he is king of the world;
其鬼不祟
the spirits do not afflict him;
其魂不疲
his soul knows no weariness.

一心定而萬物服
His single mind reposed, the ten thousand things submit---
言以虛靜推於天地
通於萬物
which is to say that his emptiness and stillness reach throughout Heaven and earth
and penetrate the ten thousand things.

此之謂天樂
This is what is called Heavenly joy.

天樂者聖人之心
以蓄天下也
Heavenly joy is the mind of the sage
by which he shepherds the world.

***

夫帝王之德
以天地為宗
以道德為主
以無為為常
The Virtue of emperors and kings
takes Heaven and earth as its ancestor,
the Way and its Virtue as its master,
inaction as its constant rule.

無為也則用天下而有餘
With inaction, you may make the world work for you and have leisure to spare;
有為也則為天下用而不足
with action, you will find yourself working for the world and never will it be enough.

故古之人貴夫無為也
Therefore the men of old prized inaction.

***

上無為也下亦無為也
是下與上同德
If superiors adopt inaction and inferiors adopt inaction as well,
then inferior and superior will share the same virtue;
下與上同德
則不臣
and if inferior and superior share the same virtue,
there will be none to act as minister.

下有為也上亦有為也
是上與下同道
If inferiors adopt action and superiors adopt action as well,
then superior and inferior will share the same way;
上與下同道
則不主
and if superior and inferior share the same way,
there will be none to act as lord.

上必無為而用天下
Superiors must adopt inaction and make the world work for them;
下必有為為天下用
inferiors must adopt action and work for the world.

此不易之道也
This is an unvarying truth.

***

故古之王天下者
知雖落天地
不自慮也
Therefore the kings of the world in ancient times,
though their knowledge encompassed all Heaven and earth,
did not of themselves lay plans;
辯雖彫萬物
不自說也
though their power of discrimination embraced[^13-4] the ten thousand things,
they did not of themselves expound any theories;
能雖窮海內
不自為也
though their abilities outshone all within the four seas,
they did not of themselves act.

[^13-4]: Reading *zhou* instead of *diao* in accordance with Zhang Binglin's interpretation.

天不產而萬物化
Heaven does not give birth, yet the ten thousand things are transformed;
地不長而萬物育
earth does not sustain, yet the ten thousand things are nourished.

帝王無為
而天下功
The emperor and the king do not act,
yet the world is benefited.

故曰莫神於天
莫富於地
莫大於帝王
So it is said, nothing so spiritual as Heaven,
nothing so rich as earth,
nothing so great as the emperor and the king.

故曰帝王之德配天地
So it is said, the Virtue of the emperor and the king is the counterpart of Heaven and earth.

此乘天地
馳萬物
而用人群之道也
This is the way to mount Heaven and earth,
to make the ten thousand things gallop,
to employ the mass of men.

***

本在於上末在於下
The source rests with the superior, the trivia with the inferior;
要在於主詳在於臣
the essential resides in the ruler, the details in his ministers.

三軍五兵之運
The blandishments of the three armies and the five weapons---
德之末也
these are the trivia of Virtue.

賞罰利害
五刑之辟
The doling out of rewards and punishments, benefit and loss,
the five penalties---
教之末也
these are the trivia of public instruction.[^13-5]

[^13-5]: The "three armies" refers to the three-divisioned army of a feudal state.
The five weapons are usually listed as spear, halberd, battle-ax, shield, and bow,
though there are other lists.
The five penalties are usually given as
tattooing, cutting off the nose, cutting off the feet, castration, and death.

禮法度數
形名比詳
Rites and laws, weights, measures,
the careful comparison of forms and names[^13-6]---
治之末也
these are the trivia of good government.

[^13-6]: That is, the correspondence between an official's title and his actual performance in office,
an important principle in Legalist doctrine.

鐘鼓之音
羽毛之容
The tones of bell and drum,
the posturings of feather and tassel---
樂之末也
these are the trivia of music.[^13-7]

[^13-7]: Music here includes the dance, in which feathers and tassels made of yak tails were used.

哭泣衰絰
隆殺之服
Lamentation and coarse garments,
the mourning periods of varying lengths---
哀之末也
these are the trivia of grief.

此五末者須精神之運
心術之動然後從之者也
These five trivia must wait for the movement of pure spirit,
for the vitality of the mind's art before they can command respect.

末學者古人有之
而非所以先也
The study of such trivia was known to antiquity,
but the men of old gave them no precedence.

***

君先而臣從
The ruler precedes, the minister follows;
父先而子從
the father precedes, the son follows;
兄先而弟從
the older brother precedes, the younger brother follows;
長先而少從
the senior precedes, the junior follows;
男先而女從
the man precedes, the woman follows;
夫先而婦從
the husband precedes, the wife follows.

夫尊卑先後
天地之行也
故聖人取象焉
Honor and lowliness, precedence and following,
are part of the workings of Heaven and earth,
and from them the sage draws his model.

***

天尊地卑
Heaven is honorable, earth lowly---
神明之位也
such are their ranks in spiritual enlightenment.

春夏先
秋冬後
Spring and summer precede,
autumn and winter follow---
四時之序也
such is the sequence of the four seasons.

萬物化作
萌區有狀
盛衰之殺
變化之流也
The ten thousand things change and grow,
their roots and buds, each with its distinctive form,
flourishing and decaying by degree,
a constant flow of change and transformation.

夫天地至神
而有尊卑先後之序
而況人道乎
If Heaven and earth, the loftiest in spirituality,
have yet their sequence of honorable and lowly, of preceder and follower,
how much more must the way of man!

宗廟尚親
In the ancestral temple, honor is determined by degree of kinship;
朝廷尚尊
in the court, by degree of nobility;
鄉黨尚齒
in the village, by degree of seniority;
行事尚賢
in the administration of affairs, by degree of worth.

大道之序也
This is the sequence of the Great Way.

***

語道而非其序者非其道也
If you speak of the Way and not of its sequence, then it is not a way;
語道而非其道者安取道
and if you speak of a way that is not a way, then how can anyone make his way by it?

是故古之明大道者
先明天而道德次之
Therefore the men of ancient times who clearly understood the Great Way
first made clear Heaven and then went on to the Way and its Virtue.

道德已明
而仁義次之
Having made clear the Way and its Virtue,
they went on to benevolence and righteousness.

仁義已明
而分守次之
Having made clear benevolence and righteousness,
they went on to the observance of duties.

分守已明
而形名次之
Having made clear the observance of duties,
they went on to forms and names.

形名已明
而因任次之
Having made clear forms and names,
they went on to the assignment of suitable offices.

因任已明
而原省次之
Having made clear the assignment of suitable offices,
they went on to the scrutiny of performance.

原省已明
而是非次之
Having made clear the scrutiny of performance,
they went on to the judgment of right and wrong.

是非已明
而賞罰次之
Having made clear the judgment of right and wrong,
they went on to rewards and punishments.

賞罰已明
而愚知處宜
貴賤履位
仁賢不肖襲情
必分其能
必由其名
Having made clear rewards and punishments,
they could be certain that stupid and wise were in their proper place,
that eminent and lowly were rightly ranked,
that good and worthy men as well as unworthy ones showed their true form,
that all had duties suited to their abilities,
that all acted in accordance with their titles.

以此事上
以此畜下
以此治物
以此修身
It was in this way that superiors were served,
inferiors were shepherded,
external things were ordered,
the inner man was trained.

知謀不用
必歸其天
Knowledge and scheming were unused,
yet all found rest in Heaven.

此之謂太平
治之至也
This was called the Great Peace,
the Highest Government.

故書曰有形有名
Hence the book says, "There are forms and there are names."[^13-8]

[^13-8]: It is not known what book the writer is quoting.
The whole passage appears to be an attempt to combine
Daoist, Confucian, and Legalist terminology and concepts of government into one comprehensive system,
the sort of eclecticism often found in thinkers of the Qin and early Han.

形名者古人有之
而非所以先也
Forms and names were known to antiquity,
but the men of old gave them no precedence.

***

古之語大道者
五變而形名可舉
九變而賞罰可言也
Those who spoke of the Great Way in ancient times
could count to five in the sequence [described earlier] and pick out "forms and names"
or count to nine and discuss "rewards and punishments."

驟而語形名不知其本也
But to jump right in and talk about "forms and names" is to lack an understanding of the source;
驟而語賞罰不知其始也
to jump right in and talk about "rewards and punishments" is to lack an understanding of the beginning.

倒道而言
迕道而說者
人之所治也
安能治人
Those who stand the Way on its head before describing it,
who turn it backward before expounding it,
may be brought to order by others,
but how could they be capable of bringing others to order?

驟而語形名賞罰
此有知治之具
非知治之道
Those who jump right in and talk about "forms and names," "rewards and punishments,"
have an understanding of the tools for bringing order
but no understanding of the way to bring order.

可用於天下
不足以用天下
They may work for the world,
but they are not worthy to make the world work for them.

此之謂辯士
一曲之人也
They are rhetoricians,
scholars cramped in one corner of learning.

禮法度數
形名比詳
Rites and laws, weights and measures,
the careful comparison of forms and names---
古人有之
the men of old had all these.

此下之所以事上
非上之所以畜下也
They are the means by which those below serve those above,
not the means by which those above shepherd those below.

---

昔者舜問於堯曰天王之
用心何如
Long ago Shun asked Yao, "As Heaven-appointed king,
how do you use your mind?"

***

堯曰吾不敖無告
不廢窮民
Yao replied, "I never abuse those who have nowhere to sue
nor reject the poor people.

苦死者
嘉孺子而哀婦人
Grieving for the dead,
comforting the orphan, pitying the widow---
此吾所以用心也
I use my mind in these things alone."

***

舜曰美則美矣
Shun said, "Admirable as far as admirableness goes.

而未大也
But not yet great."

***

堯曰然則何如
Yao said, "Then what should I do?"

***

舜曰天德而出寧
日月照而四時行
Shun said, "Heaven raised on high, earth in peace,[^13-9]
sun and moon shining, the four seasons marching---
若晝夜之有經
雲行而雨施矣
if you could be like the constant succession of day and night,
the clouds that move, the rains that fall!"

[^13-9]: Reading *deng* in place of *de*, and *tu* in place of *chu*,
in accordance with the emendations by Zhang Binglin and Sun Yirang, respectively.

***

堯曰膠膠擾擾乎
"And to think I have been going to all this bustle and bother!" said Yao.

子天之合也
"You are one who joins with Heaven;
我人之合也
I am one who joins with man."

***

夫天地者古之所大也
而黃帝堯舜之所共美也
Heaven and earth have been called great since ancient times,
have been praised in chorus by the Yellow Emperor, Yao, and Shun.

故古之王天下者
The kings of the world in ancient times---
奚為哉
what need had they for action?

天地而已矣
Heaven and earth was enough for them.

---

孔子西藏書於周室
Confucius went west to deposit his works with the royal house of Zhou.

子路謀曰由聞周之徵藏史有老聃者
免而歸居
Zilu advised him, saying, "I have heard that the Keeper of the Royal Archives is one Lao Dan,
now retired and living at home.

夫子欲藏書
則試往因焉
If you wish to deposit your works,
you might try going to see him about it."

***

孔子曰善
往見老聃
而老聃不許
"Excellent!" said Confucius
and went to see Lao Dan,
but Lao Dan would not give permission.

於是繙十二經以說
Thereupon Confucius unwrapped his Twelve Classics and began expounding them.[^13-10]

[^13-10]: There are various explanations of the phrase "Twelve Classics," for example,
the Six Confucian Classics with six commentaries,
or the _Spring and Autumn Annals_, which covers the reigns of twelve dukes of Lu.

老聃中其說曰大謾
Halfway through the exposition,
Lao Dan said, "This will take forever!

願聞其要
Just let me hear the gist of the thing!"

***

孔子曰要在仁義
"The gist of it," said Confucius, "is benevolence and righteousness."

老聃曰請問仁義人之性邪
"May I ask if benevolence and righteousness belong to the inborn nature of man?" said Lao Dan.

***

孔子曰然
"Of course," said Confucius.

君子不仁則不成
"If the gentleman lacks benevolence, he will get nowhere;
不義則不生
if he lacks righteousness, he cannot even stay alive.

仁義真人之性也
Benevolence and righteousness are truly the inborn nature of man.

又將奚為矣
What else could they be?"

***

老聃曰請問何謂仁義
Lao Dan said, "May I ask your definition of benevolence and righteousness?"

***

孔子曰中心物愷
兼愛無私
Confucius said, "To be glad and joyful[^13-11] in mind,
to embrace universal love and be without partisanship---
此仁義之情也
this is the true form of benevolence and righteousness."

[^13-11]: Reading *yi* (pleased) in place of *wu* in accordance with Zhang Binglin's emendation.

***

老聃曰意
Lao Dan said, "Hmm---
幾乎
close---
後言
except for the last part.

夫兼愛
'Universal love'---
不亦迂乎
that's a rather nebulous ideal, isn't it?

無私焉乃私也
And to be without partisanship is already a kind of partisanship.

夫子若欲使天下無失其牧乎
Do you want to keep the world from losing its simplicity?[^13-12]

[^13-12]: Reading *pu* in place of *mu* to correspond to the parallel sentence in sec. 14, p. 115.

則天地固有常矣
日月固有明矣
星辰固有列矣
禽獸固有群矣
樹木固有立矣
Heaven and earth hold fast to their constant ways,
the sun and moon to their brightness,
the stars and planets to their ranks,
the birds and beasts to their flocks,
the trees and shrubs to their stands.

夫子亦放德而行
循道而趨
已至矣
You have only to go along with Virtue in your actions,
to follow the Way in your journey,
and already you will be there.

又何偈偈乎揭仁義
若擊鼓而求亡子焉
Why these flags of benevolence and righteousness so bravely up-raised,
as though you were beating a drum and searching for a lost child?

意夫子亂人之性也
Ah, you will bring confusion to the nature of man!"

---

士成綺見老子
Shi Chengqi went to see Laozi.

而問曰吾聞夫子聖人也
吾固不辭遠道
而來願見
"I had heard that you were a sage," he said,
"and so, without minding how long the road was,
I came to beg an interview---
百舍重趼
而不敢息
a hundred nights along the way, feet covered with calluses,
and yet I did not dare to stop and rest.

今吾觀子
非聖人也
Now that I see you, though,
I find you are no sage at all.

鼠壤有餘蔬
而棄妹之者
不仁也
Rat holes heaped with leftover grain,
and yet you turn your little sister out of the house,
an unkind act indeed!

生熟不盡於前
而積歛無崖
More raw and cooked food in front of you than you can ever get through,
and yet you go on endlessly hoarding goods!"[^13-13]

[^13-13]: One can easily gather from the _Daodejing_ that Laozi favored frugality,
but nothing is known about these legends of his personal stinginess and lack of charity to his little sister.

老子漠然不應
Laozi looked blank and made no reply.

***

士成綺明日復見曰昔者吾有刺於子
今吾心正卻矣
The following day, Shi Chengqi came to see him again and said, "Yesterday I was very sharp with you,
but now I have no heart for that sort of thing.[^13-14]

[^13-14]: Following Ma Xulun's emendation and interpretation.

何故也
I wonder why that is?"

***

老子曰夫巧知神聖之人
Laozi said, "Artful wisdom, the spirit-like sage---
吾自以為脫焉
I hope I have shuffled off categories of that sort!

昔者子呼我牛也而謂之牛
If you'd called me an ox, I'd have said I was an ox;
呼我馬也而謂之馬
if you'd called me a horse, I'd have said I was a horse.

苟有其實
人與之名
而弗受
If the reality is there
and you refuse to accept the name that men give it,
you'll only lay yourself open to double harassment.

再受其殃
My submission is a constant submission;
吾服也恒服
吾非以服有服
I do not submit because I think it's time to submit."

士成綺雁行避影
履行遂進而問修身若何
Shi Chengqi backed respectfully away so that he would not tread on Laozi's shadow
and then advanced once more in a humble manner and asked how he should go about cultivating his person.

***

老子曰而容崖然
而目衝然
而顙頯然
而口闞然
而狀義然
似繫馬而止也
動而持
發也機
Laozi said, "Your face is grim,
your eyes are fierce,
your forehead is broad,
your mouth is gaping,
your manner is overbearing,
like a horse held back by a tether,
watching for a chance to bolt,
bounding off as though shot from a crossbow.

察而審
知巧
而睹於泰
Scrutinizing ever so carefully,
crafty in wisdom,
parading your arrogance---
凡以為不信
all this invites mistrust.

邊竟有人焉
其名為竊
Up in the borderlands,
a man like you would be taken for a thief!"

---

夫子曰夫道於大不終
於小不遺
The Master said: The Way does not falter before the huge,
is not forgetful of the tiny;
故萬物備
therefore the ten thousand things are complete in it.

廣廣乎
其無不容也
Vast and ample,
there is nothing it does not receive.

淵乎
其不可測也
Deep and profound,
how can it be fathomed?

形德仁義
Punishment and favor,[^13-15] benevolence and righteousness---
神之末也
非至人孰能定之
these are trivia to the spirit,
and yet who but the Perfect Man can put them in their rightful place?

[^13-15]: A Legalist term; see _Han Feizi_, sec. 7,
where punishment and favor are called "the two handles" of political power.

***

夫至人有世不亦大乎
When the Perfect Man rules the world, he has hold of a huge thing, does he not?---
而不足以為之累
yet it is not enough to snare him in entanglement.

天下奮柄而不與之偕
He works the handles that control the world but is not a party to the workings.

審乎無假而不與利遷
He sees clearly into what has no falsehood and is unswayed by thoughts of gain.

極物之真
能守其本
He ferrets out the truth of things
and knows how to cling to the source.

故外天地
遺萬物
而神未嘗有所困也
Therefore he can put Heaven and earth outside himself,
forget the ten thousand things,
and his spirit has no cause to be wearied.

通乎道合乎德
退仁義賓禮樂
至人之心有所定矣
He dismisses benevolence and righteousness,
rejects[^13-16] rites and music,
for the mind of the Perfect Man knows where to find repose.

[^13-16]: Reading *bin* with the hand radical.

---

世之所貴道者書也
Men of the world who value the Way all turn to books.

書不過語
But books are nothing more than words.

語有貴也
Words have value;
語之所貴者意也
what is of value in words is meaning.

意有所隨
意之所隨者不可以言傳也
Meaning has something it is pursuing,
but the thing that it is pursuing cannot be put into words and handed down.

而世因貴言傳書
世雖貴之
我猶不足貴也
The world values words and hands down books,
but although the world values them,
I do not think them worth valuing.

為其貴非其貴也
What the world takes to be value is not real value.

***

故視而可見者形與色也
What you can look at and see are forms and colors;
聽而可聞者名與聲也
what you can listen to and hear are names and sounds.

悲夫
What a pity!---
世人以形色名聲
為足以得彼之情
that the men of the world should suppose that form and color, name and sound,
are sufficient to convey the truth of a thing.

夫形色名聲果不足以得彼之情
則知者不言
言者不知
It is because in the end, they are not sufficient to convey truth
that "those who know do not speak,
those who speak do not know."[^13-17]

[^13-17]: The section in quotation marks is identical with the beginning of _Daodejing_ LVI.

而世豈識之哉
But how can the world understand this!

---

桓公讀書於堂上
Duke Huan was in his hall reading a book.

輪扁斲輪於堂下
釋椎鑿而上
問桓公曰敢問公之所讀者
The wheel-wright Pian, who was in the yard below chiseling a wheel,
laid down his mallet and chisel, stepped up into the hall,
and said to Duke Huan, "This book Your Grace is reading---
何言邪
may I venture to ask whose words are in it?"

公曰聖人之言也
"The words of the sages," said the duke.

曰聖人在乎
"Are the sages still alive?"

公曰已死矣
"Dead long ago," said the duke.

***

曰然則君之所讀者
古人之糟魄已夫
"In that case, what you are reading there
is nothing but the chaff and dregs of the men of old!"

***

桓公曰寡人讀書
輪人安得議乎
"Since when does a wheelwright
have permission to comment on the books I read?" said Duke Huan.

有說則可
"If you have some explanation, well and good.

無說則死
If not, it's your life!"

***

輪扁曰臣也以臣之事觀之
Wheelwright Pian said, "I look at it from the point of view of my own work.

斲輪
徐則甘而不固
When I chisel a wheel,
if the blows of the mallet are too gentle, the chisel will slide and won't take hold.

疾則苦而不入
But if they're too hard, it will bite and won't budge.

不徐不疾
Not too gentle, not too hard---
得之於手而應於心
you can get it in your hand and feel it in your mind.

口不能言
有數存焉於其間
You can't put it into words,
and yet there's a knack to it somehow.

臣不能以喻臣之子
臣之子亦不能受之於臣
I can't teach it to my son,
and he can't learn it from me.

是以行年七十
而老斲輪
So I've gone along for seventy years,
and at my age I'm still chiseling wheels.

古之人與其不可傳也死矣
然則君之所讀者
When the men of old died,
they took with them the things that couldn't be handed down.

古人之糟魄已夫
So what you are reading there must be nothing but the chaff and dregs of the men of old."
