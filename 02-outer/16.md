## 16 繕性 Mending the Inborn Nature

繕性於俗俗學
Those who set about mending the inborn nature through vulgar learning,
以求復其初
hoping thereby to return once more to the Beginning;
滑欲於俗思
those who set about muddling their desires through vulgar ways of thought,
以求致其明
hoping thereby to attain clarity---
謂之蔽蒙之民
they may be called the blind and benighted people.[^16-1]

[^16-1]: I punctuate after *xue* and *si*. The writer is attacking both the Confucian and Mohist ideals
of moral training and those schools of thought that advocated the lessening or elimination of desire.

***

古之治道者
The men of ancient times who practiced the Way
以恬養知
employed tranquillity to cultivate knowledge.

知生
Knowledge lived in them,
而無以知為也
yet they did nothing for its sake.

謂之以知養恬
So they may be said to have employed knowledge to cultivate tranquillity.

知與恬交相養
Knowledge and tranquillity took turns cultivating each other,
而和理出其性
and harmony and order emerged from the inborn nature.

***

夫德和也
Virtue is harmony,
道理也
the Way is order.

德無不容
When Virtue embraces all things,
仁也
we have benevolence.

道無不理
When the Way is in all respects well ordered,
義也
we have righteousness.

義明
When righteousness is clearly understood
而物親
and all things cling to it,
忠也
we have loyalty.

中純實
When within there is purity, fullness,
而反乎情
and a return to true form,
樂也
we have music.

信行容體
When good faith is expressed in face and body
而順乎文
and there is a compliance with elegance,
禮也
we have rites.

禮樂遍行
But if all emphasis is placed on the conduct of rites and music,
則天下亂矣
then the world will fall into disorder.

彼正而蒙己德
The ruler, in his efforts to rectify, will draw a cloud over his own virtue,
德則不冒
and his virtue will no longer extend to all things.

冒
And should he try to force it to extend,
則物必失其性也
then things would invariably lose their in-born nature.[^16-2]

[^16-2]: This passage, which attempts to derive the Confucian virtues and concerns from the Way,
presents many difficulties in interpretation.
Probably the text is faulty---judging from the parallelism;
for example, "good faith" ought to have a definition of its own
instead of being part of the definition of "rites." I follow Fukunaga's interpretation.

***

古之人在混芒之中
The men of old dwelled in the midst of crudity and chaos;
與一世
side by side with the rest of the world,
而得澹漠焉
they attained simplicity and silence there.

當是時也陰陽和靜
At that time the yin and yang were harmonious and still;
鬼神不擾
ghosts and spirits worked no mischief;
四時得節
the four seasons kept to their proper order;
萬物不傷
the ten thousand things knew no injury;
群生不夭
and living creatures were free from premature death.

人雖有知
Although men had knowledge,
無所用之
they did not use it.

此之謂至一
This was called the Perfect Unity.

當是時也
At this time,
莫之為
no one made a move to do anything,
而常自然
and there was unvarying spontaneity.

***

逮德下衰
The time came, however, when Virtue began to dwindle and decline,
及燧人伏羲始為天下
and then Suiren and Fu Xi stepped forward to take charge of the world.

是故順
As a result there was compliance,
而不一
but no longer any unity.

德又下衰
Virtue continued to dwindle and decline,
及神農黃帝始為天下
and then Shennong and the Yellow Emperor stepped forward to take charge of the world.

是故安
As a result, there was security
而不順
but no longer any compliance.

德又下衰
Virtue continued to dwindle and decline,
及唐虞始為天下
and then Yao and Shun stepped forward to take charge of the world.[^16-3]

[^16-3]: All these figures are mythical rulers or cultural heroes;
Suiren and Shennong are the discoverers of fire and agriculture, respectively.

興治化之流
They set about in various fashions to order and transform the world and,
澆淳散朴
in doing so, defiled purity and shattered simplicity.

離道以善
The Way was pulled apart for the sake of goodness;
險德以行
Virtue was imperiled for the sake of conduct.

然後去性
After this, inborn nature was abandoned,
而從於心
and minds were set free to roam,
心與心識
mind joining with mind in understanding;
知而不足以定天下
there was knowledge, but it could not bring stability to the world.

然後附之以文
After this, "culture" was added,
益之以博
and "breadth" was piled on top.

文滅質
"Culture" destroyed the substantial;
博溺心
"breadth" drowned the mind;
然後民始惑亂
and after this, the people began to be confused and disordered.

無以反其性情
They had no way to revert to the true form of their inborn nature
而復其初
or to return once more to the Beginning.

***

由是觀之
From this we may see
世喪道矣
that the world has lost the Way
道喪世矣
and the Way has lost the world;
世與道交相喪也
the world and the Way have lost each other.

道之人何由興乎世
What means does a man of the Way have to go forward in the world?

世亦何由興乎道哉
What means does the world have to go forward in the Way?

道無以興乎世
The Way cannot go forward in the world,
世無以興乎道
and the world cannot go forward in the Way.

雖聖人不在山林之中
So although the sage does not retire to dwell in the midst of the mountain forest,
其德隱矣
his Virtue is already hidden.

隱故不自隱
It is already hidden, and therefore he does not need to hide it himself.

***

古之所謂隱士者
The so-called scholars in hiding of ancient times
非伏其身而弗見也
did not conceal their bodies and refuse to let them be seen;
非閉其言而不出也
they did not shut in their words and refuse to let them out;
非藏其知而不發也
they did not stow away their knowledge and refuse to share it.

時命大謬也
But the fate of the times was too much awry.

當時命而大行乎天下
If the fate of the times had been with them and they could have done great deeds in the world,
則反一無跡
then they would have returned to Unity and left no trace behind.

不當時命而大窮乎天下
But the fate of the times was against them and brought them only great hardship in the world,
則深根
and therefore they deepened their roots,
寧極
rested in perfection,
而待
and waited.

此存身之道也
This was the way they kept themselves alive.[^16-4]

[^16-4]: As Fukunaga pointed out, this concept of good and bad times that are fated
is quite contrary to the philosophy expressed in the inner chapters,
according to which any time is as good as any other.
The thinking here is, in fact, much closer to the ideas of timeliness and fate
expressed in the Confucian _Analects_ or the _Book of Changes_.

***

古之行身者
Those in ancient times who wished to keep themselves alive
不以辯飾知
did not use eloquence to ornament their knowledge.

不以知窮天下
They did not use their knowledge to make trouble for the world;
不以知窮德
they did not use their knowledge to make trouble for Virtue.

危然處其所
Loftily they kept to their places
而反其性
and returned to their inborn nature.

己又何為哉
Having done that, what more was there for them to do?

道固不小行
The Way has no use for petty conduct;
德固不小識
Virtue has no use for petty understanding.

小識傷德
Petty understanding injures Virtue;
小行傷道
petty conduct injures the Way.

故曰
Therefore it is said,
正己而已矣
Rectify yourself, that is all.[^16-5]

[^16-5]: Why the writer quotes such an un-Daoist injunction as "Rectify yourself,"
or what he means by it, I do not know.

樂全之謂得志
When joy is complete, this is called the fulfillment of ambition.

***

古之所謂得志者
When the men of ancient times spoke of the fulfillment of ambition,
非軒冕之謂也
they did not mean fine carriages and caps.

謂其無以益其樂而已矣
They meant simply that joy was so complete that it could not be made greater.

今之所謂得志者
Nowadays, however, when men speak of the fulfillment of ambition,
軒冕之謂也
they mean fine carriages and caps.

軒冕在身
But carriages and caps affect the body alone,
非性命也
not the inborn nature and fate.

物之儻來寄者也
Such things from time to time may happen to come your way.

寄之其來不可圉
When they come, you cannot keep them from arriving,
其去不可止
but when they depart, you cannot stop them from going.

故不為軒冕肆志
Therefore carriages and caps are no excuse for becoming puffed up with pride,
不為窮約趨俗
and hardship and poverty are no excuse for fawning on the vulgar.

其樂彼與此同
You should find the same joy in one condition
故無憂而已矣
as in the other and thereby be free of care, that is all.

今寄去
But now, when the things that happened take their leave,
則不樂
you cease to be joyful.

由是觀之雖樂
From this point of view, though you have joy,
未嘗不荒也
it will always be fated for destruction.

故曰喪己於物
Therefore it is said, Those who destroy themselves in things
失性於俗者
and lose their inborn nature in the vulgar
謂之倒置之民
may be called the upside-down people.
