# 外篇 Outer Chapters

## 8 駢拇 Webbed Toes

駢拇枝指
Two toes webbed together, a sixth finger forking off---
出乎性哉而侈於德
these come from the inborn nature but are excretions as far as Virtue is concerned.[^8-1]

[^8-1]: Virtue (*de*) here seems to mean inner power or vital force; see p. 58, n. 10.
This and the following three sections
are much closer in thought to the _Daodejing_ of Laozi than the preceding sections,
and the use of the word *de* seems to accord with its use in the _Daodejing_.
Also, here we encounter for the first time in Zhuangzi the term *xing* or "inborn nature,"
which is so important to Confucian thought.

附贅縣疣
Swelling tumors and protruding wens---
出乎形哉而侈於性
these come from the body but are excretions as far as the inborn nature is concerned.

多方乎仁義而用之者
列於五藏哉
Men over-nice in the ways of benevolence and righteousness try to put these into practice,
even to line them up with the five vital organs![^8-2]

[^8-2]: The five vital organs---liver, lungs, heart, kidneys, and spleen---were
related to the five elements and later to the five Confucian virtues---benevolence,
propriety, good faith, righteousness, wisdom.

而非道德之正也
This is not the right approach to the Way and its Virtue.

是故駢於足者
連無用之肉也
Therefore he who has two toes webbed together
has grown a flap of useless flesh;
枝於手者
樹無用之指也
he who has a sixth finger forking out of his hand
has sprouted a useless digit;
多方駢枝於五藏之情者
淫僻於仁義之行
而多方於聰明之用也
and he who imposes overnice ways, webs, and forked fingers on the original form of the five vital organs
will become deluded and perverse in the practice of benevolence and righteousness,
and overnice in the use of his hearing and sight.

是故駢於明者
亂五色
淫文章
青黃
黼黻之煌煌
Thus he who is web toed in eyesight
will be confused by the five colors,
bewitched by patterns and designs,
by the dazzling hues of blue and yellow,
of embroidery and brocade---
非乎
am I wrong?

而離朱是已
So we have Li Zhu.[^8-3]

[^8-3]: Also called Li Lou;
noted for his exceptionally keen eyesight.

多於聰者
亂五聲
淫六律
金石
絲竹
黃鐘大呂之聲
He who is overnice in hearing
will be confused by the five notes,
bewitched by the six tones,
by the sounds of metal and stone,
strings and woodwinds,
the *huangzhong* and *dalü* pitch pipes---
非乎
am I wrong?

而師曠是已
So we have Music Master Kuang.[^8-4]

[^8-4]: Famous musician mentioned on p. 12.
With this passage, compare _Daodejing_ XII: "The five colors confuse the eye,
the five sounds dull the ear."

枝於仁者
擢德塞性以收名聲
使天下簧鼓以奉不及之法
He who is fork fingered with benevolence
will tear out the Virtue given him and stifle his inborn nature in order to seize fame and reputation,
leading the world on with pipe and drum in the service of an unattainable ideal---
非乎
am I wrong?

而曾史是已
So we have Zeng and Shih.[^8-5]

[^8-5]: Zeng Shen, a disciple of Confucius,
and Shih Yu, historiographer of the state of Wei,
paragons of benevolence and righteousness, respectively.

駢於辯者
纍瓦
結繩
竄句
遊心於堅白同異之間
而敝跬
譽無用之言
He who is web toed in argumentation
will pile up bricks,
knot the plumb line,
apply the curve,[^8-6]
letting his mind wander in the realm of "hard" and "white," "likeness" and "difference,"
huffing and puffing away,
lauding his useless words---
非乎
am I wrong?

[^8-6]: All seem to be building metaphors,
though the meaning of the last is doubtful.
I read *gou* instead of *ju*.

而楊墨是已
So we have Yang and Mo.[^8-7]

[^8-7]: The hedonist philosopher Yang Zhu and the advocate of universal love Mo Di.
We would expect a reference to the logicians, however,
since they were the ones who argued about "hard," "white," etc.; see p. 12, n. 9.

故此皆多駢旁枝之道
非天下之至正也
All these men walk a way that is overnice, web toed, wide of the mark, fork fingered,
not that which is the True Rightness of the world.

***

彼正正者
不失其性命之情
He who holds to True Rightness[^8-8]
does not lose the original form of his inborn nature.

[^8-8]: Reading *zhizheng* as in the preceding sentence.

故合者不為駢
So for him, joined things are not webbed toes;
而枝者不為跂
things forking off are not superfluous fingers;
長者不為有餘
the long is never too much;
短者不為不足
the short is never too little.[^8-9]

[^8-9]: At this point,
the meaning of the symbolism seems to shift (with some violence to the logic of the argument).
The webbed toes and extra fingers,
which earlier represented the forced and unnatural morality of Confucianism,
now become natural deformities such as we have seen in the earlier chapters,
which it would be wrong to try to correct.

是故鳧脛雖短
續之則憂
The duck's legs are short,
but to stretch them out would worry him;
鶴脛雖長
斷之則悲
the crane's legs are long,
but to cut them down would make him sad.

故性長非所斷
What is long by nature needs no cutting off;
性短非所續
what is short by nature needs no stretching.

無所去憂也
That would be no way to get rid of worry.

意仁義其非人情乎
I wonder, then, whether benevolence and righteousness are part of man's true form?

彼仁人
Those benevolent men---
何其多憂也
how much worrying they do!

***

且夫駢於拇者決之則泣
The man with two toes webbed together would weep if he tried to tear them apart;
枝於手者齕之則啼
the man with a sixth finger on his hand would howl if he tried to gnaw it off.

二者或有餘於數
Of these two, one has more than the usual number;
或不足於數
the other has less;
其於憂一也
but in worrying about it, they are identical.

今世之仁人蒿目
Nowadays the benevolent men of the age lift up weary eyes,[^8-10]
而憂世之患
不仁之人決性命之情而饕富貴
worrying over the ills of the world,
while the men of no benevolence tear apart the original form of their inborn nature in their greed for eminence and wealth.

[^8-10]: Following Ma Xulun's interpretation.

故意仁義其非人情乎
Therefore I wonder whether benevolence and righteousness are really part of man's true form?

自三代以下者
天下何其囂囂也
From the Three Dynasties on down,[^8-11]
what a lot of fuss and hubbub they have made in the world!

[^8-11]: The Xia, Shang, and Zhou dynasties.

***

且夫待鉤繩規矩而正者
是削其性
If we must use curve and plumb line, compass and square, to make something right,
this means cutting away its inborn nature;
待繩約膠漆而固者
是侵其德也
if we must use cords and knots, glue and lacquer, to make something firm,
this means violating its natural Virtue.

屈折禮樂
呴俞仁義
以慰天下之心者
此失其常然也
So the crouchings and bendings of rights and music,
the smiles and beaming looks of benevolence and righteousness,
which are intended to comfort the hearts of the world,
in fact destroy their constant naturalness.

***

天下有常然
For in the world, there can be constant naturalness.

常然者
曲者不以鉤
直者不以繩
圓者不以規
方者不以矩
附離不以膠漆
約束不以纆索
Where there is constant naturalness,
things are arced not by the use of the curve,
straightened not by the use of the plumb line,
rounded not by the compasses,
squared not by T squares,
joined not by glue and lacquer,
bound not by ropes and lines.

故天下
誘然皆生而不知其所以生
Then all things in the world,
simple and compliant, live and never know how they happen to live;
同焉皆得而不知其所以得
all things, rude and unwitting,[^8-12] get what they need and never know how they happen to get it.

[^8-12]: Following Fukunaga, I read *tong* with the man radical.
A similar phrase, *tonghu*, appears in sec. 9,
and *tongran* in sec. 23.

故古今不二
Past and present, it has been the same;
不可虧也
nothing can do injury to this [principle].

則仁義又奚連連如膠漆纆索
而遊乎道德之間為哉
Why, then, come with benevolence and righteousness, that tangle and train of glue and lacquer, ropes and lines,
and try to wander in the realm of the Way and its Virtue?

使天下惑也
You will only confuse the world!

***

夫小惑易方
A little confusion can alter the sense of direction;
大惑易性
a great confusion can alter the inborn nature.

何以知其然邪
How do I know this is so?

自虞氏招仁義以撓天下也
天下莫不奔命於仁義
Ever since that man of the Yu clan[^8-13] began preaching benevolence and righteousness and stirring up the world,
all the men in the world have dashed headlong for benevolence and righteousness.

[^8-13]: The sage ruler Shun, idol of the Confucian philosophers.

是非以仁義易其性與
This is because benevolence and righteousness have altered their inborn nature, is it not?

***

故嘗試論之
Let me try explaining what I mean.

自三代以下者
天下莫不以物易其性矣
From the Three Dynasties on down,
everyone in the world has altered his inborn nature because of some [external] thing.

小人
The petty man?---
則以身殉利
he will risk death for the sake of profit.

士
The knight?---
則以身殉名
he will risk it for the sake of fame.

大夫
The high official?---
則以身殉家
he will risk it for family;

聖人
the sage?---
則以身殉天下
he will risk it for the world.

故此數子者
事業不同
名聲異號
All these various men
go about the business in a different way
and are tagged differently when it comes to fame and reputation;
其於傷性以身為殉一也
but in blighting their inborn nature and risking their lives for something, they are the same.

***

臧與穀二人相與牧羊
而俱亡其羊
The slave boy and the slave girl were out together herding their sheep,
and both of them lost their flocks.

問臧奚事
Ask the slave boy how it happened:
則挾筴讀書
well, he had a bundle of writing slips and was reading a book.[^8-14]

問穀奚事
Ask the slave girl how it happened:
則博塞以遊
well, she was playing a game of toss-and-wait-your-turn.

二人者事業不同
其於亡羊均也
They went about the business in different ways,
but in losing their sheep, they were equal.

伯夷死名於首陽之下
Bo Yi died for reputation at the foot of Shouyang Mountain;
盜跖死利於東陵之上
Robber Zhi died for gain on top of Eastern Mound.[^8-15]

[^8-15]: On Bo Yi, the model of righteousness; see p. 126, n. 3;
Robber Zhi, who appears later as the subject of sec. 29,
represents the ultimate in greed and violence.

二人者所死不同
其於殘生傷性均也
The two of them died different deaths,
but in destroying their lives and blighting their inborn nature, they were equal.

奚必伯夷之是而盜跖之非乎
Why, then, must we say that Bo Yi was right and Robber Zhi wrong?

***

天下盡殉也
Everyone in the world risks his life for something.

彼其所殉仁義也
則俗謂之君子
If he risks it for benevolence and righteousness,
then custom names him a gentleman;
其所殉貨財也
則俗謂之小人
if he risks it for goods and wealth,
then custom names him a petty man.

其殉一也
則有君子焉
有小人焉
The risking is the same,
and yet we have a gentleman here,
a petty man there.

若其殘生損性
則盜跖亦伯夷已
In destroying their lives and blighting their inborn nature,
Robber Zhi and Bo Yi were two of a kind.

又惡取君子小人於其間哉
How then can we pick out the gentleman from the petty man in such a case?

***

且夫屬其性乎仁義者
雖通如曾史
非吾所謂臧也
He who applies his nature to benevolence and righteousness
may go as far with it as Zeng and Shi,
but I would not call him an expert.

屬其性於五味
雖通如俞兒
非吾所謂臧也
He who applies his nature to the five flavors
may go as far with it as Yu Er,[^8-16]
but I would not call him an expert.

[^8-16]: Apparently a famous chef and connoisseur of flavor.

屬其性乎五聲
雖通如師曠
非吾所謂聰也
He who applies his nature to the five notes
may go as far with it as Music Master Kuang,
but I would not call this good hearing.

屬其性乎五色
雖通如離朱
非吾所謂明也
He who applies his nature to the five colors
may go as far with it as Li Zhu,
but I would not call this good eyesight.

吾所謂臧者非仁義之謂也
My definition of expertness has nothing to do with benevolence and righteousness;
臧於其德而已矣
it means being expert in regard to your Virtue, that is all.

吾所謂臧者非所謂仁義之謂也
My definition of expertness has nothing to do with benevolence or righteousness;[^8-17]
任其性命之情而已矣
it means following the true form of your inborn nature, that is all.

[^8-17]: This clause is excessively wordy and merely repeats what was said earlier.
I suspect that it is corrupt and that in its original form it contained some reference to the five flavors.

吾所謂聰者非謂其聞彼也
When I speak of good hearing, I do not mean listening to others;
自聞而已矣
I mean simply listening to yourself.

吾所謂明者非謂其見彼也
When I speak of good eyesight, I do not mean looking at others;
自見而已矣
I mean simply looking at yourself.

夫不自見而見彼
不自得而得彼者
是得人之得而不自得其得者也
He who does not look at himself but looks at others,
who does not get hold of himself but gets hold of others,
is getting what other men have got and failing to get what he himself has got.

適人之適而不自適其適者也
He finds joy in what brings joy to other men but finds no joy in what would bring joy to himself.

夫適人之適而不自適其適
雖盜跖與伯夷
是同為淫僻也
And if he finds joy in what brings joy to other men but finds no joy in what brings joy to himself,
then whether he is a Robber Zhi or a Bo Yi,
he is equally deluded and perverse.

余愧乎道德
是以上不敢為仁義之操
而下不敢為淫僻之行也
I have a sense of shame before the Way and its Virtue,
and for that reason I do not venture to raise myself up in deeds of benevolence and righteousness
or to lower myself in deluded and perverse practices.
