## 14 天運 The Turning of Heaven

天其運乎
Does heaven turn?

地其處乎
Does the earth sit still?

日月其爭於所乎
Do sun and moon compete for a place to shine?

孰主張是
Who masterminds all this?

孰維綱是
Who pulls the strings?

孰居無事推而行是
Who, resting inactive himself, gives the push that makes it go this way?

意者其有機緘而不得已邪
I wonder, is there some mechanism that works it and won't let it stop?

意者其運轉而不能自止邪
I wonder if it just rolls and turns and can't bring itself to a halt?

雲者為雨乎
雨者為雲乎
Do the clouds make the rain,
or does the rain make the clouds?

孰隆施是
Who puffs them up, who showers them down like this?

孰居無事淫樂而勸是
Who, resting inactive himself, stirs up all this lascivious joy?[^14-1]

[^14-1]: The expression "clouds and rain" was used from early times to refer to sexual intercourse,
and this may be why the writer employs the odd phrase "lascivious joy."

風起北方
一西一東
有上彷徨
The winds rise in the north,
blowing now west, now east,
whirling up to wander on high.

孰噓吸是
Whose breaths and exhalations are they?

孰居無事而披拂是
Who, resting inactive himself, huffs and puffs them about like this?

***

敢問何故

巫咸袑曰來
The shaman Xian beckoned[^14-2] and said, "Come---
吾語女
I will tell you.

[^14-2]: Reading *zhao* with the hand radical, as Ma Xulun suggested.

天有六極五常
Heaven has the six directions and the five constants.[^14-3]

[^14-3]: Usually taken to be the five elements of Chinese philosophy:
wood, fire, earth, metal, and water.

帝王順之則治
When emperors and kings go along with these, there is good order;
逆之則凶
when they move contrary to these, there is disaster.

九洛之事
治成德備
With the instructions of the Nine Luo,[^14-4]
order can be made to reign and virtue completed.

[^14-4]: Probably a reference to the "Great Plan" section of the _Book of Documents_,
which is in nine divisions and was supposed to have been written
on the back of a tortoise that emerged from the Luo River.

監照下土
天下戴之
The ruler will shine mirror-like over the earth below,
and the world will bear him up.

此謂上皇
He may be called an August One on high."[^14-5]

[^14-5]: On the August Ones, see p. 113, n. 15.

---

商太宰蕩問仁於莊子
Tang, the prime minister of Shang,[^14-6] asked Zhuangzi about benevolence.

[^14-6]: Shang here presumably means the state of Song; see the introduction, p. viii.

***

莊子曰虎狼
Zhuangzi said, "Tigers and wolves---
仁也
they're benevolent."

曰何謂也
"How can you say that?"

***

莊子曰父子相親
Zhuangzi said, "Sire and cubs warm and affectionate with one another---
何為不仁
why do you say they're not benevolent?"

曰請問至仁
"What I am asking to hear about is perfect benevolence."

莊子曰至仁無親
"Perfect benevolence knows no affection," said Zhuangzi.

***

太宰曰蕩聞之
無親則不愛
不愛則不孝
The prime minister said, "I have heard that
where affection is lacking, there will be no love,
and if there is no love, there will be no filial piety.

謂至仁不孝可乎
Can you possibly say that perfect benevolence is unfilial?"

***

莊子曰不然
"No, no," said Zhuangzi.

夫至仁尚矣
"Perfect benevolence is a lofty thing---
孝固不足以言之
words like filial piety would never do to describe it.

此非過孝之言也
不及孝之言也
And what you are talking about is not something that surpasses filial piety
but something that doesn't even come up to it.

夫南行者至於郢
北面而不見冥山
If a traveler to the south turns to look north again when he reaches the city of Ying,
he will no longer see the dark northern mountains.

是何也
Why?

則去之遠也
Because they are too far away.

故曰以敬孝易
Thus it is said, to be filial out of respect is easy;
以愛孝難
to be filial out of love is hard.

以愛孝易
To be filial out of love is easy;
以忘親難
to forget parents is hard.

忘親易
To forget parents is easy;
使親忘我難
to make parents forget you is hard.

使親忘我易
To make parents forget you is easy;
兼忘天下難
to forget the whole world is hard.

兼忘天下易
To forget the whole world is easy;
使天下兼忘我難
to make the whole world forget you is hard.

夫德遺堯舜而不為也
Virtue discards Yao and Shun and rests in inaction.

利澤施於萬世
天下莫知也
Its bounty enriches ten thousand ages,
and yet no one in the world knows this.

豈直太息而言仁孝乎哉
Why all these deep sighs, this talk of benevolence and filial piety?

夫孝悌仁義
忠信貞廉
Filial piety, brotherliness, benevolence, righteousness,
loyalty, trust, honor, integrity---
此皆自勉以役其德者也
for all of these, you must drive yourself and make a slave of Virtue.

不足多也
They are not worth prizing.

故曰至貴國爵并焉
So it is said, Highest eminence scorns the titles of the kingdom;
至富國財并焉
greatest wealth rejects the riches of the kingdom;
至願名譽并焉
loftiest desire ignores fame and reputation.

是以道不渝
It is the Way alone that never varies."

---

北門成問於黃帝曰帝張咸池之樂於洞庭之野
吾始聞之懼
Cheng of North Gate said to the Yellow Emperor, "When Your Majesty performed the Xianchi music in the wilds around Lake Dongting,
I listened, and at first I was afraid.

復聞之怠
卒聞之而惑
I listened some more and felt weary,
and then I listened to the end and felt confused.

蕩蕩默默
乃不自得
Overwhelmed, speechless,
I couldn't get hold of myself."

***

帝曰女殆其然哉
"It's not surprising you felt that way," said the emperor.

吾奏之以人
徵之以天
行之以禮義
建之以太清
"I performed it through man,
tuned it to Heaven,
went forward with ritual principle,
and established it in Great Purity.

夫至樂者先應之以人事
順之以天理
行之以五德
應之以自然
Perfect music must first respond to the needs of man,
accord with the reason of Heaven,
proceed by the Five Virtues,
and blend with spontaneity;
然後調理四時
太和萬物
only then can it bring order to the four seasons
and bestow a final harmony on the ten thousand things.[^14-7]

[^14-7]: The thirty-five characters that make up this sentence
are omitted in some editions because there is strong suspicion
that they are part of a commentary that was erroneously copied into the body of the text.

四時迭起
Then the four seasons will rise one after the other;
萬物循生
the ten thousand things will take their turn at living.

一盛一衰
文武倫經
Now flourishing, now decaying,
the civil and military strains will keep them in step;
一清一濁
陰陽調和
流光其聲
蟄蟲始作
吾驚之以雷霆
now with clear notes, now with dull ones,
the yin and the yang will blend all in harmony,
the sounds flowing forth like light,
like hibernating insects that start to wriggle again,
like the crash of thunder with which I awe the world.

其卒無尾
At the end, no tail;
其始無首
at the beginning, no head;
一死一生
一僨一起
所常無窮
而一不可待
now dead, now alive,
now flat on the ground, now up on its feet,
its constancy is unending,
yet there is nothing that can be counted on.

女故懼也
That's why you felt afraid.

***

吾又奏之以陰陽之和
燭之以日月之明
"Then I played it with the harmony of yin and yang,
lit it with the shining of sun and moon;
其聲能短能長
能柔能剛
變化齊一
不主故常
its notes I was able to make long or short,
yielding or strong,
modulating about a single unity
but bowing before no rule or constancy.

在谷滿谷
在阬滿阬
In the valley they filled the valley;
in the void they filled the void;
塗郤守神
以物為量
plugging up the crevices, holding back the spirit,
accepting things on their own terms.

其聲揮綽
其名高明
Its notes were clear and radiant,[^14-8]
its fame high and bright.

[^14-8]: Following Ma Xulun's interpretation.

是故鬼神守其幽
日月星辰行其紀
Therefore the ghosts and spirits kept to their darkness,
and the sun, moon, stars, and constellations marched in their orbits.

吾止之於有窮
流之於無止
I made it stop where there is an end to things,
made it flow where there is no stopping.

予欲慮之而不能知也
望之而不能見也
逐之而不能及也
You[^14-9] try to fathom it but can't understand,
try to gaze at it but can't see,
try to overtake it but can't catch up.

[^14-9]: Following the texts that read *zi* instead of *yu*.

儻然立於四虛之道
倚於槁梧而吟
You stand dazed before the four-directioned emptiness of the Way
or lean on your desk and moan.

目知窮乎所欲見
Your eyes fail before you can see;
力屈乎所欲逐
your strength knuckles under before you can catch up.[^14-10]

[^14-10]: Since in the preceding passage, the order of the verbs was
"understand," "see," and "catch up," this sentence probably
began originally with a clause describing Cheng's inability to understand,
of which only the single character *zhi* now remains.

吾既不及已夫
It was nothing I could do anything about.

形充空虛
乃至委蛇
Your body melted into the empty void,
and this brought you to an idle freedom.

汝委蛇故怠
It was this idle freedom that made you feel weary.

***

吾又奏之以無怠之聲
調之以自然之命
"Then I played it with unwearying notes
and tuned it to the command of spontaneity.

故若混逐叢生林
樂而無形
布揮而不曳
幽昏而無聲
Therefore there seemed to be a chaos where things grow in thickets together,
a maturity where nothing takes form,
a universal plucking where nothing gets pulled,
a clouded obscurity where there is no sound.

動於無方
居於窈冥
It moved in no direction at all,
rested in mysterious shadow.

或謂之死
或謂之生
或謂之實
或謂之榮
Some called it death,
some called it life,
some called it fruit,
some called it flower.

行流散徙
不主常聲
It flowed and scattered
and bowed before no constant tone.

世疑之
稽於聖人
聖也者達於情而遂於命也
The world, perplexed by it,
went to the sage for instruction,
for the sage is the comprehender of true form and the completer of fate.

天機不張
而五官皆備
When the Heavenly mechanism is not put into action,
and yet the five vital organs are all complete---
此之謂天樂
this may be called the music of Heaven.

無言而心說
Wordless, it delights the mind.

故有焱氏為之頌曰
Therefore the lord of Yan sang its praises thus:

聽
'Listen---
之不聞其聲
you do not hear its sound;
視
之
look---
不見其形
you do not see its form.

充滿天地
苞裏六極
It fills all Heaven and earth,
enwraps all the six directions.'

汝欲聽之而無接焉
You wanted to hear it but had no way to go about it.

而故惑也
That was why you felt confused.[^14-11]

[^14-11]: As the reader well may feel at this point.
On the whole, I follow Fukunaga in the interpretation of this difficult and deliberately paradoxical passage,
though I am not confident that I really understand what it is all about.
It should be noted that because the words for "joy" and "music" are written with the same character,
phrases translated here as "perfect music," "the music of Heaven," etc.,
can also be interpreted to refer to the states of emotion.
The phrase "perfect music" in fact appears later as the title of sec. 18,
where I have rendered it as "Supreme Happiness."

***

樂也者始於懼
懼故祟
"Music begins with fear,
and because of this fear, there is dread, as of a curse.

吾又次之以怠
怠故遁
Then I add the weariness,
and because of the weariness, there is compliance.

卒之於惑
惑故愚
I end it all with confusion,
and because of the confusion, there is stupidity.

愚故道
道可載而與之俱也
And because of the stupidity, there is the Way,
the Way that can be lifted up and carried around wherever you go."

---

孔子西遊於衛
顏淵問師金曰以夫子之行為奚如
When Confucius was away in the west visiting the state of Wei,
Yan Yuan said to Music Master Jin, "What do you think of my master's trip?"[^14-12]

[^14-12]: Yan Yuan, or Yan Hui, was Confucius's favorite disciple.
Music Master Jin was presumably an official of Confucius's native state of Lu.

***

師金曰惜乎
Music Master Jin said, "A pity---
而夫子其窮哉
your master will most likely end up in trouble."

顏淵曰何也
"How so?" asked Yan Yuan.

***

師金曰夫芻狗之未陳也
盛以篋衍
巾以文繡
尸祝齊戒以將之
Music Master Jin said, "Before the straw dogs are presented at the sacrifice,
they are stored in bamboo boxes
and covered over with patterned embroidery,
while the impersonator of the dead and the priest fast and practice austerities in preparation for fetching them.

及其已陳也
行者踐其首脊
But after they have once been presented,
then all that remains for them is to be trampled on, head and back, by passersby;
蘇者取而爨之而已
to be swept up by the grass cutters and burned.[^14-13]

[^14-13]: The straw dogs, also mentioned in _Daodejing_ V,
apparently acted as scapegoats to draw off evil influences at the sacrifice;
hence they were treated with reverence before the sacrifice but thrown away afterward,
and to attempt to put them back in their original boxes would only invite bad luck.

將復取而盛以篋衍
巾以文繡
遊居寢臥其下
彼不得夢
And if anyone should come along and put them back in their bamboo boxes,
cover them over with patterned embroidery,
and linger or lie down to sleep beneath them,
he would dream no proper dreams;
必且數眯焉
on the contrary, he would most certainly be visited again and again by nightmares.

***

今而夫子亦取先王已陳芻狗
聚弟子游居寢臥其下
"Now your master has picked up some old straw dogs that had been presented by the former kings
and has called together his disciples to linger and lie down in sleep beneath them.

故伐樹於宋
削跡於衛
窮於商周
Therefore the people chopped down the tree on him in Song,
wiped away his footprints in Wei,
and made trouble for him in Shang and Zhou---
是非其夢邪
such were the dreams he had.

圍於陳蔡之間
七日不火食
死生相與鄰
They besieged him between Chen and Cai,
and for seven days he ate no cooked food,
till he hovered on the border between life and death---
是非其眯邪
such were the nightmares he had.[^14-14]

[^14-14]: These various difficulties and persecutions
that Confucius and his disciples encountered in their wanderings from state to state
are mentioned in the _Analects_ or other early texts;
here, as earlier, the name Shang seems to stand for Song.

***

夫水行莫如用舟
而陸行莫如用車
"Nothing is as good as a boat for crossing water,
nothing as good as a cart for crossing land.

以舟之可行於水也
而求推之於陸
則沒世不行尋常
But although a boat will get you over water,
if you try to push it across land,
you may push till your dying day and hardly move it any distance at all.

古今非水陸與
周魯非舟車與
And are the past and present not like the water and the land,
and the states of Zhou and Lu not like a boat and a cart?

今蘄行周於魯
是猶推舟於陸也
To hope to practice the ways of Zhou in the state of Lu
is like trying to push a boat over land---
勞而無功
身必有殃
a great deal of work, no success,
and certain danger to the person who tries it.

彼未知夫無方之傳
應物而不窮者也
The man who tries to do so has failed to understand the turning that has no direction,
that responds to things and is never at a loss.

***

且子獨不見夫桔槔者乎
"Have you never seen a well sweep?

引之則俯
Pull it, and down it goes;
舍之則仰
let go, and up it swings.

彼人之所引
It allows itself to be pulled around by men;
非引人也
it doesn't try to pull them.

故俯仰而不得罪於人
So it can go up and down and never be blamed by anybody.

***

故夫三皇五帝之禮義法度
不矜於同
而矜於治
"Thus it is that the rituals and regulations of the Three August Ones and the Five Emperors
are prized not because they were uniform
but because they were capable of bringing about order.[^14-15]

[^14-15]: The Three August Ones (*huang*) and Five Emperors (*di*) are legendary sage rulers of high antiquity,
though it is not certain just which of the numerous legendary rulers the writer
would have included in his list of three and five.
Later on in the chapter, the phrase seems to mean sage rulers in general.

故譬三皇五帝之禮義法度
其猶柤梨橘柚邪
The rituals and regulations of the Three August Ones and the Five Emperors
may be compared to the haw, the pear,
the orange, and the citron.

其味相反
而皆可於口
Their flavors are quite different,
yet all are pleasing to the mouth.

故禮義法度者
應時而變者也
Rituals and regulations
are something that change in response to the times.

今取猨狙而衣以周公之服
彼必齕齧挽裂
盡去而後慊
If you take a monkey and dress him in the robes of the Duke of Zhou,
he will bite and tear at them,
not satisfied until he has divested himself of every stitch.

觀古今之異
猶猨狙之異乎周公也
And a glance will show that past and present are no more alike
than are a monkey and the Duke of Zhou!

***

故西施病心而矉其里
"The beautiful Xishi, troubled with heartburn, frowned at her neighbors.

其里之醜人見而美之
歸亦捧心而矉其里
An ugly woman of the neighborhood, seeing that Xishi was beautiful,
went home and likewise pounded her breast and frowned at her neighbors.

其里之富人見之
堅閉門而不出
貧人見之
挈妻子而去之走
But at the sight of her, the rich men of the neighborhood
shut tight their gates and would not venture out,
while the poor men
grabbed their wives and children by the hand and scampered off.

彼知矉美
而不知矉之所以美
The woman understood that someone frowning could be beautiful,
but she did not understand where the beauty of the frown came from.

惜乎
A pity, indeed!

而夫子其窮哉
Your master is going to end up in trouble!"

---

孔子行年五十有一而不聞道
Confucius had gone along until he was fifty-one and had still not heard the Way.

乃南之沛
見老聃
Finally he went south to Pei
and called on Lao Dan.

老聃曰子來乎
"Ah, you have come," said Lao Dan.

吾聞子北方之賢者也
"I've heard that you are a worthy man of the northern region.

子亦得道乎
Have you found the Way?"

孔子曰未得也
"Not yet," said Confucius.

老子曰子惡乎求之哉
"Where did you look for it?" asked Lao Dan.

***

曰吾求之於度數
五年而未得也
"I looked for it in rules and regulations,
but five years went by and I still hadn't found it."

老子曰子又惡乎求之哉
"Where else did you look for it?" asked Lao Dan.

***

曰吾求之於陰陽
十有二年而未得
"I looked for it in the yin and yang,
but twelve years went by and I still hadn't found it."

***

老子曰然
"It stands to reason!" said Lao Dan.

使道而可獻
則人莫不獻之於其君
"If the Way could be presented,
there is no man who would not present it to his ruler.

使道而可進
則人莫不進之於其親
If the Way could be offered,
there is no man who would not offer it to his parents.

使道而可以告人
則人莫不告其兄弟
If the Way could be reported,
there is no man who would not report it to his brothers.

使道而可以與人
則人莫不與其子孫
If the Way could be bequeathed,
there is no man who would not bequeath it to his heirs.

然而不可者
But it cannot---
無佗也
and for none other than the following reason:

中無主而不止
If there is no host on the inside to receive it, it will not stay;
外無正而不行
if there is no mark on the outside to guide it, it will not go.

由中出者不受於外
聖人不出
If what is brought forth from the inside is not received on the outside,
then the sage will not bring it forth.

由外入者無主於中
聖人不隱
If what is taken in from the outside is not received by a host on the inside,
the sage will not entrust it.[^14-16]

[^14-16]: There are other ways to interpret this perplexing passage.
The point is that the Way can be transmitted only telepathically,
and therefore the sage must make certain that the mind of the other party
is capable of receiving it before he extracts it from his own mind and hands it over.

***

名公器也
"Fame is a public weapon---
不可多取
don't reach for it too often.

仁義先王之蘧廬也
Benevolence and righteousness are the grass huts of the former kings;
止可以一宿
而不可以久處
you may stop in them for one night,
but you mustn't tarry there for long.

覯而多責
A lengthy stay would invite many reproaches.

古之至人假道於仁
託宿於義
The Perfect Man of ancient times used benevolence as a path to be borrowed,
righteousness as a lodge to take shelter in.

以遊逍遙之虛
食於苟簡之田
立於不貸之圃
He wandered in the free and easy wastes,
ate in the plain and simple fields,
and strolled in the garden of no bestowal.

逍遙無為也
Free and easy, he rested in inaction;
苟簡易養也
plain and simple, it was not hard for him to live;
不貸無出也
bestowing nothing, he did not have to hand things out.

古者謂是采真之遊
The men of old called this the wandering of the Truth-Picker.

***

以富為是者不能讓祿
"He who considers wealth a good thing can never bear to give up his income;
以顯為是者不能讓名
he who considers eminence a good thing can never bear to give up his fame.

親權者不能與人柄
He who has a taste for power can never bear to hand over authority to others.

操之則慄
Holding tight to these things, such men shiver with fear;
舍之則悲
should they let them go, they would pine in sorrow.

而一無所鑒
以闚其所不休者
They never stop for a moment of reflection,
never cease to gaze with greedy eyes---
是天之戮民也
they are men punished by Heaven.

怨恩
取與
諫教
生殺
Resentment and kindness,
taking away and giving,
reproof and instruction,
life and death---
八者正之器也
these eight things are the weapons of the corrector.[^14-17]

[^14-17]: On one level, this refers to the ruler, who rules by means of punishments, rewards, etc.;
*zheng* (to correct) is etymologically the same as *zheng* (to govern).
On another level, the passage is talking about the enlightened man who has a "correct" understanding of the Way.

唯循大變無所湮者
為能用之
Only he who complies with the Great Change and allows no blockage
will be able to use them.

故曰正者正也
Therefore it is said, The corrector must be correct.

其心以為不然者
天門弗開矣
If the mind cannot accept this fact,
then the doors of Heaven will never open!"

---

孔子見老聃而語仁義
Confucius called on Lao Dan and spoke to him about benevolence and righteousness.

老聃曰夫播穅眯目
則天地四方易位矣
Lao Dan said, "Chaff from the winnowing fan can so blind the eye
that heaven, earth, and the four directions all seem to shift place.

蚊虻噆膚
則通昔不寐矣
A mosquito or a horsefly stinging your skin
can keep you awake a whole night.

夫仁義憯然
乃憤吾心
亂莫大焉
And when benevolence and righteousness in all their fearfulness
come to muddle the mind,[^14-18]
the confusion is unimaginable.

[^14-18]: Following texts that read *kui* in place of *fen*.

吾子使天下無失其朴
吾子亦放風而動
總德而立矣
If you want to keep the world from losing its simplicity,
you must move with the freedom of the wind,
stand in the perfection of Virtue.

又奚傑然
若負建鼓而求亡子者邪
Why all this huffing and puffing,
as though you were carrying a big drum and searching for a lost child!

夫鵠不日浴而白
The snow goose needs no daily bath to stay white;
烏不日黔而黑
the crow needs no daily inking to stay black.

黑白之朴不足以為辯
Black and white in their simplicity offer no ground for argument;
名譽之觀不足以為廣
fame and reputation in their clamorousness[^14-19] offer no ground for envy.

[^14-19]: Following texts that read *huan* in place of *guan*.

泉涸魚相與處於陸
相呴以溼相濡以沫
When the springs dry up and the fish are left stranded on the ground,
they spew one another with moisture and wet one another down with spit---
不若相忘於江湖
but it would be much better if they could forget one another in the rivers and lakes!"

***

孔子見老聃歸
三日不談
When Confucius returned from his visit with Lao Dan, 
he did not speak for three days.

弟子問曰夫子見老聃
His disciples said, "Master, you've seen Lao Dan---
亦將何歸哉
what estimation would you make of him?"

***

孔子曰吾乃今於是乎見龍
Confucius said, "At last I may say that I have seen a dragon---
龍合而成體
散而成章
乘乎雲氣而養乎陰陽
a dragon that coils to show his body at its best,
that sprawls out to display his patterns at their best,
riding on the breath of the clouds, feeding on the yin and yang.

予口張而不能嗋
My mouth fell open and I couldn't close it;
予又何規老聃哉
my tongue flew up and I couldn't even stammer.

How could I possibly make any estimation of Lao Dan!"

***

子貢曰然則人固有尸居而龍見
雷聲而淵默
Zigong said, "Then is it true that the Perfect Man can command corpse-like stillness and dragon vision,
the voice of thunder and the silence of deep pools;
發動如天地者乎
that he breaks forth into movement like Heaven and earth?

賜亦可得而觀乎
If only I, too, could get to see him!"

***

遂以孔子聲見老聃
In the end,  he went with an introduction from Confucius and called on Lao Dan.

老聃方將倨堂
Lao Dan was about to sit down in the hall and stretch out his legs.

而應微曰予年運而往矣
In a small voice he said, "I've lived to see a great many years come and go.

子將何以戒我乎
What advice is it you have for me?"

***

子貢曰夫三王五帝之治天下不同
其係聲名一也
Zigong said, "The Three August Ones and the Five Emperors ruled the world in ways that were not the same,
though they were alike in the praise and acclaim they won.

而先生獨以為非聖人
I am told, sir, that you alone do not regard them as sages.

如何哉
May I ask why?"

***

老聃曰小子少進
Lao Dan said, "Young man, come a little closer!

子何以謂不同
Why do you say that they ruled in ways that were not the same?"

***

對曰堯授舜
舜授禹
"Yao ceded the throne to Shun,
and Shun ceded it to Yu.

禹用力而湯用兵
Yu wore himself out over it, and Tang even resorted to war.

文王順紂而不敢逆
King Wen obeyed Zhou and did not dare to rebel;
武王逆紂而不肯順
but his son King Wu turned against Zhou and refused to remain loyal.

故曰不同
Therefore I say that they were not the same."

***

老聃曰小子少進
余語汝三皇五帝之治天下
Lao Dan said, "Young man, come a little closer,
and I will tell you how the Three August Ones and the Five Emperors ruled the world.

黃帝之治天下
使民心一
In ancient times the Yellow Emperor ruled the world
by making the hearts of the people one.

民有其親死不哭
而民不非也
Therefore, if there were those among the people who did not wail at the death of their parents,
the people saw nothing wrong in this.

堯之治天下
使民心親
Yao ruled the world
by making the hearts of the people affectionate.

民有為其親殺其殺
而民不非也
Therefore, if there were those among the people who decided to mourn for longer or shorter periods according to the degree of kinship of the deceased,
the people saw nothing wrong in this.

舜之治天下
使民心競
Shun ruled the world
by making the hearts of the people rivalrous.

民孕婦十月生子
子生五月而能言
不至乎孩而始誰
Therefore the wives of the people became pregnant and gave birth in the tenth month as in the past,
but their children were not five months old before they were able to talk,
and their baby laughter had hardly rung out before they had begun to distinguish one person from another.

則人始有夭矣
It was then that premature death first appeared.

禹之治天下
使民心變
Yu ruled the world
by causing the hearts of the people to change.

人有心
而兵有順
It was assumed that each man had a heart of his own,
that recourse to arms was quite all right.

殺盜非殺
Killing a thief is not a case of murder, they said;
人自為種而天下耳
every man in the world should look out for his own kind.

是以天下大駭
儒墨皆起
其作始有倫
As a result, there was great consternation in the world,
and the Confucians and Mohists all came forward,
creating for the first time the rules of ethical behavior.

而今乎婦女何言哉
But what would they say about those men who nowadays make wives of their daughters?[^14-20]

[^14-20]: The sentence is obscure.
It is apparent from Guo Xiang's note that he took it as a reference to incest,
although later commentators,
repelled or unconvinced by his interpretation,
have suggested various other interpretations or emendations.

***

余語汝三皇五帝之治天下
"I will tell you how the Three August Ones and the Five Emperors ruled the world!

名曰治之
而亂莫甚焉
They called it 'ruling,'
but in fact they were plunging it into the worst confusion.

三皇之知上悖日月之明
下睽山川之精
中墮四時之施
The 'wisdom' of the Three August Ones was such as blotted out the brightness of sun and moon above,
sapped the vigor of hills and streams below,
and overturned the round of the four seasons in between.

其知憯於蠣蠆之尾
Their wisdom was more fearsome than the tail of the scorpion;
鮮規之獸
莫得安其性命之情者
down to the smallest beast,
not a living thing was allowed to rest in the true form of its nature and fate.

而猶自以為聖人
And yet they considered themselves sages!

不可恥乎
Was it not shameful---
其無恥也
their lack of shame!"

***

子貢蹴蹴然立不安
Zigong, stunned and speechless, stood wondering which way to turn.

---

孔子謂老聃曰丘治
Confucius said to Lao Dan, "I have been studying the Six Classics---
詩書
禮樂
易春秋六經
自以為久矣
孰知其故矣
the *Odes*, the *Documents*,
the *Ritual*, the *Music*,
the *Changes*, and the *Spring and Autumn*,
for what I would call a long time,
and I know their contents through and through.

以奸者七十二君
論先王之道而明周召之跡
一君無所鉤用
But I have been around to seventy-two different rulers with them,
expounding the ways of the former kings and making clear the path trod by the dukes of Zhou and Shao,
and yet not a single ruler has found anything to excite his interest.

甚矣夫

人之難說也
道之難明邪
How difficult it is to persuade others,
how difficult to make clear the Way!"

***

老子曰幸矣子之不遇治世之君也
Laozi said, "It's lucky you didn't meet with a ruler who would try to govern the world as you say.

夫六經先王之陳跡也
The Six Classics are the old worn-out paths of the former kings---
豈其所以跡哉
they are not the thing that walked the path.

今子之所言猶迹也
What you are expounding are simply these paths.

夫迹履之所出
Paths are made by shoes that walk them;
而迹豈履哉
they are by no means the shoes themselves!

***

夫白鶂之相視
眸子不運而風化
"The white fish hawk has only to stare unblinking at its mate
for fertilization to occur.

蟲雄鳴於上風
雌應於下風而風化
With insects, the male cries on the wind above,
the female cries on the wind below, and there is fertilization.

類自為雌雄
故風化
The creature called the *lei* is both male and female,
and so it can fertilize itself.

性不可易
命不可變
時不可止
道不可壅
Inborn nature cannot be changed,
fate cannot be altered,
time cannot be stopped,
the Way cannot be obstructed.

苟得其道無自而不可
Get hold of the Way and there's nothing that can't be done;
失焉者無自而可
lose it and there's nothing that can be done."

***

孔子不出三月復見
Confucius stayed home for three months and then went to see Lao Dan once again.

曰丘得之矣
"I've got it," he said.

烏鵲孺
"The magpie hatches its young;
魚傅沫
the fish spit out their milt;
細要者化
the slim-waisted wasp has its stages of transformation;
有弟而兄啼
and when baby brother is born, big brother howls.[^14-21]

[^14-21]: That is, the older child is weaned when the younger is born;
the phrase signifies mammalian birth,
as opposed to the other three types of reproduction mentioned earlier.

久矣夫
丘不與化為人
For a long time now,
I have not been taking my place as a man along with the process of change.

不與化為人
安能化人
And if I do not take my own place as a man along with the process of change,
how can I hope to change other men?"

***

老子曰可丘
Laozi said, "Good, Qiu---
得之矣
now you've got it!"
