## 15 刻意 Constrained in Will

刻意尚行
離世異俗
高論怨誹
為亢而已矣
To be constrained in will, lofty in action,
aloof from the world, apart from its customs,
elevated in discourse, sullen and critical,
indignation his whole concern---
此山谷之士
非世之人
枯槁赴淵者之所好也
such is the life favored by the scholar in his mountain valley,
the man who condemns the world,
the worn and haggard one who means to end it all with a plunge into the deep.

語仁義忠信
恭儉推讓
為修而已矣
To discourse on benevolence, righteousness, loyalty, and good faith,
to be courteous, temperate, modest, and deferential,
moral training his whole concern---
此平世之士
教誨之人
遊居學者之所好也
such is the life favored by the scholar who seeks to bring the world to order,
the man who teaches and instructs,
who at home and abroad lives for learning.

語大功
立大名
禮君臣
正上下
為治而已矣
To talk of great accomplishments,
win a great name,
define the etiquette of ruler and subject,
regulate the position of superior and inferior,
the ordering of the state his only concern---
此朝廷之士
尊主強國之人
致功并兼者之所好也
such is the life favored by the scholar of court and council,
the man who would honor his sovereign and strengthen his country,
the bringer of accomplishment,
the annexer of territory.

就藪澤
處閒曠
釣魚閒處
無為而已矣
To repair to the thickets and ponds,
living idly in the wilderness,
angling for fish in solitary places,
inaction his only concern---
此江海之士
避世之人
閒暇者之所好也
such is the life favored by the scholar of the rivers and seas,
the man who withdraws from the world,
the unhurried idler.

吹呴呼吸
吐故納新
熊經鳥申
為壽而已矣
To pant, to puff, to hail, to sip,
to spit out the old breath and draw in the new,
practicing bear-hangings and bird-stretchings,
longevity his only concern---
此道引之士
養形之人
彭祖壽考者之所好也
such is the life favored by the scholar who practices Induction,
the man who nourishes his body,
who hopes to live to be as old as Pengcu.[^15-1]

[^15-1]: In this last sentence, which describes the practitioner of Induction (*daoyin*),
a kind of yoga technique involving exercises and breath control.
I follow Waley's translations of technical terms such as "bear-hangings" and "bird-stretchings,"
whose meaning can only be guessed. See Waley's _Three Ways of Thought in Ancient China_, p. 44.

***

若夫不刻意而高
But to attain loftiness without constraining the will;
無仁義而修
無功名而治
無江海而閒
不道引而壽
to achieve moral training without benevolence and righteousness,
good order without accomplishments and fame,
leisure without rivers and seas,
long life without Induction;
無不忘也
無不有也
澹然無極而眾美從之
to lose everything
and yet possess everything,
at ease in the illimitable, where all good things come to attend---
此天地之道
聖人之德也
this is the Way of Heaven and earth,
the Virtue of the sage.

故曰夫恬惔寂寞
虛無無為
So it is said, Limpidity, silence,
emptiness, inaction---
此天地之平
而道德之質也
these are the level of Heaven and earth,
the substance of the Way and its Virtue.

故曰聖人休
So it is said, The sage rests;
休焉則平易矣
平易則恬惔矣
平易恬惔
則憂患不能入
邪氣不能襲
with rest comes peaceful ease,
with peaceful ease comes limpidity,
and where there is ease and limpidity,
care and worry cannot get at him,
noxious airs cannot assault him.

故其德全而神不虧
Therefore his Virtue is complete and his spirit unimpaired.

***

故曰聖人之生也天行
其死也物化
So it is said, With the sage, his life is the working of Heaven,
his death the transformation of things.

靜而與陰同德
In stillness, he and the yin share a single Virtue;
動而與陽同波
in motion, he and the yang share a single flow.

不為福先
不為禍始
He is not the bearer of good fortune
or the initiator of bad fortune.

感而後應
Roused by something outside himself, only then does he respond;
迫而後動
pressed, only then does he move;
不得已而後起
finding he has no choice, only then does he rise up.

去知與故
循天之理
He discards knowledge and purpose
and follows along with the reasonableness of Heaven.

故無天災
無物累
無人非
無鬼責
Therefore he incurs no disaster from Heaven,
no entanglement from things,
no opposition from man,
no blame from the spirits.

其生若浮
其死若休
His life is a floating,
his death a rest.

不思慮
不豫謀
He does not ponder or scheme,
does not plot for the future.

光矣而不耀
A man of light, he does not shine;
信矣而不期
of good faith, he keeps no promises.

其寢不夢
其覺無憂
He sleeps without dreaming,
wakes without worry.

其神純粹
其魂不罷
His spirit is pure and clean,
his soul never wearied.

虛無恬惔
乃合天德
In emptiness, nonbeing, and limpidity,
he joins with the Virtue of Heaven.

***

故曰悲樂者德之邪
So it is said, Grief and happiness are perversions of Virtue;
喜怒者道之過
joy and anger are transgressions of the Way;
好惡者德之失
love and hate are offenses against Virtue.

故心不憂樂
德之至也
When the mind is without care or joy,
this is the height of Virtue.

一而不變
靜之至也
When it is unified and unchanging,
this is the height of stillness.

無所於忤
虛之至也
When it grates against nothing,
this is the height of emptiness.

不與物交
惔之至也
When it has no commerce with things,
this is the height of limpidity.

無所於逆
粹之至也
When it rebels against nothing,
this is the height of purity.

***

故曰形勞而不休則弊
So it is said, If the body is made to labor and take no rest, it will wear out;
精用而不已則勞
勞則竭
if the spiritual essence is taxed without cessation, it will grow weary,
and weariness will bring exhaustion.

水之性不雜則清
莫動則平
It is the nature of waterthat if it is not mixed with other things, it will be clear,
and if nothing stirs it, it will be level.

鬱閉而不流
亦不能清
But if it is dammed and hemmed in and not allowed to flow,
then it, too, will cease to be clear.

天德之象也
As such, it is a symbol of Heavenly Virtue.

故曰純粹而不雜
So it is said, To be pure, clean, and mixed with nothing;
靜一而不變
still, unified, and unchanging;
惔而無為
limpid and inactive;
動而以天行
moving with the workings of Heaven---
此養神之道也
this is the way to care for the spirit.

***

夫有干越之劍者
柙而藏之
不敢用也
寶之至也
The man who owns a sword from Gan or Yue
lays it in a box and stores it away,
not daring to use it,
for to him it is the greatest of treasures.

精神四達
並流
Pure spirit reaches in the four directions,
flows now this way, now that---
無所不極
there is no place it does not extend to.

上際於天
Above, it brushes Heaven;
下蟠於地
below, it coils on the earth.

化育萬物
不可為象
It transforms and nurses the ten thousand things,
but no one can make out its form.

其名為同帝
Its name is called One-with-Heaven.

純素之道
惟神是守
The way to purity and whiteness
is to guard the spirit, this alone;
守而勿失
與神為一
一之精通
合於天倫
guard it and never lose it,
and you will become one with spirit,
one with its pure essence,
which communicates and mingles with the Heavenly Order.[^15-2]

[^15-2]: The word *jing* is being used in this passage in a number of different ways,
a fact that is very difficult to bring out in translation.
At the beginning of the paragraph, *jing*,
translated as "spiritual essence," means the vital energy of the body;
later the word appears as an adjective in the compound "pure spirit" (*jingshen*),
that is, vital or essential spirit.
Finally, it appears as a noun, "essence," or "purity."
Because it may also mean "semen," the passage can be interpreted as dealing with the sexual regimen.

野語有之曰眾人重利
廉士重名
賢人尚志
聖人貴精
The common saying has it, "The ordinary man prizes gain,
the man of integrity prizes name,
the worthy man honors ambition,
the sage values spiritual essence."

故素也者謂其無所與雜也
Whiteness means there is nothing mixed in;
純也者謂其不虧其神也
purity means the spirit is never impaired.

能體純素
謂之真人
He who can embody purity and whiteness
may be called the True Man.
