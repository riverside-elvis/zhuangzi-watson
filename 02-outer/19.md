## 19 達生 Mastering life

### Abandoning the world and forgetting life

達生之情者
He who has mastered the true nature of life
不務生之所無以為
does not labor over what life cannot do.

達命之情者
He who has mastered the true nature of fate
不務知之所無奈何
does not labor over what knowledge cannot change.

養形
He who wants to nourish his body
必先之以物
must, first of all, turn to things.

物有餘
And yet it is possible to have more than enough things
而形不養者有之矣
and for the body still to go un-nourished.

有生
He who has life
必先無離形
must, first of all, see to it that it does not leave the body.

形不離
And yet it is possible for life never to leave the body
而生亡者有之矣
and still fail to be preserved.

生之來不能卻
The coming of life cannot be fended off;
其去不能止
its departure cannot be stopped.

悲夫世之人
How pitiful the men of the world,
以為養形足以存生
who think that simply nourishing the body is enough to preserve life!

而養形果不足以存生[^rse-19-1]
[And yet if simply nourishing the body isn't enough to preserve life---]
則世奚足為哉
Then why is what the world does worth doing?
雖不足為
and yet it cannot be left undone---
而不可不為者
It may not be worth doing,
其為不免矣
this is unavoidable.

[^rse-19-1]: Present in ctext and Liu, but not translated by Watson. --RSE

***

夫欲免為形者
He who wants to avoid doing anything for his body
莫如棄世
had best abandon the world.

棄世
By abandoning the world,
則無累
he can be without entanglements.

無累
Being without entanglements,
則正平
he can be upright and calm.

正平
Being upright and calm,
則與彼更生
he can be born again with others.

更生
Being born again,
則幾矣
he can come close [to the Way].

***

事奚足棄
But why is abandoning the affairs of the world worthwhile,
而生奚足遺
and why is forgetting life worthwhile?

棄事
If you abandon the affairs of the world,
則形不勞
your body will be without toil.

遺生
If you forget life,
則精不虧
your vitality will be unimpaired.

夫形全精復
With your body complete and your vitality made whole again,
與天為一
you may become one with Heaven.

天地者萬物之父母也
Heaven and earth are the father and mother of the ten thousand things.

合則成體
They join to become a body;
散則成始
they part to become a beginning.

形精不虧
When the body and vitality are without flaw,
是謂能移
this is called being able to shift.

精而又精
Vitality added to vitality,
反以相天
you return to become the Helper of Heaven.

### Barrier Keeper Yin tells Master Liezi how to stay whole (the drunken man)

子列子問關尹曰
Master Liezi said to the Barrier Keeper Yin,
至人潛行不窒
"The Perfect Man can walk under water without choking,
蹈火不熱
can tread on fire without being burned,
行乎萬物之上而不慄
and can travel above the ten thousand things without being frightened.

請問何以至於此
May I ask how he manages this?"

***

關尹曰
The Barrier Keeper Yin replied,
是純氣之守也
"This is because he guards the pure breath---
非知巧果敢之列
it has nothing to do with wisdom, skill, determination, or courage.

居吾語女
Sit down and I will tell you about it.

凡有貌象聲色者
All that have faces, forms, voices, colors---
皆物也
these are all mere things.

物與物何以相遠
How could one thing and another thing be far removed from each other?

夫奚足以至乎先
And how could any of them be worth considering as a predecessor?

是形[^rse-19-2]色而已
They are forms, colors---nothing more.

[^rse-19-2]: 形 is in Liu and translated by Wastson, but is missing from ctext. --RSE

則物之造乎不形
But things have their creation in what has no form,
而止乎無所化
and their conclusion in what has no change.

夫得是而窮之者
If a man can get hold of *this* and exhaust it fully,
物焉得而止焉
then how can things stand in his way?

彼將處乎不淫之度
He may rest within the bounds that know no excess,
而藏乎無端之紀
hide within the borders that know no source,
遊乎萬物之所終始
wander where the ten thousand things have their end and beginning,
壹其性
unify his nature,
養其氣
nourish his breath,
合其德
unite his virtue,
以通乎物之所造
and thereby communicate with that which creates all things.

夫若是者其天守全
A man like this guards what belongs to Heaven and keeps it whole.

其神無郤
His spirit has no flaw,
物奚自入焉
so how can things enter in and get at him?

***

夫醉者之墜車
"When a drunken man falls from a carriage,
雖疾不死
though the carriage may be going very fast, he won't be killed.

骨節與人同
He has bones and joints the same as other men,
而犯害與人異
and yet he is not injured as they would be,
其神全也
because his spirit is whole.

乘亦不知也
He didn't know he was riding,
墜亦不知也
and he doesn't know he has fallen out.

死生
Life and death,
驚懼
alarm and terror,
不入乎其胷中
do not enter his breast,
是故遻物而不慴
and so he can bang against things without fear of injury.

彼得全於酒而猶若是
If he can keep himself whole like this by means of wine,
而況得全於天乎
how much more can he keep himself whole by means of Heaven!

聖人藏於天
The sage hides himself in Heaven---
故莫之能傷也
hence there is nothing that can do him harm.

***

復讎者不折鏌干
"A man seeking revenge does not go so far as to smash the sword of his enemy;
雖有忮心者不怨飄瓦
a man, no matter how hot tempered, does not rail at the tile that happens to fall on him.

是以天下平均
To know that all things in the world are equal and the same---
故無攻戰之亂
無殺戮之刑者
由此道也
this is the only way
to eliminate the chaos of attack and battle
and the harshness of punishment and execution!

***

不開人之天
"Do not try to develop what is natural to man;
而開天之天
develop what is natural to Heaven.

開天者德生
He who develops Heaven benefits life;
開人者賊生
he who develops man injures life.

不厭其天
Do not reject what is of Heaven,
不忽於人
do not neglect what is of man,
民幾乎以其真
and the people will be close to the attainment of Truth."[^19-1]

[^19-1]: I follow the text as it stands,
though it would perhaps be preferable to adopt Ma Xulun's suggestion,
dropping the *min* and translating "and you will be close to the attainment of Truth."

### Confucius questions the cicada catcher

仲尼適楚
When Confucius was on his way to Chu,
出於林中見痀僂者承蜩猶掇之也
he passed through a forest where he saw a hunchback catching cicadas with a sticky pole as easily as though he were grabbing them with his hand.

***

仲尼曰
Confucius said,
子巧乎
"What skill you have!

有道邪
Is there a special way to this?"

***

曰我有道也
"I have a way," said the hunchback.

五六月累丸二
"For the first five or six months, I practice balancing two balls on top of each other on the end of the pole,
而不墜
and if they don't fall off,
則失者錙銖
I know I will lose very few cicadas.

累三
Then I balance three balls,
而不墜
and if they don't fall off,
則失者十一
I know I'll lose only one cicada in ten.

累五
Then I balance five balls,
而不墜
and if they don't fall off,
猶掇之也
I know it will be as easy as grabbing them with my hand.

吾處身也若厥株拘
I hold my body like a stiff tree trunk
吾執臂也若槁木之枝
and use my arm like an old dry limb.

雖天地之大
No matter how huge heaven and earth
萬物之多
or how numerous the ten thousand things,
而唯蜩翼之知
I'm aware of nothing but cicada wings.

吾不反不側
Not wavering, not tipping,
不以萬物易蜩之翼
not letting any of the other ten thousand things take the place of those cicada wings---
何為而不得
how can I help but succeed?"

***

孔子顧謂弟子曰
Confucius turned to his disciples and said,
用志不分乃凝於神
"He keeps his will undivided and concentrates his spirit---
其痀僂丈人之謂乎
that would serve to describe our hunchback gentleman here, would it not?"

### Confucius tells Yan Yuan about swimming under water and betting on archery

顏淵問仲尼曰
Yan Yuan said to Confucius,
吾嘗濟乎觴深之淵
"I once crossed the gulf at Goblet Deeps,
津人操舟若神
and the ferryman handled the boat with supernatural skill.

吾問焉曰
I asked him,
操舟可學邪
'Can a person learn how to handle a boat?'
曰
and he replied,
可
'Certainly.

善游者數能
A good swimmer will get the knack of it in no time.

若乃夫沒人
And if a man can swim under water,
則未嘗見舟
he may never have seen a boat before,
而便操之也
and still he'll know how to handle it!'

吾問焉而不吾告
I asked him what he meant by that, but he wouldn't tell me.

敢問何謂也
May I venture to ask you what it means?"

***

仲尼曰
Confucius said,
善游者數能
"A good swimmer will get the knack of it in no time---
忘水也
that means he's forgotten the water.

若乃夫沒人
If a man can swim under water,
之未嘗見舟
he may never have seen a boat before,
而便操之也
and still he'll know how to handle it---
彼視淵若陵
that's because he sees the water as so much dry land
視舟之覆猶其車卻也
and regards the capsizing of a boat as he would the overturning of a cart.

覆卻萬方陳乎前
The ten thousand things[^19-2] all may be capsizing and turning over at the same time right in front of him,
而不得入其舍
and it can't get at him and affect what's inside---
惡往而不暇
so where could he go and not be at ease?

[^19-2]: Following the interpretation of Yu Yue, who supplies a *wu* after the *wan*.

***

以瓦注者巧
"When you're betting for tiles in an archery contest, you shoot with skill.

以鉤注者憚
When you're betting for fancy belt buckles, you worry about your aim.

以黃金注者殙
And when you're betting for real gold, you're a nervous wreck.

其巧一也
Your skill is the same in all three cases---
而有所矜
but because one prize means more to you than another,
則重外也
you let outside considerations weigh on your mind.

凡外重者內拙
He who looks too hard at the outside gets clumsy on the inside."

### Tian Kaizhi tells Duke Wei of Zhou about lashing stragglers (Confucius' three rules)

田開之見周威公
Tian Kaizhi went to see Duke Wei of Zhou.

威公曰
Duke Wei said,
吾聞祝腎學生
"I hear that Zhu Xian is studying how to live.

吾子與祝腎游
You are a friend of his---
亦何聞焉
what have you heard from him on the subject?"

***

田開之曰
Tian Kaizhi said,
開之操拔篲以倚門庭
"I merely wield a broom and tend his gate and garden---
亦何聞於夫子
how should I have heard anything from the Master?"

***

威公曰
Duke Wei said,
田子無讓
"Don't be modest, Master Tian.

寡人願聞之
I am anxious to hear about it."

***

開之曰
Tian Kaizhi said,
聞之夫子曰
"I have heard the Master say,
善養生者若牧羊然
'He who is good at nourishing life is like a herder of sheep---
視其後者而鞭之
he watches for stragglers and whips them up.'"

威公曰何謂也
"What does that mean?" asked Duke Wei.

***

田開之曰
Tian Kaizhi said,
魯有單豹者
"In Lu there was Shan Bao---
巖居而水飲
he lived among the cliffs, drank only water,
不與民共利
and didn't go after gain like other people.

行年七十
He went along like that for seventy years
而猶有嬰兒之色
and still had the complexion of a little child.

不幸遇餓虎
Unfortunately, he met a hungry tiger
餓虎殺而食之
who killed him and ate him up.

有張毅者
Then there was Zhang Yi---
高門懸薄無不走也
there wasn't one of the great families and fancy mansions that he didn't rush off to visit.

行年四十
He went along like that for forty years,
而有內熱之病以死
and then he developed an internal fever, fell ill, and died.

豹養其內
Shan Bao looked after what was on the inside
而虎食其外
and the tiger ate up his outside.

毅養其外
Zhang Yi looked after what was on the outside
而病攻其內
and the sickness attacked him from the inside.

此二子者皆不鞭其後者也
Both these men failed to give a lash to the stragglers.[^19-3]

[^19-3]: That is, stick to a happy medium.

***

仲尼曰
"Confucius has said,
無入而藏
'Don't go in and hide;
無出而陽
don't come out and shine;
柴立其中央
stand stock-still in the middle.'

三者若得其名必極
He who can follow these three rules is sure to be called the finest.

夫畏塗者
When people are worried about the safety of the roads,
十殺一人
if they hear that one traveler in a party of ten has been murdered,
則父子兄弟相戒也
then fathers and sons, elder and younger brothers, will warn one another
必盛卒徒而後敢出焉
to be careful and will not venture out until they have a large escort of armed men.

不亦知乎
That's wise of them, isn't it?

人之所取畏者
But when it comes to what people really ought to be worried about---
衽席之上
the time when they are lying in bed
飲食之間
or sitting around eating and drinking---
而不知為之戒者
then they don't have sense enough to take warning.

過也
That's a mistake!"

### The Invocator of the Ancestors questions a pig

祝宗人
The Invocator of the Ancestors,
玄端
dressed in his black, square-cut robes,
以臨牢筴說彘曰
peered into the pigpen and said,
汝奚惡死
"Why should you object to dying?

吾將三月豢汝
I'm going to fatten you for three months,
十日戒
practice austerities for ten days,
三日齊
fast for three days,
藉白茅
spread the white rushes,
加汝肩尻乎彫俎之上
and lay your shoulders and rump on the carved sacrificial stand---
則汝為之乎
you'll go along with that, won't you?

為彘謀
True, if I were planning things from the point of view of a pig,
曰不如食以糠糟
I'd say it would be better to eat chaff and bran
而錯之牢筴之中
and stay right there in the pen.

自為謀
But if I were planning for myself,
則苟生有軒冕之尊
I'd say that if I could be honored as a high official while I lived
死得於腞楯之上聚僂之中
and get to ride in a fine hearse and lie among the feathers and trappings when I died,
則為之
I'd go along with that.

為彘謀則去之
Speaking for the pig, I'd give such a life a flat refusal,
自為謀則取之
but speaking for myself, I'd certainly accept.

所異彘者何也
I wonder why I look at things differently from a pig?"

### Duke Huan goes hunting in the marsh and sees a ghost

桓公田於澤
Duke Huan was hunting in a marsh,
管仲御
with Guan Zhong as his carriage driver,
見鬼焉
when he saw a ghost.

公撫管仲之手曰
The duke grasped Guan Zhong's hand and said,
仲父何見
"Father Zhong, what do you see?"[^19-4]

[^19-4]: Duke Huan of Qi (r. 685–643 BCE) later became the first of the *ba*---
dictators or hegemons who imposed their will on the other feudal lords.
Guan Zhong (d. 645 BCE) was his chief minister.
As a special mark of esteem, the duke customarily addressed him as "Father Zhong."

對曰臣無所見
"I don't see anything," replied Guan Zhong.

***

公反誒詒為病
When the duke returned home, he fell into a stupor, grew ill,
數日不出
and for several days did not go out.

***

齊士有皇子告敖者曰
A gentleman of Qi named Huangzi Gaoao said,
公則自傷
"Your Grace, you are doing this injury to yourself!

鬼惡能傷公
How could a ghost have the power to injure you!

夫忿滀之氣散而不反
If the vital breath that is stored up in a man becomes dispersed and does not return,
則為不足
then he suffers a deficiency.

上而不下
If it ascends and fails to descend again,
則使人善怒
it causes him to be chronically irritable.

下而不上
If it descends and does not ascend again,
則使人善忘
it causes him to be chronically forgetful.

不上不下中身當心
And if it neither ascends nor descends but gathers in the middle of the body in the region of the heart,
則為病
then he becomes ill."

***

桓公曰
Duke Huan said,
然則有鬼乎
"But do ghosts really exist?"

***

曰有
"Indeed they do.

沈有履
There is the Li on the hearth[^19-5]
灶有髻
and the Ji in the stove.

[^19-5]: Following Yu Yue's emendation and interpretation.

戶內之煩壤雷霆處之
The heap of clutter and trash just inside the gate is where the Leiting lives.

東北方之下者倍阿鮭蠪躍之
In the northeast corner the Beia and Guilong leap about,
西北方之下者則泆陽處之
and the northwest corner is where the Yiyang lives.

水有罔象
In the water is the Gangxiang;
山有夔
in the mountains, the Kui;[^19-6]
野有彷徨
in the meadows, the Panghuang;
澤有委蛇
and in the marshes, the Weituo."

[^19-6]: The one-legged creature who appeared on p. 133.

***

公曰
The duke said,
請問委蛇之狀何如
"May I ask what a Weituo looks like?"

***

皇子曰委蛇其大如轂
Huangzi said, "The Weituo is as big as a wheel hub,
其長如轅
as tall as a carriage shaft,
紫衣而朱冠
has a purple robe and a vermilion hat,
其為物也惡
and, as creatures go, is very ugly.

聞雷車之聲
When it hears the sound of thunder or a carriage,
則捧其首而立
it grabs its head and stands up.

見之者殆乎霸
Anyone who sees it will soon become a dictator."

***

桓公囅然而笑曰
Duke Huan's face lit up, and he said with a laugh,
此寡人之所見者也
"*That* must have been what I saw!"

於是正衣冠與之坐
Then he straightened his robe and hat and sat up on the mat with Huangzi,
不終日而不知病之去也
and before the day was over, though he didn't notice it, his illness went away.

### Ji Xingzi trains gamecocks for the king

紀渻子為王養鬥雞
Ji Xingzi was training gamecocks for the king.

十日而問雞已乎
After ten days, the king asked if they were ready.

***

曰未也
"Not yet.

方虛憍而恃氣
They're too haughty and rely on their nerve."

日又問
Another ten days and the king asked again.

***

曰未也
"Not yet.

猶應嚮景
They still respond to noises and movements."

十日又問
Another ten days and the king asked again.

***

曰未也
"Not yet.

猶疾視而盛氣
They still look around fiercely and are full of spirit."

十日又問
Another ten days and the king asked again.

***

曰幾矣
"They're close enough.

雞雖有鳴者
已無變矣
and they show no sign of change.
Another cock can crow,

望之似木雞矣
Look at them from a distance, and you'd think they were made of wood.

其德全矣
Their virtue is complete.

異雞無敢應者反走矣
Other cocks won't dare face up to them but will turn and run."

### Confucius questions a diver at the waterfall in Lüliang

孔子觀於呂梁
Confucius was seeing the sights at Lüliang,
縣水三十仞
where the water falls from a height of thirty fathoms
流沫四十里
and races and boils along for forty *li*,
黿鼉魚龞之所不能游也
so swift that no fish or other water creature can swim in it.

見一丈夫游之
He saw a man dive into the water,
以為有苦而欲死也
and supposing that the man was in some kind of trouble and intended to end his life,
使弟子並流而拯之
he ordered his disciples to line up on the bank and pull the man out.

數百步
而出被髮行歌
而游於塘下
But after the man had gone a couple of hundred paces,
he came out of the water and began strolling along the base of the embankment,
his hair streaming down, singing a song.

孔子從而問焉曰
Confucius ran after him and said,
吾以子為鬼察子則人也
"At first I thought you were a ghost, but now I see you're a man.

請問蹈水有道乎
May I ask if you have some special way of staying afloat in the water?"

***

曰亡吾無道
"I have no way.

吾始乎故
I began with what I was used to,
長乎性
grew up with my nature,
成乎命
and let things come to completion with fate.

與齊俱入
I go under with the swirls
與汩偕出
and come out with the eddies,
從水之道而不為私焉
following along the way the water goes and never thinking about myself.

此吾所以蹈之也
That's how I can stay afloat."

***

孔子曰
Confucius said,
何謂始乎故
"What do you mean by saying that you began with what you were used to,
長乎性
grew up with your nature,
成乎命
and let things come to completion with fate?"

***

曰吾生於陵而安於陵
"I was born on the dry land and felt safe on the dry land---
故也
that was what I was used to.

長於水而安於水
I grew up with the water and felt safe in the water---
性也
that was my nature.

不知吾所以然而然
I don't know why I do what I do---
命也
that's fate."

### Woodworker Qing makes a bell stand

梓慶削木為鐻
Woodworker Qing[^19-7] carved a piece of wood and made a bell stand,
鐻成
and when it was finished,
見者驚猶鬼神
everyone who saw it marveled, for it seemed to be the work of gods or spirits.

[^19-7]: A carpenter of Lu, mentioned in the _Zuozhuan_ under Duke Xiang, fourth year (569 BCE).

魯侯見而問焉曰
When the marquis of Lu saw it, he asked,
子何術以為焉
"What art is it you have?"

***

對曰臣工人
Qing replied, "I am only a craftsman---
何術之有
how would I have any art?

雖然有一焉
There is one thing, however.

臣將為鐻
When I am going to make a bell stand,
未嘗敢以耗氣也
I never let it wear out my energy.

必齊以靜心
I always fast in order to still my mind.

齊三日
When I have fasted for three days,
而不敢懷慶賞爵祿
I no longer have any thought of congratulations or rewards, of titles or stipends.

齊五日
When I have fasted for five days,
不敢懷非譽巧拙
I no longer have any thought of praise or blame, of skill or clumsiness.

齊七日
And when I have fasted for seven days,
輒然忘吾有四枝形體也
I am so still that I forget I have four limbs and a form and body.

當是時也無公朝
By that time, the ruler and his court no longer exist for me.

其巧專而外骨消
My skill is concentrated, and all outside distractions fade away.

然後入山林
After that, I go into the mountain forest
觀天性形軀至矣
and examine the Heavenly nature of the trees.

然後成見鐻
If I find one of superlative form and I can see a bell stand there,
然後加手焉
I put my hand to the job of carving;
不然則已
if not, I let it go.

則以天合天
This way I am simply matching up 'Heaven' with 'Heaven.'[^19-8]

[^19-8]: That is, matching his own innate nature with that of the tree.

器之所以疑神者其是與
That's probably the reason that people wonder if the results were not made by spirits."

### Dongye Ji's horses break down

東野稷以御見莊公
Dongye Ji was displaying his carriage driving before Duke Zhuang.

進退中繩
He drove back and forth as straight as a measuring line
左右旋中規
and wheeled to left and right as neat as a compass-drawn curve.

莊公以為文弗過也
Duke Zhuang concluded that even Zao Fu[^19-9] could do no better
使之鉤百而反
and ordered him to make a hundred circuits and then return to the palace.

[^19-9]: Zao Fu was a famous master of the art of carriage driving. I emend *wen* to *fu*.

顏闔遇之入見
Yan He happened along at the moment and went in to see the duke.

曰稷之馬將敗
"Dongye Ji's horses are going to break down," he said.

公密而不應
The duke was silent and gave no answer.

少焉果敗而反
In a little while Dongye Ji returned, his horses having in fact broken down.

公曰子何以知之
The duke asked Yan He, "How did you know that was going to happen?"

曰其馬力竭矣
Yan He said, "The strength of the horses was all gone,
而猶求焉
and still he was asking them to go on---
故曰敗
that's why I said they would break down."

### Artisan Chui gets comfortable

工倕旋而蓋規矩
Artisan Chui could draw as true as a compass or a T square
指與物化
because his fingers changed along with things
而不以心稽
and he didn't let his mind get in the way.

故其靈臺一而不桎
Therefore his Spirit Tower[^19-10] remained unified and unobstructed.

[^19-10]: A Daoist term for the mind.

***

忘足履之適也
You forget your feet when the shoes are comfortable.

忘要帶之適也
You forget your waist when the belt is comfortable.

知忘是非心之適也
Understanding forgets right and wrong when the mind is comfortable.

不內變
There is no change in what is inside,
不外從
no following what is outside,
事會之適也
when the adjustment to events is comfortable.

始乎適而未嘗不適者
You begin with what is comfortable and never experience what is uncomfortable
忘適之適也
when you know the comfort of forgetting what is comfortable.

### Master Bian Qingzi startles Sun Xiu (the bird of Lu)

有孫休者踵門而詫子扁慶子
A certain Sun Xiu appeared at the gate of Master Bian Qingzi to pay him a call.

曰休居鄉
"When I was living in the village," he said,
不見謂不修
"no one ever said I lacked good conduct.

臨難不見謂不勇
When I faced difficulty, no one ever said I lacked courage.

然而田原不遇歲
Yet when I worked the fields, it never seemed to be a good year for crops,
事君不遇世
and when I served the ruler, it never seemed to be a good time for advancement.

賓於鄉里
So I am an outcast from the villages,
逐於州部
an exile from the towns.

則胡罪乎天哉
What crime have I committed against Heaven?

休惡遇此命也
Why should I meet this fate?"

***

扁子曰
Master Bian said,
子獨不聞夫至人之自行邪
"Have you never heard how the Perfect Man conducts himself?

忘其肝膽
He forgets his liver and gall
遺其耳目
and thinks no more about his eyes and ears.

芒然彷徨乎塵垢之外
Vague and aimless, he wanders beyond the dirt and dust;
逍遙乎無事之業
free and easy, tending to nothing is his job.

是謂為而不恃
This is what is called 'doing but not looking for any thanks,
長而不宰
bringing up but not bossing.'[^19-11]

[^19-11]: The same saying is found in the _Daodejing_, secs. X and LI.

今汝飾知以驚愚
Now you show off your wisdom in order to astound the ignorant,
修身以明汙
work at your good conduct in order to distinguish yourself from the disreputable,
昭昭乎若揭日月而行也
going around bright and shining as though you were carrying the sun and moon in your hand!

汝得全而形軀
You've managed to keep your body in one piece;
具而九竅
you have all the ordinary nine openings;
無中道夭於聾盲跛蹇
you haven't been struck down midway by blindness or deafness, lameness or deformity---
而比於人數亦幸矣
compared with a lot of people, you're a lucky man.

又何暇乎天之怨哉
How do you have any time to go around complaining against Heaven?

子往矣
Be on your way!"

***

孫子出
After Master Sun had left,
扁子入坐有間
Master Bian went back into the house, sat down for a while,
仰天而歎
and then looked up to heaven and sighed.

弟子問曰
One of his disciples asked,
先生何為歎乎
"Why does my teacher sigh?"

***

扁子曰
Master Bian said,
向者休來
"Just now Sun Xiu came to see me,
吾告之以至人之德
and I described to him the virtue of the Perfect Man.

吾恐其驚而遂至於惑也
I'm afraid he was very startled and may end up in a complete muddle."

***

弟子曰不然
"Surely not," said the disciple.

孫子之所言是邪
"Was what Master Sun said right
先生之所言非邪
and what my teacher said wrong?

非固不能惑是
If so, then wrong can certainly never make a muddle out of right.

孫子所言非邪
Or was what Master Sun said wrong
先生所言是邪
and what my teacher said right?

彼固惑而來矣
If so, then he must already have been in a muddle when he came here,
又奚罪焉
so what's the harm?"

***

扁子曰不然
"You don't understand," said Master Bian.

昔者有鳥止於魯郊
"Once long ago a bird alighted in the suburbs of the Lu capital.

魯君說之
The ruler of Lu was delighted with it,
為具太牢以饗之
had a Tailao sacrifice prepared for it to feast on,
奏九韶以樂之
and the Nine Shao music performed for its enjoyment.

鳥乃始憂悲眩視
But the bird immediately began to look unhappy and dazed
不敢飲食
and did not dare to eat or drink.

此之謂以己養養鳥也
This is what is called trying to nourish a bird with what would nourish you.

若夫以鳥養養鳥者
If you want to nourish a bird with what will nourish a bird,
宜棲之深林
you had best let it roost in the deep forest,
浮之江湖
float on the rivers and lakes,
食之以委蛇
and live on snakes---
則平陸而已矣
then it can feel at ease.[^19-12]

***

今休款啟寡聞之民也
"Now Sun Xiu is a man of ignorance and little learning.

吾告以至人之德
For me to describe to him the virtue of the Perfect Man
譬之若載鼷以車馬
is like taking a mouse for a ride in a carriage
樂鴳以鐘鼓也
or trying to delight a quail with the music of bells and drums.

彼又奚能無驚乎哉
How could he help but be startled?"

[^19-12]: The text of the last part of the sentence appears to be corrupt, and I make little sense of it.
The same anecdote, in somewhat more detailed form, appeared on p. 143.
