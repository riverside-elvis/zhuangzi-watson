### 21 田子方 Tian Zifang

### Tian Zifang tells Marquis Wen of Wei about his teacher

田子方侍坐於魏文侯
Tian Zifang was sitting in attendance on Marquis Wen of Wei.[^21-1]

[^21-1]: Marquis Wen (r. 424–387 BCE) guided the state of Wei during the crucial years
when it first won recognition as an independent feudal domain;
he is famous in history as a patron of learning.
Tian Zifang appears to have been one of the philosophers attracted to his court.

數稱谿工文侯曰
When he repeatedly praised one Qi Gong, Marquis Wen asked,
谿工子之師邪
"Is Qi Gong your teacher?"

***

子方曰非也
"No," replied Zifang.

無擇之里人也
"He comes from the same neighborhood as I do.

稱道數當
Discussing the Way with him, I've found he often hits the mark---
故無擇稱之
that's why I praise him."

文侯曰然則子無師邪
"Have you no teacher then?" asked Marquis Wen.

子方曰有
"I have," said Zifang.

曰子之師誰邪
"Who is your teacher?"

子方曰東郭順子
"Master Shun from east of the Wall," said Zifang.

文侯曰然則夫子何故未嘗稱之
"Then why have you never praised *him*?" asked Marquis Wen.

***

子方曰
Zifang said,
其為人也真
"He's the kind of man who is True---
人貌而天虛
the face of a human being, the emptiness of Heaven.

緣而葆真
He follows along and keeps tight hold of the True;
清而容物
pure, he can encompass all things.

物無道正容以悟之
If men do not have the Way, he has only to put on a straight face, and they are enlightened;
使人之意也消
he causes men's intentions to melt away.

無擇何足以稱之
But how could any of this be worth praising!"

***

子方出
Zifang retired from the room,
文侯儻然終日不言
and Marquis Wen, stupefied, sat for the rest of the day in silence.

召前立臣而語之曰
Then he called to the ministers who stood in attendance on him and said,
遠矣全德之君子
"How far away he is---the gentleman of Complete Virtue!

始吾以聖知之言
I used to think that the words of the wisdom of the sages
仁義之行為至矣
and the practices of benevolence and righteousness were the highest ideal.

吾聞子方之師
But now that I have heard about Zifang's teacher,
吾形解而不欲動
my body has fallen apart, and I feel no inclination to move;
口鉗而不欲言
my mouth is manacled, and I feel no inclination to speak.

吾所學者直土梗耳
These things that I have been studying are so many clay dolls[^21-2]---nothing more!

[^21-2]: That melt and turn to mud when the rains come.

夫魏真為我累耳
This state of Wei is in truth only a burden to me!"

---

### Wenbo Xuexi receives a visitor in Qi

溫伯雪子適齊
Wenbo Xuezi, journeying to Qi,
舍於魯
stopped along the way in the state of Lu.[^21-3]

[^21-3]: Wenbo Xuezi is vaguely identified as a man of the state of Chu in the south;
hence he refers to the states of Qi and Lu, the centers of Confucian learning, as "middle states."

魯人有請見之者
A man of Lu requested an interview with him,
溫伯雪子曰不可
but Wenbo Xuezi said, "No indeed!

吾聞中國之君子
I have heard of the gentlemen of these middle states---
明乎禮義
enlightened on the subject of ritual principles
而陋於知人心
but stupid in their understanding of men's hearts.

吾不欲見也
I have no wish to see any such person."

***

至於齊
He arrived at his destination in Qi and,
反舍於魯
on his way home, had stopped again in Lu
是人也又請見
when the man once more requested an interview.

溫伯雪子曰
Wenbo Xuezi said,
往也蘄見我
"In the past he made an attempt to see me,
今也又蘄見我
and now he's trying again.

是必有以振我也
He undoubtedly has some means by which he hopes to 'save' me!"

***

出而見客入而歎
He went out to receive the visitor and returned to his own rooms with a sigh.

明日見客又入而歎
The following day, he received the visitor once more and once more returned with a sigh.

其僕曰
His groom said,
每見之客也必入而歎
"Every time you receive this visitor, you come back sighing.

何邪
Why is that?"

***

曰吾固告子矣
"I told you before, didn't I?

中國之民明乎禮義
These men of the middle states are enlightened in ritual principles
而陋乎知人心
but stupid in the understanding of men's hearts.

昔之見我者
Yesterday, when this man came to see me,
進退一成規一成矩
his advancings and retirings were as precise as though marked by compass or T square.

從容一若龍一若虎
In looks and bearing, he was now a dragon, now a tiger.

其諫我也似子
He remonstrated with me as though he were my son,
其道我也似父
offered me guidance as though he were my father!

是以歎也
That is why I sighed."

***

仲尼見之而不言
Confucius also went for an interview with Wenbo Xuezi but returned without having spoken a word.

子路曰
Zilu said,
吾子欲見溫伯雪子久矣
"You have been wanting to see Wenbo Xuezi for a long time.

見之而不言
Now you had the chance to see him;
何邪
why didn't you say anything?"

***

仲尼曰
Confucius said,
若夫人者目擊而道存矣
"With that kind of man, one glance tells you that the Way is there before you.

亦不可以容聲矣
What room does that leave for any possibility of speech?"

### Confucius leaves Yan Yuan in the dust

顏淵問於仲尼曰
Yan Yuan said to Confucius,
夫子步亦步
"Master, when you walk, I walk;
夫子趨亦趨
when you trot, I trot;
夫子馳亦馳
when you gallop, I gallop.

夫子奔逸絕塵
But when you break into the kind of dash that leaves even the dust behind,
而回瞠若乎後矣
all I can do is stare after you in amazement!"

***

夫子曰回何謂邪
"Hui, what are you talking about?" asked the Master.

***

曰夫子步亦步也
"When you walk, I walk---
夫子言亦言也
that is, I can speak just as you speak.

夫子趨亦趨也
When you trot, I trot---
夫子辯亦辯也
that is, I can make discriminations just as you do.

夫子馳亦馳也
When you gallop, I gallop---
夫子言道回亦言道也
that is, I can expound the Way just as you do.

及奔逸絕塵
But when you break into the kind of dash that leaves even the dust behind
而回瞠若乎後者
and all I can do is stare after you in amazement---
夫子不言而信
by that I mean that you do not have to speak to be trusted,
不比而周
that you are catholic and not partisan,[^21-4]
無器而民滔乎前
that although you lack the regalia of high office, the people still congregate before you,
而不知所以然而已矣
and with all this, you do not know why it is so."

[^21-4]: Compare _Analects_ II, 14: "The gentleman is catholic and not partisan."

***

仲尼曰
"Ah," said Confucius,
惡可不察與
"we had best look into this!

夫哀莫大於心死
There is no grief greater than the death of the mind---
而人死亦次之
beside it, the death of the body is a minor matter.

日出東方而入於西極
The sun rises out of the east, sets at the end of the west,
萬物莫不比方
and each one of the ten thousand things moves side by side with it.

有目有趾者
Creatures that have eyes and feet
待是而後成功
待晝而作
must wait for it before their success is complete.

是出則存
Its rising means they may go on living;
是入則亡
its setting means they perish.

萬物亦然
For all the ten thousand things, it is thus.

有待也而死
They must wait for something before they can die,
有待也而生
wait for something before they can live.

吾一受其成形
Having once received this fixed bodily form,
而不化以待盡
I will hold on to it, unchanging, in this way waiting for the end.

效物而動
I move after the model of other things,
日夜無隙
day and night without break,
而不知其所終
but I do not know what the end will be.

薰然其成形
Mild, genial, my bodily form takes shape.

知命不能規乎其前
I understand my fate, but I cannot fathom what has gone before it.

丘以是日徂
This is the way I proceed, day after day.

***

吾終身與汝交一臂而失之
"I have gone through life linked arm in arm with you, yet now you fail [to understand me]---
可不哀與
is this not sad?

女殆著乎吾所以著也
You see in me, I suppose, the part that can be seen---
彼已盡矣
but that part is already over and gone.

而女求之以為有
For you to come looking for it, thinking it still exists,
是求馬於唐肆也
is like looking for a horse after the horse fair is over.[^21-5]

[^21-5]: Reading *kong* in place of *tang* in accordance with Ma Xulun's suggestion.

吾服女也甚忘
I serve you best when I have utterly forgotten you,
女服吾也亦甚忘
and you likewise serve me best when you have utterly forgotten me.

雖然女奚患焉
But even so, why should you repine?

雖忘乎故吾
Even if you forget the old me,
吾有不忘者存
I will still possess something that will not be forgotten!"[^21-6]

[^21-6]: This beautiful passage, whose exact meaning I only dimly follow,
presents numerous difficulties of interpretation.
The verb *fu*, which I have translated as "serve," may be taken in many different ways.

### Confucius spies Lao Dan's hair

孔子見老聃
Confucius went to call on Lao Dan.

老聃新沐
Lao Dan had just finished washing his hair
方將被髮而乾
and had spread it over his shoulders to dry.

慹然似非人
Utterly motionless, he did not even seem to be human.

孔子便而待之
Confucius, hidden from sight,[^21-7] stood waiting
少焉見曰
and then, after some time, presented himself and exclaimed,
丘也眩與
"Did my eyes play tricks on me,
其信然與
or was that really true?

[^21-7]: Following Zhang Binglin's interpretation.

向者先生形體掘若槁木
A moment ago, sir, your form and body seemed stiff as an old dead tree,
似遺物離人而立於獨也
as though you had forgotten things, taken leave of men, and were standing in solitude itself!"

***

老聃曰
Lao Dan said,
吾遊心於物之初
"I was letting my mind wander in the Beginning of things."

孔子曰何謂邪
"What does that mean?" asked Confucius.

***

曰心困焉而不能知
"The mind may wear itself out but can never understand it;
口辟焉而不能言
the mouth may gape but can never describe it.

嘗為汝議乎其將
Nevertheless, I will try explaining it to you in rough outline.

***

至陰肅肅
"Perfect Yin is stern and frigid;
至陽赫赫
Perfect Yang is bright and glittering.

肅肅出乎天
The sternness and frigidity come forth from heaven;
赫赫發乎地
the brightness and glitter emerge from the earth;[^21-8]
兩者交通成和
而物生焉
the two mingle, penetrate, come together, harmonize,
and all things are born therefrom.

[^21-8]: Ordinarily, the yang principle represents heaven, and the yin principle, earth.
Whether the reversal of their roles here is deliberate or the result of textual error, I do not know.
Waley (_Three Ways of Thought_, p. 16) emends the text to put them in their usual order.

或為之紀
Perhaps someone manipulates the cords that draw it all together,
而莫見其形
but no one has ever seen his form.

消息滿虛
Decay, growth, fullness, emptiness,
一晦一明
now murky, now bright,
日改月化
the sun shifting, the moon changing phase---
日有所為
day after day these things proceed,
而莫見其功
yet no one has seen him bringing them about.

生有所乎萌
Life has its sproutings,
死有所乎歸
death its destination,
始終相反乎無端
end and beginning tail one another in unbroken round,
而莫知其所窮
and no one has ever heard of their coming to a stop.

非是也
If it is not as I have described it,
且孰為之宗
then who else could the Ancestor of all this be?"

***

孔子曰
Confucius said,
請問遊是
"May I ask what it means to wander in such a place?"

***

老聃曰
Lao Dan said,
夫得是至美至樂也
"It means to attain Perfect Beauty and Perfect Happiness.

得至美而遊乎至樂
He who attains Perfect Beauty and wanders in Perfect Happiness
謂之至人
may be called the Perfect Man."

***

孔子曰
Confucius said,
願聞其方
"I would like to hear by what means this may be accomplished."

***

曰草食之獸不疾易藪
"Beasts that feed on grass do not fret over a change of pasture;
水生之蟲不疾易水
creatures that live in water do not fret over a change of stream.

行小變而不失其大常也
They accept the minor shift as long as the all-important constant is not lost.

喜怒哀樂不入於胸次
[Be like them,] and joy, anger, grief, and happiness can never enter your breast.

夫天下也者萬物之所一也
In this world, the ten thousand things come together in One;
得其所一而同焉
and if you can find that One and become identical with it,
則四支百體將為塵垢
then your four limbs and hundred joints will become dust and sweepings;
而死生終始將為晝夜
life and death, beginning and end, will be mere day and night,
而莫之能滑
and nothing whatever can confound you---
而況得喪禍福之所介乎
certainly not the trifles of gain or loss, good or bad fortune!

***

棄隸者若棄泥塗
"A man will discard the servants who wait on him as though they were so much earth or mud,
知身貴於隸也
for he knows that his own person is of more worth than the servants who tend it.

貴在於我
Worth lies within yourself,
而不失於變
and no external shift will cause it to be lost.

且萬化而未始有極也
And since the ten thousand transformations continue without even the beginning of an end,
夫孰足以患心
how could they be enough to bring anxiety to your mind?

已為道者解乎此
He who practices the Way understands all this."[^21-9]

[^21-9]: One may also, like Guo Xiang, take the word *jie* (understand) to mean "free";
that is, "He who practices the Way is freed from all this." Compare sec. 6, p. 48: "the freeing of the bound."

***

孔子曰
Confucius said,
夫子德配天地
"Your virtue, sir, is the very counterpart of Heaven and earth,
而猶假至言以修心
and yet even you must employ these perfect teachings in order to cultivate your mind.

古之君子
Who, then, even among the fine gentlemen of the past,
孰能脫焉
could have avoided such labors?"

***

老聃曰不然
"Not so!" said Lao Dan.

夫水之於汋也
"The murmuring of the water is its natural talent,
無為而才自然矣
not something that it does deliberately.

至人之於德也
The Perfect Man stands in the same relationship to virtue.

不修而物不能離焉
Without cultivating it, he possesses it to such an extent that things cannot draw away from him.

若天之自高
It is as natural as the height of heaven,
地之自厚
the depth of the earth,
日月之自明
the brightness of sun and moon.

夫何修焉
What is there to be cultivated?"

***

孔子出以告顏回曰
When Confucius emerged from the interview, he reported what had passed to Yan Hui, saying,
丘之於道也
"As far as the Way is concerned,
其猶醯雞與
I was a mere gnat in the vinegar jar!

微夫子之發吾覆也
If the Master hadn't taken off the lid for me,
吾不知天地之大全也
I would never have understood the Great Integrity of Heaven and earth!"

### Duke Ai orders the gentlemen of Lu to dress according to their doctrine (or the only Confucian in Lu)

莊子見魯哀公
Zhuangzi went to see Duke Ai of Lu.

哀公曰
Duke Ai said,
魯多儒士
"We have a great many Confucians here in the state of Lu,
少為先生方者
but there seem to be very few men who study your methods, sir!"

莊子曰魯少儒
"There are few Confucians in the state of Lu!" said Zhuangzi.

***

哀公曰舉魯國而儒服
"But the whole state of Lu is dressed in Confucian garb!" said Duke Ai.

何謂少乎
"How can you say they are few?"

***

莊子曰周聞之
"I have heard," said Zhuangzi,
儒者冠圜冠者知天時
"that the Confucians wear round caps on their heads to show that they understand the cycles of heaven,
履句屨者知地形
that they walk about in square shoes to show that they understand the shape of the earth,
緩佩玦者事至而斷
and that they tie ornaments in the shape of a broken disk at their girdles in order to show that when the time comes for decisive action, they must 'make the break.'

君子有其道者
But a gentleman may embrace a doctrine
未必為其服也
without necessarily wearing the garb that goes with it,
為其服者
and he may wear the garb
未必知其道也
without necessarily comprehending the doctrine.

公固以為不然
If Your Grace does not believe this is so,
何不號於國中曰
then why not try issuing an order to the state proclaiming:
無此道而為此服者
'All those who wear the garb without practicing the doctrine that goes with it
其罪死
will be sentenced to death!'"

***

於是哀公號之五日
Duke Ai did in fact issue such an order,
而魯國無敢儒服者
and within five days there was no one in the state of Lu who dared wear Confucian garb.

獨有一丈夫儒服而立乎公門
Only one old man came in Confucian dress and stood in front of the duke's gate.

公即召而問以國事
The duke at once summoned him and questioned him on affairs of state,
千轉萬變而不窮
and though the discussion took a thousand turnings and ten thousand shifts, the old man was never at a loss for words.

莊子曰
Zhuangzi said,
以魯國而儒者一人耳
"In the whole state of Lu, then, there is only one man who is a real Confucian.

可謂多乎
How can you say there are a great many of them?"

### Duke Mu of Qin hands over the government to Boli Xi

百里奚爵祿不入於心
Boli Xi did not let title and stipend get inside his mind.

故飯牛而牛肥
He fed the cattle and the cattle grew fat,
使秦穆公忘其賤與之政也
and this fact made Duke Mu of Qin forget Boli Xi's lowly position and turn over the government to him.[^21-10]

[^21-10]: Boli Xi, a statesman of the seventh century BCE,
was taken captive when his state was overthrown and, for a time, led the life of a lowly cattle tender.
His worth was eventually recognized by Duke Mu of Qin, who made him his high minister.

有虞氏死生不入於心
Shun, the man of the Yu clan, did not let life and death get inside his mind.

故足以動人
So he was able to influence others.[^21-11]

[^21-11]: Shun's parents and younger brother made several attempts to kill him,
but he did not allow this to alter his filial behavior.
[Boli Xi is also a man of the Yu clan, so does this refer to Shun or to Boli Xi? --RSE]

### Lord Yuan of Song wants to have some pictures painted

宋元君將畫圖
Lord Yuan of Song wanted to have some pictures painted.

眾史皆至
The crowd of court clerks all gathered in his presence,
受揖而立
received their drawing panels,[^21-12] and took their places in line,
舐筆和墨
licking their brushes, mixing their inks;
在外者半
so many of them that there were more outside the room than inside it.

[^21-12]: Following Ma Xulun's emendation.
It is not clear just what kind of paintings the ruler of Song is commissioning,
and some commentators take them to be mere maps.
But the description of the "true artist" that follows suggests a more creative type of activity.

有一史後至者
There was one clerk who arrived late,
儃儃然不趨
sauntering in without the slightest haste.

受揖不立因之舍
When he received his drawing panel, he did not look for a place in line but went straight to his own quarters.

公使人視之
The ruler sent someone to see what he was doing,
則解衣般礡臝
and it was found that he had taken off his robes, stretched out his legs, and was sitting there naked.

君曰可矣
"Very good," said the ruler.

是真畫者也
"This is a true artist!"

### King Wen hands over the government to an old fisherman

文王觀於臧
King Wen was seeing the sights at Zang
見一丈夫釣
when he spied an old man fishing.[^21-13]

[^21-13]: King Wen, honored as the founder of the Zhou dynasty,
was one of the ancient sages most often and extravagantly praised by Confucius and his followers.

而其釣莫釣
Yet his fishing wasn't really fishing.

非持其釣有釣者也
He didn't fish as though he were fishing for anything
常釣也
but as though it were his constant occupation to fish.

文王欲舉而授之政
King Wen wanted to summon him and hand over the government to him,
而恐大臣父兄之弗安也
but he was afraid that the high officials and his uncles and brothers would be uneasy.

欲終而釋之
He thought perhaps he had better forget the matter and let it rest,
而不忍百姓之無天也
and yet he couldn't bear to deprive the hundred clans of such a Heaven-sent opportunity.

於是旦而屬之夫夫曰
At dawn the next day he therefore reported to his ministers, saying,
昔者寡人夢見良人黑色而髯
"Last night I dreamed I saw a fine man, dark complexioned and bearded,
乘駁馬而偏朱蹄
mounted on a dappled horse that had red hoofs on one side.

號曰
He commanded me, saying,
寓而政於臧丈人
'Hand over your rule to the old man of Zang---
庶幾乎民有瘳乎
then perhaps the ills of the people may be cured!'

***

諸大夫蹴然曰
The ministers, awestruck, said,
先君王也
"It was the king, your late father!"

文王曰然則卜之
"Then perhaps we should divine to see what ought to be done," said King Wen.

***

諸大夫曰先君之命王
"It is the command of your late father!" said the ministers.

其無它
"Your Majesty must have no second thoughts.

又何卜焉
What need is there for divination?"

***

遂迎臧丈人而授之政
In the end, therefore, the king had the old man of Zang escorted to the capital and handed over the government to him,
典洗無更
but the regular precedents and laws remained unchanged,
偏令無出
and not a single new order was issued.

***

三年文王觀於國
At the end of three years, King Wen made an inspection tour of the state.

則列士壞植散群
He found that the local officials had smashed their gate bars and disbanded their cliques,
長官者不成德
that the heads of government bureaus achieved no special distinction,
斔斛不敢入於四竟
and that persons entering the four borders from other states no longer ventured to bring their own measuring cups and bushels with them.

列士壞植散群
The local officials had smashed their gate bars and disbanded their cliques
則尚同也
because they had learned to identify with their superiors.[^21-14]

[^21-14]: The term "identifying with one's superior" is taken from the teachings of Mozi.
According to this doctrine, each class of society is to follow the orders
and ethical teaching of the class above, the whole hierarchy being headed by the Son of Heaven,
in this case, King Wen.

長官者不成德
The heads of government bureaus achieved no special distinction
則同務也
because they looked on all tasks as being of equal distinction.

斔斛不敢入於四竟
Persons entering the four borders from other states no longer ventured to bring their own measuring cups and bushels with them
則諸侯無二心也
because the feudal lords had ceased to distrust the local measures.

***

文王於是焉以為大師
King Wen thereupon concluded that he had found a Great Teacher,
北面而問曰
and facing north as a sign of respect, he asked,
政可以及天下乎
"Could these methods of government be extended to the whole world?"

***

臧丈人昧然而不應
But the old man of Zang looked blank and gave no answer,
泛然而辭
evasively mumbling some excuse;
朝令而夜遁
and when orders went out the next morning to make the attempt,
終身無聞
the old man ran away the very same night and was never heard of again.

***

顏淵問於仲尼曰
Yan Yuan questioned Confucius about this story, saying,
文王其猶未邪
"King Wen didn't amount to very much after all, did he!

又何以夢為乎
And why did he have to resort to that business about the dream?"

***

仲尼曰默
"Quiet!" said Confucius.

汝無言
"No more talk from you!

夫文王盡之也
King Wen was perfection itself---
而又何論刺焉
how can there be any room for carping and criticism!

彼直以循斯須也
The dream---that was just a way of getting out of a moment's difficulty."

### Lie Yukou demonstrates his archery to Buhun Wuren

列御寇為伯昏無人射
Lie Yukou was demonstrating his archery to Bohun Wuren.[^21-15]

[^21-15]: Lie Yukou appeared in sec. 1, p. 3;
Bohun Wuren, in sec. 5, p. 35.

引之盈貫
He drew the bow as far as it would go,
措杯水其肘上發之
placed a cup of water on his elbow, and let fly.

適矢復沓方矢復寓
One arrow had no sooner left his thumb ring than a second was resting in readiness beside his arm guard,
當是時猶象人也
and all the while he stood like a statue.[^21-16]

[^21-16]: In the interpretation of these archery terms, I follow Ma Xulun's emendations.

伯昏無人曰
Bohun Wuren said,
是射之射
"This is the archery of an archer,
非不射之射也
not the archery of a nonarcher!

嘗與汝登高山
Try climbing up a high mountain with me,
履危石臨百仞之淵
scrambling over the steep rocks to the very brink of an eight-hundred-foot chasm---
若能射乎
then we'll see what kind of shooting you can do!"

***

於是無人遂登高山
Accordingly, they proceeded to climb a high mountain,
履危石臨百仞之淵
scrambling over the steep rocks to the brink of an eight-hundred-foot chasm.

背逡巡
There Bohun Wuren, turning his back to the chasm,
足二分垂在外
walked backward until his feet projected halfway off the edge of the cliff,
揖御寇而進之
bowed to Lie Yukou, and invited him to come forward and join him.

御寇伏地
But Lie Yukou cowered on the ground,
汗流至踵
sweat pouring down all the way to his heels.

伯昏無人曰
Bohun Wuren said,
夫至人者上闚青天
"The Perfect Man may stare at the blue heavens above,
下潛黃泉
dive into the Yellow Springs below,
揮斥八極
ramble to the end of the eight directions,
神氣不變
yet his spirit and bearing undergo no change.

今汝怵然有恂目之志
And here you are in this cringing, eye-batting state of mind---
爾於中也殆矣夫
if you tried to take aim now, you would be in certain peril!"

### Sunshu Ao becomes premier three times

肩吾問於孫叔敖曰
Jian Wu said to Sunshu Ao,
子三為令尹
"Three times you have become premier,
而不榮華
yet you didn't seem to glory in it.[^21-17]

[^21-17]: Jian Wu appeared in sec. 1, p. 4, and sec. 7, p. 55.
Sunshu Ao was a sixth-century statesman of Chu.

三去之
Three times you were dismissed from the post,
而無憂色
but you never looked glum over it.

吾始也疑子
At first I doubted that this was really true,
今視子之鼻間栩栩然
but now I stand before your very nose and see how calm and unconcerned you are.

子之用心獨奈何
Do you have some unique way of using your mind?"

***

孫叔敖曰
Sunshu Ao replied,
吾何以過人哉
"How am I any better than other men?

吾以其來不可卻也
I considered that the coming of such an honor could not be fended off
其去不可止也
and that its departure could not be prevented.

吾以為得失之非我也
As far as I was concerned, the question of profit or loss did not rest with me,
而無憂色而已矣
and so I had no reason to put on a glum expression, that was all.

我何以過人哉
How am I any better than other men?

且不知其在彼乎其在我乎
Moreover, I'm not really certain whether the glory resides in the premiership or in me.

其在彼邪亡乎我
If it resides in the premiership, then it means nothing to me.

在我邪亡乎彼
And if it resides in me, then it means nothing to the premiership.

方將躊躇
Now I'm about to go for an idle stroll,
方將四顧
to go gawking in the four directions.

何暇至乎人貴人賤哉
What leisure do I have to worry about who holds an eminent position and who a humble one?"

***

仲尼聞之曰
Confucius, hearing of the incident, said,
古之真人
"He was a True Man of old,
知者不得說
the kind that the wise cannot argue with,
美人不得濫
the beautiful cannot seduce,
盜人不得劫
the violent cannot intimidate;
伏戲黃帝不得友
even Fu Xi or the Yellow Emperor could not have befriended him.

死生亦大矣
Life and death are great affairs,
而無變乎己
and yet they are no change to him---
況爵祿乎
how much less to him are things like titles and stipends!

若然者
With such a man,
其神經乎大山而無介
his spirit may soar over Mount Tai without hindrance,
入乎淵泉而不濡
may plunge into the deepest springs without getting wet,
處卑細而不憊
may occupy the meanest, most humble position without distress.

充滿天地
He fills all Heaven and earth,
既以與人己愈有
and the more he gives to others, the more he has for himself."

### The lord of Fan is preserved

楚王與凡君坐
The king of Chu was sitting with the lord of Fan.[^21-18]

[^21-18]: Fan was a small state subservient to the much larger and more powerful state of Chu,
which eventually overthrew it.

少焉楚王左右曰凡亡者三
After a little while, three of the king of Chu's attendants reported that the state of Fan had been destroyed.

凡君曰
The lord of Fan said,
凡之亡也不足以喪吾存
"The destruction of Fan is not enough to make me lose what I am intent on preserving.[^21-19]

[^21-19]: That is, the Way.
The whole passage is a play on the two levels of meaning,
political and philosophical, of the words "destruction" (*wang*) and "preservation" (*cun*).

夫凡之亡也不足以喪吾存
And if the destruction of Fan is not enough to make me lose what I preserve,
則楚之存不足以存存
then the preservation of Chu is not enough to make it preserve what it ought to preserve.

由是觀之
Looking at it this way, then,
則凡未始亡
Fan has not yet begun to be destroyed,
而楚未始存也
and Chu has not yet begun to be preserved!"
