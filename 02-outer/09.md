## 9 馬蹄 Horses’ Hoofs

馬蹄可以踐霜雪
毛可以禦風寒
Horses' hoofs are made for treading frost and snow,
their coats for keeping out wind and cold.

齕草飲水
翹足而陸
To munch grass, drink from the stream,
lift up their feet and gallop---
此馬之真性也
this is the true nature of horses.

雖有義臺路寢
無所用之
Though they might possess great terraces and fine halls,
they would have no use for them.

***

及至伯樂
Then along comes Bo Luo.[^9-1]

[^9-1]: Frequently mentioned in early texts as an expert judge of horses.

曰我善治馬
燒之剔之
刻之雒之
連之以羈馽
編之以皁棧
"I'm good at handling horses!" he announces and proceeds
to singe them, shave them,
pare them, brand them,
bind them with martingale and crupper,
tie them up in stable and stall.

馬之死者十二三矣
By this time, two or three out of ten horses have died.

飢之渴之
馳之驟之
整之齊之
前有橛飾之患
而後有鞭筴之威
He goes on to starve them, make them go thirsty,
race them, prance them,
pull them into line, and force them to run side by side,
in front of them the worry of bit and rein,
behind them the terror of whip and crop.

而馬之死者已過半矣
By this time, more than half the horses have died.

***

陶者曰我善治埴
The potter says, "I'm good at handling clay!

圓者中規
To round it, I apply the compass;
方者中矩
to square it, I apply the T square."

匠人曰我善治木
The carpenter says, "I'm good at handling wood!

曲者中鉤
To arc it, I apply the curve;
直者應繩
to make it straight, I apply the plumb line."

夫埴木之性
豈欲中規矩鉤繩哉
But as far as inborn nature is concerned, the clay and the wood
surely have no wish to be subjected to compass and square, curve and plumb line.

然且世世稱之曰
伯樂善治馬
Yet generation after generation sings out in praise, saying,
"Bo Luo is good at handling horses!

而陶匠善治埴木
The potter and the carpenter are good at handling clay and wood!"

此亦治天下者之過也
And the same fault is committed by the men who handle the affairs of the world!

***

吾意善治天下者不然
In my opinion, someone who was really good at handling the affairs of the world would not go about it like this.

彼民有常性
The people have their constant inborn nature.

織而衣
耕而食
To weave for their clothing,
to till for their food---
是謂同德
this is the Virtue they share.

一而不黨命曰天放
They are one in it and not partisan, and it is called the Emancipation of Heaven.

故至德之世
其行填填
Therefore, in a time of Perfect Virtue,
the gait of men is slow and ambling;
其視顛顛
their gaze is steady and mild.

是時也
山無蹊隧
澤無舟梁
In such an age,
mountains have no paths or trails,
lakes no boats or bridges.

萬物群生連屬其鄉
The ten thousand things live species by species, one group settled close to another.

禽獸成群
Birds and beasts form their flocks and herds;
草木遂長
grass and trees grow to fullest height.

是故禽獸可係羈而遊
烏鵲之巢可攀援而闚
So it happens that you can tie a cord to the birds and beasts and lead them about
or bend down the limb and peer into the nest of the crow and the magpie.

夫至德之世
同與禽獸居
族與萬物並
In this age of Perfect Virtue,
men live the same as birds and beasts,
group themselves side by side with the ten thousand things.

惡乎知君子小人哉
Who then knows anything about "gentleman" or "petty man"?

同乎無知
Dull and unwitting,[^9-2] men have no wisdom;
其德不離
thus their Virtue does not depart from them.

[^9-2]: Reading *tong* with the man radical; see p. 62, n. 12.

同乎無欲
Dull and unwitting, they have no desire;
是謂素樸
this is called uncarved simplicity.

素樸而民性得矣
In uncarved simplicity, the people attain their true nature.[^9-3]

[^9-3]: The terms *su* and *pu* (uncarved simplicity),
appear frequently in the _Daodejing_, for example, ch. XIX.
Waley translates them as "Simplicity" and "the Uncarved Block," respectively.

***

及至聖人
蹩躠為仁
踶跂為義
而天下始疑矣
Then along comes the sage,
huffing and puffing after benevolence,
reaching on tiptoe for righteousness,
and the world for the first time has doubts;
澶漫為樂
摘僻為禮
而天下始分矣
mooning and mouthing over his music,
snipping and stitching away at his rites,
and the world for the first time is divided.

故純樸不殘孰為犧尊
Thus, if the plain unwrought substance had not been blighted, how would there be any sacrificial goblets?

白玉不毀孰為珪璋
If the white jade had not been shattered, how would there be any scepters and batons?

道德不廢安取仁義
If the Way and its Virtue had not been cast aside, how would there be any call for benevolence and righteousness?

性情不離安用禮樂
If the true form of the inborn nature had not been abandoned, how would there be any use for rights and music?

五色不亂孰為文采
If the five colors had not confused men, who would fashion patterns and hues?

五聲不亂孰應六律
If the five notes had not confused them, who would try to tune things by the six tones?

夫殘樸以為器
That the unwrought substance was blighted in order to fashion implements---
工匠之罪也
this was the crime of the artisan.

毀道德以為仁義
That the Way and its Virtue were destroyed in order to create benevolence and righteousness---
聖人之過也
this was the fault of the sage.

***

夫馬陸居則食草飲水
When horses live on the plain, they eat grass and drink from the streams.

喜則交頸相靡
Pleased, they twine their necks together and rub;
怒則分背相踶
angry, they turn back to back and kick.

馬知已此矣
This all horses know how to do.

夫加之以衡扼
齊之以月題
而馬知介倪
闉扼
鷙曼
詭銜
竊轡
But if you pile poles and yokes on them
and line them up in crossbars and shafts,
then they will learn to snap the crossbars,
break the yoke,
rip the carriage top,
champ the bit,
and chew the reins.[^9-4]

[^9-4]: There are many different interpretations of the terms in this sentence.
I follow Ma Xulun's emendations and interpretations.

故馬之知而態至盜者
Thus horses learn how to commit the worst kinds of mischief.[^9-5]

[^9-5]: Following texts that read *neng* rather than *tai*.

伯樂之罪也
This is the crime of Bo Luo.

***

夫赫胥氏之時
民居不知所為
行不知所之
In the days of He Xu,[^9-6]
people stayed home but didn't know what they were doing,
walked around but didn't know where they were going.

[^9-6]: Legendary ruler of high antiquity.

含哺而熙
Their mouths crammed with food, they were merry;
鼓腹而遊
drumming on their bellies, they passed the time.

民能以此矣
This was as much as they were able to do.

及至聖人
屈折禮樂以匡天下之形
Then the sage came along
with the crouchings and bendings of rites and music, which were intended to reform the bodies of the world;
縣跂仁義以慰天下之心
with the reaching-for-a-dangled-prize of benevolence and righteousness, which was intended to comfort the hearts of the world.

而民乃始踶跂好知
爭歸於利
不可止也
Then for the first time, people learned to stand on tiptoe and covet knowledge,
to fight to the death over profit,
and there was no stopping them.

此亦聖人之過也
This, in the end, was the fault of the sage.
