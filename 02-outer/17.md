## 17 秋水 Autumn Floods

### The Lord of the River journeys east to the North Sea

秋水時至
The time of the autumn floods came,
百川灌河
and the hundred streams poured into the Yellow River.

涇流之大
Its racing current swelled to such proportions that,
兩涘渚崖之間
looking from bank to bank or island to island,
不辯牛馬
it was impossible to distinguish a horse from a cow.

於是焉河伯欣然自喜
Then the Lord of the River[^17-1] was beside himself with joy,
以天下之美為盡在己
believing that all the beauty in the world belonged to him alone.

[^17-1]: The Lord of the River, the god of the Yellow River, appeared on p. 45, under the name Pingyi.

順流而東行至於北海
Following the current, he journeyed east until at last he reached the North Sea.

東面而視
Looking east,
不見水端
he could see no end to the water.

***

於是焉河伯始旋其面目
The Lord of the River began to wag his head and roll his eyes.

望洋向若而歎曰
Peering far off in the direction of Ruo,[^17-2] he sighed and said,
野語有之曰
"The common saying has it,
聞道百
'He has heard the Way a mere hundred times,
以為莫己若者
but he thinks he's better than anyone else.'

我之謂也
It applies to me.

[^17-2]: The god of the sea.

且夫我嘗聞少仲尼之聞
In the past, I heard men belittling the learning of Confucius
而輕伯夷之義者
and making light of the righteousness of Bo Yi,[^17-3]
始吾弗信
though I never believed them.

[^17-3]: Bo Yi, who relinquished his kingdom to his brother
and later chose to die of starvation rather than serve a ruler he considered unjust,
was regarded as a model of righteousness.

今我睹子之難窮也
Now, however, I have seen your unfathomable vastness.

吾非至於子之門
If I hadn't come to your gate,[^17-4]
則殆矣
I should have been in danger.

[^17-4]: The Lord of the River has literally come to the gate of the sea.
But a second meaning is implied, that is, "If I hadn't become your disciple."

吾長見笑於大方之家
I should forever have been laughed at by the masters of the Great Method!"

***

北海若曰
Ruo of the North Sea said,
井蛙不可以語於海者
"You can't discuss the ocean with a well frog---
拘於虛也
he's limited by the space he lives in.

夏蟲不可以語於冰者
You can't discuss ice with a summer insect---
篤於時也
he's bound to a single season.

曲士不可以語於道者
You can't discuss the Way with a cramped scholar---
束於教也
he's shackled by his doctrines.

今爾出於崖涘觀於大海
Now you have come out beyond your banks and borders and have seen the great sea---
乃知爾醜
so you realize your own pettiness.

爾將可與語大理矣
From now on, it will be possible to talk to you about the Great Principle.

***

天下之水
"Of all the waters of the world,
莫大於海
none is as great as the sea.

萬川歸之
Ten thousand streams flow into it---
不知何時止
I have never heard of a time when they stopped---
而不盈
and yet it is never full.

尾閭泄之
The water leaks away at Weilü[^17-5]---
不知何時已
I have never heard of a time when it didn't---
而不虛
and yet the sea is never empty.

[^17-5]: Said by some commentators to be a huge fiery stone against which seawater turns to steam.

春秋不變
Spring or autumn, it never changes.

水旱不知
Flood or drought, it takes no notice.

此其過江河之流
It is so much greater than the streams of the Yangzi or the Yellow River
不可為量數
that it is impossible to measure the difference.

而吾未嘗以此自多者
But I have never, for this reason, prided myself on it.

自以比形於天地
I take my place with heaven and earth
而受氣於陰陽
and receive breath from the yin and yang.

吾在天地之間
I sit here between heaven and earth
猶小石小木之在大山也
as a little stone or a little tree sits on a huge mountain.

方存乎見少
Since I can see my own smallness,
又奚以自多
what reason would I have to pride myself?

***

計四海之在天地之間也
"Compare the area within the four seas with all that is between heaven and earth---
不似礨空之在大澤乎
is it not like one little anthill in a vast marsh?

計中國之在海內
Compare the Middle Kingdom with the area within the four seas---
不似稊米之在大倉乎
is it not like one tiny grain in a great storehouse?

號物之數謂之萬
When we refer to the things of creation, we speak of them as numbering ten thousand---
人處一焉
and man is only one of them.

人卒九州
We talk of the Nine Provinces where men are most numerous,
穀食之所生
and yet of the whole area where grain and foods are grown
舟車之所通
and where boats and carts pass back and forth,
人處一焉
man occupies only one fraction.[^17-6]

[^17-6]: As it stands in the original, this sentence makes little sense to me,
and the translation represents no more than a tentative attempt to extract some meaning.

此其比萬物也
Compared to the ten thousand things,
不似豪末之在於馬體乎
is he not like one little hair on the body of a horse?

五帝之所連
What the Five Emperors passed along,
三王之所爭
what the Three Kings fought over,
仁人之所憂
what the benevolent man grieves about,
任士之所勞
what the responsible man labors over---
盡此矣
all is no more than this![^17-7]

[^17-7]: The Five Emperors were five legendary rulers of high antiquity,
of whom the Yellow Emperor, Yao, and Shun are the most famous.
The Three Kings were the founders of the Three Dynasties, the Xia, the Shang.
and the Zhou.

伯夷辭之以為名
Bo Yi gained a reputation by giving it up;
仲尼語之以為博
Confucius passed himself off as learned because he talked about it.

此其自多也
But in priding themselves in this way,
不似爾向之自多於水乎
were they not like you a moment ago priding yourself on your floodwaters?"

***

河伯曰然則
"Well then," said the Lord of the River,
吾大天地而小毫末可乎
"if I recognize the hugeness of heaven and earth and the smallness of the tip of a hair, will that do?"

***

北海若曰否
"No indeed!" said Ruo of the North Sea.

夫物量無窮
"There is no end to the weighing of things,
時無止
no stop to time,
分無常
no constancy to the division of lots,
終始無故
no fixed rule to beginning and end.

是故大知觀於遠近
Therefore great wisdom observes both far and near,
故小而不寡
and for that reason, it recognizes small without considering it paltry,
大而不多
recognizes large without considering it unwieldy,
知量無窮
for it knows that there is no end to the weighing of things.

證曏今故
It has a clear understanding of past and present,
故遙而不悶
and for that reason, it spends a long time without finding it tedious,
掇而不跂
a short time without fretting at its shortness,
知時無止
for it knows that time has no end.

察乎盈虛
It perceives the nature of fullness and emptiness,
故得而不喜
and for that reason, it does not delight if it acquires something
失而不憂
or worry if it loses it,
知分之無常也
for it knows that there is no constancy to the division of lots.

明乎坦塗
It comprehends the Level Road,
故生而不說
and for that reason, it does not rejoice in life
死而不禍
or look on death as a calamity,
知終始之不可故也
for it knows that no fixed rule can be assigned to beginning and end.

***

計人之所知
"Calculate what man knows,
不若其所不知
and it cannot compare with what he does not know.

其生之時
Calculate the time he is alive,
不若未生之時
and it cannot compare with the time before he was born.

以其至小
Yet man takes something so small
求窮其至大之域
and tries to exhaust the dimensions of something so large!

是故迷亂而不能自得也
Hence he is muddled and confused and can never get anywhere.

由此觀之
Looking at it this way,
又何以知毫末之足以定至細之倪
how do we know that the tip of a hair can be singled out as the measure of the smallest thing possible?

又何以知天地之足以窮至大之域
Or how do we know that heaven and earth can fully encompass the dimensions of the largest thing possible?"

***

河伯曰
The Lord of the River said,
世之議者皆曰
"Men who debate such matters these days all claim
至精無形
that the minutest thing has no form
至大不可圍
and the largest thing cannot be encompassed.

是信情乎
Is this a true statement?"

***

北海若曰
Ruo of the North Sea said,
夫自細
"If from the standpoint of the minute,
視大者
we look at what is large,
不盡
we cannot see to the end.

自大
If from the standpoint of what is large,
視細者
we look at what is minute,
不明
we cannot distinguish it clearly.

夫精小之微也
The minute is the smallest of the small,
垺大之殷也
the gigantic is the largest of the large,
故異便
and it is therefore convenient to distinguish between them.

此勢之有也
But this is merely a matter of circumstance.

夫精粗者
Before we can speak of coarse or fine, however,
期於有形者也
there must be some form.

無形者數之所不能分也
If a thing has no form, then numbers cannot express its dimensions,
不可圍者數之所不能窮也
and if it cannot be encompassed, then numbers cannot express its size.

可以言論者物之粗也
We can use words to talk about the coarseness of things,
可以意致者物之精也
and we can use our minds to visualize the fineness of things.

言之所不能論
But what words cannot describe
意之所不能察致者
and the mind cannot succeed in visualizing---
不期精粗焉
this has nothing to do with coarseness or fineness.

***

是故大人之行
"Therefore the Great Man in his actions
不出乎害人
will not harm others,
不多仁恩
but he makes no show of benevolence or charity.

動不為利
He will not move for the sake of profit,
不賤門隸
but he does not despise the porter at the gate.

貨財弗爭
He will not wrangle for goods or wealth,
不多辭讓
but he makes no show of refusing or relinquishing them.

事焉不惜人
He will not enlist the help of others in his work,
不多食乎力
but he makes no show of being self-supporting,
不賤貪污
and he does not despise the greedy and base.

行殊乎俗
His actions differ from those of the mob,
不多辟異
but he makes no show of uniqueness or eccentricity.

為在從眾
He is content to stay behind with the crowd,
不賤佞諂
but he does not despise those who run forward to flatter and fawn.

世之爵祿不足以為勸
All the titles and stipends of the age are not enough to stir him to exertion;
戮恥不足以為辱
all its penalties and censures are not enough to make him feel shame.

知是非之不可為分
He knows that no line can be drawn between right and wrong,
細大之不可為倪
no border can be fixed between great and small.

聞曰
I have heard it said,
道人不聞
'The Man of the Way wins no fame,
至德不得
the highest virtue[^17-8] wins no gain,
大人無己
the Great Man has no self.'

約分之至也
To the most perfect degree, he goes along with what has been allotted to him."

[^17-8]: A play on the homophones *de* (virtue) and *de* (gain, or acquisition).

***

河伯曰
The Lord of the River said,
若物之外
"Whether they are external
若物之內
or internal to things,
惡至而倪貴賤
I do not understand how we come to have these distinctions of noble and mean
惡至而倪小大
or of great and small."

***

北海若曰
Ruo of the North Sea said,
以道觀之
"From the point of view of the Way,
物無貴賤
things have no nobility or meanness.

以物觀之
From the point of view of things themselves,
自貴而相賤
each regards itself as noble and other things as mean.

以俗觀之
From the point of view of common opinion,
貴賤不在己
nobility and meanness are not determined by the individual himself.

***

以差觀之
"From the point of view of differences,
因其所大而大之
if we regard a thing as big because there is a certain bigness to it,
則萬物莫不大
then among all the ten thousand things there are none that are not big.

因其所小而小之
If we regard a thing as small because there is a certain smallness to it,
則萬物莫不小
then among the ten thousand things there are none that are not small.

知天地之為稊米也
If we know that heaven and earth are tiny grains
知豪末之為丘山也
and the tip of a hair is a range of mountains,
則差數等矣
then we have perceived the law of difference.

***

以功觀之
"From the point of view of function,
因其所有而有之
if we regard a thing as useful because there is a certain usefulness to it,
則萬物莫不有
then among all the ten thousand things there are none that are not useful.

因其所無而無之
If we regard a thing as useless because there is a certain uselessness to it,
則萬物莫不無
then among the ten thousand things there are none that are not useless.

知東西之相反
If we know that east and west are mutually opposed
而不可以相無
but that one cannot do without the other,
則功分定矣
then we can estimate the degree of function.

***

以趣觀之
"From the point of view of preference,
因其所然而然之
if we regard a thing as right because there is a certain right to it,
則萬物莫不然
then among the ten thousand things there are none that are not right.

因其所非而非之
If we regard a thing as wrong because there is a certain wrong to it,
則萬物莫不非
then among the ten thousand things there are none that are not wrong.

知堯桀之自然而相非
If we know that Yao and Jie each thought himself right and condemned the other as wrong,
則趣操睹矣
then we may understand how there are preferences in behavior.

***

昔者堯舜讓而帝
"In ancient times Yao abdicated in favor of Shun, and Shun ruled as emperor;
之噲讓而絕
Kuai abdicated in favor of Zhi, and Zhi was destroyed.[^17-9]

[^17-9]: In 316 BCE, King Kuai of Yan was persuaded to imitate the example of Yao
by ceding his throne to his minister Zi Zhi.
In no time the state was torn by internal strife,
and three years later it was invaded and annexed by the state of Qi.

湯武爭而王
Tang and Wu fought and became kings;
白公爭而滅
Duke Bo fought and was wiped out.[^17-10]

[^17-10]: Tang and Wu were the founders of the Shang and Zhou dynasties, respectively.
Duke Bo was a scion of the royal family of Chu who led an unsuccessful revolt against its ruler
and was defeated and forced to commit suicide in 479 BCE.

由此觀之
Looking at it this way,
爭讓之禮
we see that struggling or giving way,
堯桀之行
behaving like a Yao or like a Jie,
貴賤有時
may at one time be noble and at another time be mean.

未可以為常也
It is impossible to establish any constant rule.

***

梁麗可以衝城
"A beam or pillar can be used to batter down a city wall,
而不可以窒穴
but it is no good for stopping up a little hole---
言殊器也
this refers to a difference in function.

騏驥驊騮一日而馳千里
Thoroughbreds like Qiji and Hualiu could gallop a thousand *li* in one day,
捕鼠不如狸狌
but when it came to catching rats, they were no match for the wildcat or the weasel---
言殊技也
this refers to a difference in skill.

鴟鵂夜撮蚤
The horned owl catches fleas at night and can spot the tip of a hair,
察毫末
but when daylight comes,
晝出瞋目而不見丘山
no matter how wide it opens its eyes, it cannot see a mound or a hill---
言殊性也
this refers to a difference in nature.

故曰蓋師是而無非
Now do you say that you are going to make Right your master and do away with Wrong,
師治而無亂乎
or make Order your master and do away with Disorder?

是未明天地之理
If you do, then you have not understood the principle of heaven and earth
萬物之情者也
or the nature of the ten thousand things.

是猶師天而無地
This is like saying that you are going to make Heaven your master and do away with Earth,
師陰而無陽
or make Yin your master and do away with Yang.

其不可行明矣
Obviously it is impossible.

然且語而不舍
If men persist in talking this way without stop,
非愚則誣也
they must be either fools or deceivers!

***

帝王殊禪
"Emperors and kings have different ways of ceding their thrones;
三代殊繼
the Three Dynasties had different rules of succession.

差其時逆其俗者
Those who went against the times and flouted custom
謂之篡夫
were called usurpers;
當其時順其俗者
those who went with the times and followed custom
謂之義徒
were called companions of righteousness.

默默乎河伯
Be quiet, be quiet, O Lord of the River!

女惡知貴賤之門
How could you understand anything about the gateway of nobility and meanness
大小之家
or the house of great and small?"

***

河伯曰然則我何為乎
"Well then," said the Lord of the River, "what should I do and what should I not do?

何不為乎
How am I to know in the end
吾辭受趣舍
what to accept and what to reject,
吾終奈何
what to abide by and what to discard?"

***

北海若曰
Ruo of the North Sea said,
以道觀之
"From the point of view of the Way,
何貴何賤
what is noble or what is mean?

是謂反衍
These are merely what are called endless changes.

無拘而志
Do not hobble your will,
與道大蹇
or you will be departing far from the Way!

何少
What is few,
何多
or what is many?

是謂謝施
These are merely what are called boundless turnings.[^17-11]

[^17-11]: I follow Fukunaga's interpretation of these terms.

無一而行
Do not strive to unify your actions,
與道參差
or you will be at sixes and sevens with the Way!

嚴乎若國之有君
Be stern like the ruler of a state---
其無私德
he grants no private favor.

繇繇乎若祭之有社
Be benign and impartial like the god of the soil at the sacrifice---
其無私福
he grants no private blessing.

泛泛乎若四方之無窮
Be broad and expansive like the endlessness of the four directions---
其無所畛域
they have nothing that bounds or hedges them.

兼懷萬物
Embrace the ten thousand things universally---
其孰承翼
how could there be one you should give special support to?

是謂無方
This is called being without bent.

萬物一齊
When the ten thousand things are unified and equal,
孰短孰長
then which is short and which is long?

***

道無終始
"The Way is without beginning or end,
物有死生
but things have their life and death---
不恃其成
you cannot rely on their fulfillment.

一虛一滿
One moment empty, the next moment full---
不位乎其形
you cannot depend on their form.

年不可舉
The years cannot be held off;
時不可止
time cannot be stopped.

消
Decay,
息
growth,
盈
fullness,
虛
and emptiness
終則有始
end and then begin again.

是所以語大義之方
It is thus that we must describe the plan of the Great Meaning
論萬物之理也
and discuss the principles of the ten thousand things.

物之生也若驟若馳
The life of things is a gallop, a headlong dash---
無動而不變
with every movement they alter,
無時而不移
with every moment they shift.

何為乎
What should you do
何不為乎
and what should you not do?

夫固將自化
Everything will change of itself, that is certain!"

***

河伯曰然
"If that is so," said the Lord of the River,
則何貴於道邪
"then what is there valuable about the Way?"

***

北海若曰
Ruo of the North Sea said,
知道者
"He who understands the Way
必達於理
is certain to have command of basic principles.

達於理者
He who has command of basic principles
必明於權
is certain to know how to deal with circumstances.

明於權者
And he who knows how to deal with circumstances
不以物害己
will not allow things to do him harm.

至德者
When a man has perfect virtue,
火弗能熱
fire cannot burn him,
水弗能溺
water cannot drown him,
寒暑弗能害
cold and heat cannot afflict him,
禽獸弗能賊
birds and beasts cannot injure him.

非謂其薄之也
I do not say that he makes light of these things.

言察乎安危
I mean that he distinguishes between safety and danger,
寧於禍福
contents himself with fortune or misfortune,
謹於去就
and is cautious in his comings and goings.

莫之能害也
Therefore nothing can harm him.

***

故曰
"Hence it is said:
天在內
the Heavenly is on the inside,
人在外
the human is on the outside.

德在乎天
Virtue resides in the Heavenly.

知天人之行
Understand the actions of Heaven and man,
本乎天
base yourself on Heaven,
位乎得
take your stand in virtue,[^17-12]
蹢䠱而屈伸
and then, although you hasten or hold back, bend or stretch,
反要而語極
you may return to the essential and speak of the ultimate."

[^17-12]: Actually, the text reads "gain" (*de*);
perhaps this is merely a mistake for the *de* meaning "virtue,"
or perhaps a play on the two words is intended. See p. 129, n. 8.

曰何謂天何謂人
"What do you mean by the Heavenly and the human?"

***

北海若曰
Ruo of the North Sea said,
牛馬四足
"Horses and oxen have four feet---
是謂天
this is what I mean by the Heavenly.

落馬首
Putting a halter on the horse's head,
穿牛鼻
piercing the ox's nose---
是謂人
this is what I mean by the human.

故曰
So I say:
無以人滅天
do not let what is human wipe out what is Heavenly;
無以故滅命
do not let what is purposeful wipe out what is fated;
無以得殉名
do not let [the desire for] gain lead you after fame.

謹守而勿失
Be cautious, guard it, and do not lose it---
是謂反其真
this is what I mean by returning to the True."

### The Kui, the millipede, the snake and the wind 

夔憐蚿
The Kui[^17-13] envies the millipede,
蚿憐蛇
the millipede envies the snake,
蛇憐風
the snake envies the wind,
風憐目
the wind envies the eye,
目憐心
and the eye envies the mind.

[^17-13]: A being with only one leg. Sometimes it is described as a spirit or a strange beast,
sometimes as a historical personage---the Music Master Kui.

***

夔謂蚿曰
The Kui said to the millipede,
吾以一足趻踔而行
"I have this one leg that I hop along on,
予無如矣
though I make little progress.

今子之使萬足獨奈何
Now how in the world do you manage to work all those ten thousand legs of yours?"

***

蚿曰
The millipede said,
不然
"You don't understand.

子不見夫唾者乎
Haven't you ever watched a man spit?

噴則大者如珠
He just gives a hawk and out it comes, some drops as big as pearls,
小者如霧
some as fine as mist,
雜而下者不可勝數也
raining down in a jumble of countless particles.

今予動吾天機
Now all I do is put in motion the heavenly mechanism in me---
而不知其所以然
I'm not aware of how the thing works."

***

蚿謂蛇曰
The millipede said to the snake,
吾以眾足行
"I have all these legs that I move along on,
而不及子之無足
but I can't seem to keep up with you who have no legs.

何也
How is that?"

***

蛇曰
The snake said,
夫天機之所動
"It's just the heavenly mechanism moving me along---
何可易邪
how can I change the way I am?

吾安用足哉
What would I do with legs if I had them?"

***

蛇謂風曰
The snake said to the wind,
予動吾脊脅而行
"I move my backbone and ribs and manage to get along,
則有似也
though I still have some kind of body.

今子蓬蓬然起於北海
But now you come whirling up from the North Sea
蓬蓬然入於南海
and go whirling off to the South Sea,
而似無有
and you don't seem to have any body.

何也
How is that?"

***

風曰
The wind said,
然予蓬蓬然起於北海
"It's true that I whirl up from the North Sea
而入於南海也
and whirl off to the South Sea.

然而指我則勝我
But if you hold up a finger against me you've defeated me,
䠓我亦勝我
and if you trample on me you've likewise defeated me.

雖然夫折大木
On the other hand, I can break down big trees
蜚大屋者
and blow over great houses---
唯我能也
this is a talent that I alone have.

故以眾小不勝為大勝也
So I take all the mass of little defeats and make them into a Great Victory.

為大勝者
To make a Great Victory---
唯聖人能之
only the sage is capable of that!"

### The men of Song mistake Confucius for Yang Huo

孔子遊於匡
When Confucius was passing through Kuang,
人圍之數匝
the men of Song surrounded him with several encirclements of troops,
而絃歌不惙
but he went right on playing his lute and singing without a stop.[^17-14]

[^17-14]: The _Analects_ twice states (IX, 5; XI, 22): "The Master was put in fear in Kuang."
It is said that the people of the state in which Kuang was situated,
here identified as Song, mistook Confucius for an enemy of theirs named Yang Huo.

子路入見曰
Zi Lu went in to see him and said,
何夫子之娛也
"Master, how can you be so carefree?"

***

孔子曰
Confucius said,
來吾語女
"Come, I will explain to you.

我諱窮久矣
For a long time I have tried to stay out of the way of hardship.

而不免命也
That I have not managed to escape it is due to fate.

求通久矣
For a long time I have tried to achieve success.

而不得時也
That I have not been able to do so is due to the times.

當堯舜
If it happens to be the age of a Yao or a Shun,
而天下無窮人
then there are no men in the world who face hardship---
非知得也
but this is not because their wisdom saves them.

當桀紂
If it happens to be the age of a Jie or a Zhou,
而天下無通人
then there are no men in the world who achieve success---
非知失也
but this is not because their wisdom fails them.

時勢適然
It is time and circumstance that make it so.

***

夫水行不避蛟龍者
"To travel across the water without shrinking from the sea serpent or the dragon---
漁父之勇也
this is the courage of the fisherman.

陸行不避兕虎者
To travel over land without shrinking from the rhinoceros or the tiger---
獵夫之勇也
this is the courage of the hunter.

白刃交於前
To see the bare blades clashing before him
視死若生者
and to look on death as though it were life---
烈士之勇也
this is the courage of the man of ardor.[^17-15]

[^17-15]: A man who is willing to sacrifice his life to save others or to preserve his honor.

知窮之有命
To understand that hardship is a matter of fate,
知通之有時
that success is a matter of the times,
臨大難而不懼者
and to face great difficulty without fear---
聖人之勇也
this is the courage of the sage.

由處矣
Be content with it, Zi Lu.

吾命有所制矣
My fate has been decided for me."

***

無幾何將甲者進辭
Shortly afterward the leader of the armed men came forward and apologized.

曰以為陽虎也
"We thought you were Yang Huo,
故圍之
and that was why we surrounded you.

今非也
Now that we see you aren't,
請辭而退
we beg to take leave and withdraw."

### Gongsun Long asks Prince Mou of Wei how to argue with Zhuangzi (the frog in the caved-in well)

公孫龍問於魏牟曰
Gongsun Long said to Prince Mou of Wei,[^17-16]
龍少學先生之道
"When I was young, I studied the Way of the former kings,
長而明仁義之行
and when I grew older, I came to understand the conduct of benevolence and righteousness.

[^17-16]: The logician Gongsun Long, who spent much time discussing the concepts
of sameness and difference or the relationship of attributes such as hardness and whiteness
to the thing they qualify, was mentioned on p. 10, n. 7, and p. 12, n. 9.
Prince Mou of Wei was the reputed author of a Daoist work in four sections that is no longer extant.

合同異
I reconciled difference and sameness,
雜堅白
distinguished hardness and whiteness,
然不然
and proved that not so was so,
可不可
that the unacceptable was acceptable.

困百家之知
I confounded the wisdom of the hundred schools
窮眾口之辯
and demolished the arguments of a host of speakers.

吾自以為至達已
I believed that I had attained the highest degree of accomplishment.

今吾聞莊子之言
But now I have heard the words of Zhuangzi,
汒焉異之
and I am bewildered by their strangeness.

不知論之不及與
I don't know whether my arguments are not as good as his,
知之弗若與
or whether I am no match for him in understanding.

今吾無所開吾喙
I find now that I can't even open my beak.

敢問其方
May I ask what you advise?"

***

公子牟隱机太息
Prince Mou leaned on his armrest and gave a great sigh,
仰天而笑曰
and then he looked up at the sky and laughed, saying,
子獨不聞夫埳井之鼃乎
"Haven't you ever heard about the frog in the caved-in well?

謂東海之鱉曰
He said to the great turtle of the Eastern Sea,
吾樂與
'What fun I have!

出跳梁乎井幹之上
I come out and hop around the railing of the well,
入休乎缺甃之崖
or I go back in and take a rest in the wall where a tile has fallen out.

赴水則接腋持頤
When I dive into the water, I let it hold me up under the armpits and support my chin,
蹶泥則沒足滅跗
and when I slip about in the mud, I bury my feet in it and let it come up over my ankles.

還虷蟹與科斗
I look around at the mosquito larvae and the crabs and tadpoles,
莫吾能若也
and I see that none of them can match me.

且夫擅一壑之水
To have complete command of the water of one whole valley
而跨跱埳井之樂
and to monopolize all the joys of a caved-in well---
此亦至矣
this is the best there is!

夫子奚不時來入觀乎
Why don't you come some time and see for yourself?'

***

東海之鱉左足未入
"But before the great turtle of the Eastern Sea had even gotten his left foot in the well,
而右膝已縶矣
his right knee was already wedged fast.

於是逡巡而卻
He backed out and withdrew a little,
告之海
and then began to describe the sea.

曰夫千里之遠不足以舉其大
'A distance of a thousand *li* cannot indicate its greatness;
千仞之高不足以極其深
a depth of a thousand fathoms cannot express how deep it is.

禹之時
In the time of Yu,
十年九潦
there were floods for nine years out of ten,
而水弗為加益
and yet its waters never rose.

湯之時
In the time of Tang,
八年七旱
there were droughts for seven years out of eight,
而崖不為加損
and yet its shores never receded.

夫不為頃久推移
Never to alter or shift, whether for an instant or an eternity;
不以多少進退者
never to advance or recede, whether the quantity of water flowing in is great or small---
此亦東海之大樂也
this is the great delight of the Eastern Sea!'

***

於是埳井之鼃聞之
"When the frog in the caved-in well heard this,
適適然驚
he was dumbfounded with surprise,
規規然自失也
crestfallen, and completely at a loss.

且夫知不知是非之竟
Now your knowledge cannot even define the borders of right and wrong,
而猶欲觀於莊子之言
and still you try to see through the words of Zhuangzi---
是猶使蚊負山
this is like trying to make a mosquito carry a mountain on its back
商蚷馳河也
or a pill bug race across the Yellow River.

必不勝任矣
You will never be up to the task!

***

且夫知不知論極妙之言
"He whose understanding cannot grasp these minute and subtle words
而自適一時之利者
but is fit only to win some temporary gain---
是非埳井之鼃與
is he not like the frog in the caved-in well?

且彼方跐黃泉而登大皇
Zhuangzi, now---at this very moment he is treading the Yellow Springs[^17-17] or leaping up to the vast blue.

[^17-17]: The underworld.

無南無北
To him there is no north or south---
奭然四解
in utter freedom he dissolves himself in the four directions
淪於不測
and drowns himself in the unfathomable.

無東無西
To him there is no east or west---
始於玄冥
he begins in the Dark Obscurity
反於大通
and returns to the Great Thoroughfare.

子乃規規然
Now you come niggling along
而求之以察
and try to spy him out
索之以辯
or fix some name to him,
是直用管窺天
but this is like using a tube to scan the sky
用錐指地也
or an awl to measure the depth of the earth---
不亦小乎
the instrument is too small, is it not?

子往矣
You'd better be on your way!

且子獨不聞壽陵餘子之學行於邯鄲與
Or perhaps you've never heard about the young boy of Shouling who went to learn the Handan Walk.

未得國能
He hadn't mastered what the Handan people had to teach him
又失其故行矣
when he forgot his old way of walking,
直匍匐而歸耳
so he had to crawl all the way back home.

今子不去
Now if you don't get on your way,
將忘子之故
you're likely to forget what you knew before
失子之業
and be out of a job!"

***

公孫龍口呿而不合
Gongsun Long's mouth fell open and wouldn't stay closed.

舌舉而不下
His tongue stuck to the roof of his mouth and wouldn't come down.

乃逸而走
In the end he broke into a run and fled.

### The king of Chu troubles Zhuangzi (the sacred tortoise)

莊子釣於濮水
Once, when Zhuangzi was fishing in the Pu River,
楚王使大夫二人往先焉曰
the king of Chu sent two officials to go and announce to him:
願以境內累矣
"I would like to trouble you with the administration of my realm."

***

莊子持竿不顧曰
Zhuangzi held on to the fishing pole and, without turning his head, said,
吾聞楚有神龜
"I have heard that there is a sacred tortoise in Chu
死已三千歲矣
that has been dead for three thousand years.

王巾笥
The king keeps it wrapped in cloth and boxed,
而藏之廟堂之上
and stores it in the ancestral temple.

此龜者寧其死為留骨而貴乎
Now would this tortoise rather be dead and have its bones left behind and honored?

寧其生而曳尾於塗中乎
Or would it rather be alive and dragging its tail in the mud?"

二大夫曰寧生而曳尾塗中
"It would rather be alive and dragging its tail in the mud," said the two officials.

***

莊子曰
Zhuangzi said,
往矣
"Go away!

吾將曳尾於塗中
I'll drag my tail in the mud!"

### Huizi tries to shoo Zhuangzi from Liang

惠子相梁
When Huizi was prime minister of Liang,
莊子往見之
Zhuangzi set off to visit him.

或謂惠子曰
Someone said to Huizi,
莊子來欲代子相
"Zhuangzi is coming because he wants to replace you as prime minister!"

於是惠子恐
With this, Huizi was filled with alarm
搜於國中三日三夜
and searched all over the state for three days and three nights trying to find Zhuangzi.

莊子往見之曰
Zhuangzi then came to see him and said,
南方有鳥其名為鵷鶵
"In the south there is a bird called the Yuanchu---
子知之乎
I wonder if you've ever heard of it?

夫鵷鶵發於南海而飛於北海
The Yuanchu rises up from the South Sea and flies to the North Sea,
非梧桐不止
and it will rest on nothing but the Wutong tree,
非練實不食
eat nothing but the fruit of the Lian,
非醴泉不飲
and drink only from springs of sweet water.

於是鴟得腐鼠
Once there was an owl who had gotten hold of a half-rotten old rat,
鵷鶵過之
and as the Yuanchu passed by,
仰而視之曰
it raised its head, looked up at the Yuanchu, and said,
嚇
'Shoo!'

今子欲以子之梁國
Now that you have this Liang state of yours,
而嚇我邪
are you trying to shoo me?"

### Zhuangzi and Huizi discuss what fish enjoy

莊子與惠子遊於濠梁之上莊子曰
Zhuangzi and Huizi were strolling along the dam of the Hao River when Zhuangzi said,
儵魚出遊從容
"See how the minnows come out and dart around where they please!

是魚樂也
That's what fish really enjoy!"

***

惠子曰
Huizi said,
子非魚
"You're not a fish---
安知魚之樂
how do you know what fish enjoy?"

***

莊子曰
Zhuangzi said,
子非我
"You're not I,
安知我不知魚之樂
so how do you know that I don't know what fish enjoy?"

***

惠子曰
Huizi said,
我非子
"I'm not you,
固不知子矣
so I certainly don't know what you know.

子固非魚也
On the other hand, you're certainly not a fish---
子之不知魚之樂全矣
so that still proves that you don't know what fish enjoy!"

***

莊子曰
Zhuangzi said,
請循其本
"Let's go back to your original question, please.

子曰汝安知魚樂
You asked me *how* I know what fish enjoy---
云者既已知吾知之而問我
so you already knew that I knew it when you asked the question.

我知之濠上也
I know it by standing here beside the Hao."
