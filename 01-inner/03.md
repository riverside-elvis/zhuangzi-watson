## 3 養生主 The Secret of Caring for Life

吾生也有涯
Your life has a limit,
而知也无涯
but knowledge has none.[^3-1]

[^3-1]: The chapter is very brief and would appear to be mutilated.

以有涯隨无涯殆已
If you use what is limited to pursue what has no limit, you will be in danger.

已而為知者殆而已矣
If you understand this and still strive for knowledge, you will be in danger for certain!

為善
If you do good,
无近名
stay away from fame.

為惡
If you do evil,
无近刑
stay away from punishments.

緣督
Follow the middle;
以為經可以保身
go by what is constantand you can stay in one piece,
可以全生
keep yourself alive,
可以養親
look after your parents,
可以盡年
and live out your years.

***

庖丁為文惠君解牛
Cook Ding was cutting up an ox for Lord Wenhui.[^3-2]

[^3-2]: Identified as King Hui of Wei, who appeared on p. 5.

手之所觸肩之所倚
At every touch of his hand, every heave of his shoulder,
足之所履膝之所踦
every move of his feet, every thrust of his knee---
砉然嚮然
zip! zoop!

奏刀騞然莫不中音
He slithered the knife along with a zing, and all was in perfect rhythm,
合於桑林之舞
as though he were performing the dance of the Mulberry Grove
乃中經首之會
or keeping time to the Jingshou music.[^3-3]

[^3-3]: The Mulberry Grove is identified as a rain dance from the time of King Tang of the Shang dynasty,
and the Jingshou music, as part of a longer composition from the time of Yao.

***

文惠君曰譆善哉
"Ah, this is marvelous!" said Lord Wenhui.

技蓋至此乎
"Imagine skill reaching such heights!"

***

庖丁釋刀對曰
Cook Ding laid down his knife and replied,
臣之所好者道也
"What I care about is the Way,
進乎技矣
which goes beyond skill.

始臣之解牛之時
When I first began cutting up oxen,
所見无非牛者
all I could see was the ox itself.

三年之後未嘗見全牛也
After three years I no longer saw the whole ox.

方今之時
And now---
臣以神遇而不以目視
now I go at it by spirit and don't look with my eyes.

官知止
Perception and understanding have come to a stop,
而神欲行
and spirit moves where it wants.

依乎天理
I go along with the natural makeup,
批大郤
strike in the big hollows,
導大窾
guide the knife through the big openings,
因其固然
and follow things as they are.

技經肯綮之未嘗
So I never touch the smallest ligament or tendon,
而況大軱乎
much less a main joint.

***

良庖歲更刀
"A good cook changes his knife once a year---
割也
because he cuts.

族庖月更刀
A mediocre cook changes his knife once a month---
折也
because he hacks.

今臣之刀十九年矣
I've had this knife of mine for nineteen years
所解數千牛矣
and I've cut up thousands of oxen with it,
而刀刃若新發於硎
and yet the blade is as good as though it had just come from the grindstone.

彼節者有間
There are spaces between the joints,
而刀刃者无厚
and the blade of the knife has really no thickness.

以无厚入有間恢恢乎其於遊
If you insert what has no thickness into such spaces, then there's plenty of room---
刃必有餘地矣
more than enough for the blade to play about in.

是以十九年
That's why after nineteen years,
而刀刃若新發於硎
the blade of my knife is still as good as when it first came from the grindstone.

***

雖然每至於族
"However, whenever I come to a complicated place,
吾見其難為
I size up the difficulties,
怵然為戒
tell myself to watch out and be careful,
視為止
keep my eyes on what I'm doing,
行為遲
work very slowly,
動刀甚微
and move the knife with the greatest subtlety, until---
謋然已解
flop! the whole thing comes apart
如土委地
like a clod of earth crumbling to the ground.

提刀而立為之四顧
I stand there holding the knife and look all around me,
為之躊躇滿志
completely satisfied and reluctant to move on,
善刀而藏之
and then I wipe off the knife and put it away." [^3-4]

[^3-4]: Waley (_Three Ways of Thought in Ancient China_, p. 73) takes this whole paragraph to refer to the working methods
of a mediocre carver and hence translates it very differently. There is a great deal to be said for his interpretation,
but after much consideration I have decided to follow the traditional interpretation because it seems to me
that the extreme care and caution that the cook uses when he comes to a difficult place
is also a part of Zhuangzi's "secret of caring for life."

***

文惠君曰善哉
"Excellent!" said Lord Wenhui.

吾聞庖丁之言得養生焉
"I have heard the words of Cook Ding and learned how to care for life!"

***

公文軒見右師
When Gongwen Xuan saw the Commander of the Right,[^3-5]
而驚曰
he was startled and said,
是何人也
"What kind of man is this?

[^3-5]: Probably the ex–Commander of the Right, as he has been punished by having one foot amputated,
a common penalty in ancient China. It is mutilating punishments such as these
that Zhuangzi has in mind when he talks about the need to "stay in one piece."

惡乎介也
How did he come to lose his foot?

天與
Was it Heaven?

其人與
Or was it man?"

***

曰天也非人也
"It was Heaven, not man," said the commander.

天之生是使獨也
"When Heaven gave me life, it saw to it that I would be one-footed.

人之貌有與也
Men's looks are given to them.

以是知其天也非人也
So I know this was the work of Heaven and not of man.

澤雉十步一啄
The swamp pheasant has to walk ten paces for one peck
百步一飲
and a hundred paces for one drink,
不蘄畜乎樊中
but it doesn't want to be kept in a cage.

神雖王不善也
Though you treat it like a king, its spirit won't be content."

---

老聃死秦失弔之
When Lao Dan[^3-6] died, Qin Shi went to mourn for him,
三號而出
but after giving three cries, he left the room.

[^3-6]: Laozi, the reputed author of the _Daodejing_.

***

弟子曰非夫子之友邪
"Weren't you a friend of the Master?" asked Laozi's disciples.

***

曰然
"Yes."

***

然則弔焉若此可乎
"And you think it's all right to mourn him this way?"

***

曰然
"Yes," said Qin Shi.

始也吾以為其人也
"At first I took him for a real man,
而今非也
but now I know he wasn't.

向吾入而弔焉
A little while ago, when I went in to mourn,
有老者哭之如哭其子
I found old men weeping for him as though they were weeping for a son,
少者哭之如哭其母
and young men weeping for him as though they were weeping for a mother.

彼其所以會之
To have gathered a group like *that*,
必有不蘄言而言
he must have done something to make them talk about him, though he didn't ask them to talk
不蘄哭而哭者
or make them weep for him, though he didn't ask them to weep.

是遁天
This is to hide from Heaven,
倍情
turn your back on the true state of affairs,
忘其所受
and forget what you were born with.

古者謂之遁天之刑
In the old days, this was called the crime of hiding from Heaven.

適來夫子時也
Your master happened to come because it was his time,
適去夫子順也
and he happened to leave because things follow along.

安時而處順
If you are content with the time and willing to follow along,
哀樂不能入也
then grief and joy have no way to enter.

古者謂是帝之縣解
In the old days, this was called being freed from the bonds of God.

指窮於為薪火傳也
"Though the grease burns out of the torch, the fire passes on,
不知其盡也
and no one knows where it ends." [^3-7]

[^3-7]: The first part of this last sentence is scarcely intelligible,
and there are numerous suggestions for how it should be interpreted or emended.
I follow Zhu Guiyao in reading "grease" instead of "finger."
For the sake of reference, I list some of the other possible interpretations as I understand them:
"When the fingers complete the work of adding firewood, the fire passes on" (Guo Xiang);
"Though the fingers are worn out gathering firewood, the fire passes on" (Yu Yue);
"What we can point to are the fagots that have been consumed,
but the fire is transmitted elsewhere" (Legge, Fukunaga).
